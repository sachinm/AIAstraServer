# Marketing, Pricing and the Path to $100,000 MRR

November 2026 | Internal planning model

## What we sell

Lead with a personal Vedic companion. Free Happy Hour(s) gives people a reason to check timing as their plans change. The Self dashboard gives a chart-based starting point, with Love and Work available and next in line for the newest card design. Sell an ongoing place to return to a question or plan. Demo a Love card, an upcoming engagement conversation, and a follow-up grounded in the chart and context. Demonstrate timing as a traditional interpretation. Future outcomes remain uncertain.

The checked-in catalog is Free, Plus ($50/month or $500/year, ₹2,499/month or ₹24,999/year), and Unlimited ($200/month or $2,000/year, ₹9,999/month or ₹99,999/year). The billing design documents 20 free and 100 Plus questions monthly, with deployed limits controlled by configuration. The checkout code can record interest without collecting payment. Verify the live gateway and store receipts before reporting paying users. An upgrade click or recorded plan selection is an intent signal.

| Offer | United States pilot | India pilot |
|---|---|---|
| Free | Panchang, basic dashboard views, starter questions | Same free entry, with local time and tested English/Hindi onboarding |
| Recurring default | Plus at $50 with deeper readings and continued chat. Test $24.99 with 25 questions on a separate cohort if price is the main barrier | Keep ₹2,499 Plus visible for frequent users. Test a ₹799 monthly bundle only after seeing demand for recurring use |
| Occasional use | Test 5 questions for $9.99 alongside the subscription | Test ₹99 for one, ₹299 for five, ₹499 for ten. No auto-renewal |
| Dashboard upgrades | Test $5.99/month per dashboard for free and entry-plan users | Test ₹199 for a 30-day dashboard pass or ₹249/month for repeat users |
| Heavy users | $200 Unlimited remains in the catalog. Measure actual usage costs before promoting it | ₹9,999 Unlimited remains in the catalog. Keep it out of broad acquisition campaigns until demand and costs are known |

Launch add-on tests with one dashboard, then extend to Self, Love and Work. Include those dashboard upgrades in Unlimited and show bundle savings clearly. Treat a paid dashboard as deeper cards, timing and analysis. Show chat credit terms separately. When a reading fails, keep the question credit available. Purchased question credits stay available after cancellation and do not expire. Let users cancel or downgrade easily and keep access to prior conversations subject to account settings and deletion. Use a single disclosed introductory or win-back offer per eligible user during each experiment, with a holdout. Never target offers based on a distress conversation. Measure voluntary cancellations, billing failures, reactivation, and pack repurchase independently. [RevenueCat 2026 subscription benchmarks](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)

## What $100,000 per month takes

Target **$100,000 subscription MRR, equal to $1.2 million annualized recurring revenue**. MRR below is modeled from contracted recurring charges, excluding taxes and after recurring discounts, before store fees and variable costs. Normalize annual subscriptions by 12. Record passes, packs, API usage and advertising separately. Model INR at ₹94 per dollar as a rounded planning assumption. [November exchange-rate reference](https://www.ibja.co/Upload/IBJA_Bullion%20Daily%20Report%20-%2012-11-2026.pdf)

| Lower-price sensitivity | 3,500 US at $24.99 and 1,500 India at ₹799 | $100,215 |

The base case needs **2,200 concurrent subscribers**. If 5% of monthly active users pay for recurring plans, it requires roughly **44,000 MAU**. At 3% it needs about 73,300, and at 8% it needs 27,500. These are scenarios, with the denominator defined as users who complete a meaningful monthly action. At an assumed 8% monthly churn, 176 recurring additions a month replace losses at the target. Growing from zero to 2,200 over 18 months of selling at that churn rate requires about 227 new recurring subscribers a month if additions are level, or about 4,086 cumulative adds. At $50 blended CAC that would cost about $204,300. Because the seed budget also covers brand, creative and early tests, the model needs material organic acquisition or growth funding as cohorts mature. An India pack-only month needs roughly 31,439 sales at ₹299 to produce $100,000 in monthly pack revenue. That figure measures transaction sales.

Show gross MRR, store proceeds, contribution after AI/cloud/support/refunds, and cash separately. Illustrative $100K gross less a 15% channel fee leaves $85K before those costs, and a 30% fee leaves $70K. Rates depend on channel and eligibility. Track answer cost by mode and gross margin by offer. Use a 90-day cohort margin model that subtracts payment fees, refunds, promotions and variable costs before authorizing more acquisition. ## Acquisition and release

1. **Before public release.** Recruit 100 invited users per market through Jyotish educators, US diaspora communities and India-based creators. Run 15 interviews per market and track the first timing check, first dashboard card read, and first follow-up. Test which hook earns an actual return. Use real receipts for any permitted paid pilot and record interest separately.
2. **Public release in both countries.** Release iPhone, iPad and Android together after store approval and billing checks. US creative leads with ongoing personal planning, timing and chart-based questions. India creative leads with Panchang and a low-commitment paid question. Start with high-intent search pages, local-language creator demos, and community referrals. Use the same companion positioning across markets. Feature a relationship example for the demo.
3. **First 90 days after launch.** Set a $20K capped acquisition test drawn from the marketing budget, $10K per market. In each market, split initial spend $4K creators, $4K intent search and $2K app-store ads. Give each channel a unique attribution code. Compare activated users, contribution per acquired user, first renewal or pack repurchase, and D30 return. Release subsequent budget only where cohorts show positive observed 90-day contribution after acquisition, or where a small, explicitly capped test can resolve a known question.
4. **Scale in steps.** Use $10K, $25K, $50K and $100K MRR as reporting checkpoints. At each step review recurring adds net of churn, first renewal, repeat use, payback by region, and unit costs. For India report both subscription MRR and pack repurchase. Measure win-back against a holdout, with offers available after cancellation or when users return to a project on their own.

Use a provisional launch-quality bar of 99.5% crash-free beta sessions, 95% successful completion of tested onboarding and reading flows, and no open blocker in checkout, restoration, cancellation or account deletion. Use D7/D30 meaningful use and paid evidence as learnings, with rates and denominators visible and sample size disclosed. Start with timing or project reminders that users request, and track how often they resume the workflow.
