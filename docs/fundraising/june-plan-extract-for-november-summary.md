# June Plan Extract for the November Executive Summary

14 November 2026 | Internal source notes | Companion to `plan-key-points-rd-edition.md`

**Purpose.** An investor may read only the executive summary. The June plan spreads its best thinking across 36 pages. This document pulls that thinking forward, and sets it against what has actually been built since, so both can be folded into the executive summary of the November revision.

**Two readings of "what is there today".** It can mean what the June plan said existed, or what the market offers. Both are covered: section 3 compares June with November, and section 4 compares the market with AdAstra AI.

## 1. Paragraphs ready to fold into the executive summary

These are written to slot into the 12 November executive summary. Each is self-contained.

**Why this exists.** People are not looking for horoscopes. They are looking for answers about work, relationships, family and timing. Good guidance has been limited by cost, availability and how much a practitioner can prepare for one client. AdAstra AI removes the preparation limit. People use computers without knowing assembly language, and they should be able to benefit from Vedic astrology without learning chart mechanics. The system does the chart work for every question, and the person receives a practical answer.

**What has been built since June.** The June plan listed the research step before each answer as "not built" and called it the critical path. It is now built. A calculation engine computes the chart first. Specialist agents then read separate layers of it, a cross-check reconciles them, and a writer answers. Self, Love and Work dashboards are in the signed-in app, along with free daily timing, a Life timeline, and terms, privacy and refund pages. The earlier retrieval approach, which the June plan admitted used placeholder embeddings, has been replaced.

**How quality is engineered.** The June plan said accuracy work should be treated as co-equal with go-to-market. That is where most of the effort has gone. Every reading method is registered as a technique with a written contract: what it reads, under which rules, and what it returns. 136 techniques were registered on 6 November. An internal admin application lets staff run one technique for one chart and inspect its inputs, steps and output. On 8 November a benchmark ran 30 dashboard readings across 10 test charts, and an automated audit found every claim shown tied to a calculated chart fact.

**What is not yet proven.** No practitioner has reviewed the readings, so no accuracy claim is made. There are no outside users, no payments and no revenue. The June plan's month-six targets (750 payers, about $12K MRR, twelve dashboards, an advisor-validated benchmark) were not met. No outside money has been raised and no hires have been made. Everything built to date was built by the founder.

**How it reaches people.** The principle from June stands: acquire questions, not users. People do not search for an astrology app. They search for an answer to a decision in front of them. The November plan starts smaller than June did. First, 100 invited users per market through Jyotish educators, US diaspora communities and India-based creators, with 15 interviews per market. Then a public release in the US and India together once the mobile apps pass store review. Then a capped $20K test across creators, search and app-store ads, with more spend released only where cohorts return and pay.

**Business and ask.** The catalog is Free, Plus at $50 or INR 2,499 a month, and Unlimited at $200 or INR 9,999. Lower price points, question packs and single-dashboard upgrades are planned tests, not validated offers. The operating milestone is $100K MRR, which the base case reaches with 1,800 US and 400 India subscribers. The seed target is $2M in preferred equity, covering mobile apps, billing, practitioner review and the first paying cohorts in the US and India.

## 5. The research and accuracy focus, stated for an investor

This is the main change of emphasis since June and deserves its own lines in the summary.

- **Each reading method is a registered technique.** A technique has a contract that states which chart tables it reads, which rules apply, and what it returns. 136 were registered on 6 November: 58 for Self, 27 for Love, 29 for Work and 22 for general readings.
- **Staff can study one technique on one chart.** The admin application's lab runs a single technique and shows what went in, what each step did, what the writer received and what came out. A technique can be examined this way before it reaches a dashboard. A formal release gate is designed but not yet in force.
- **Every claim must point at a calculated fact.** An automated audit checks this. It passed all 30 runs in the 8 November benchmark.
- **The 25 dashboard techniques were checked against four classical schools.** The benchmark report records this as an internal review produced during development. It covers the 25 techniques in the benchmark, not all 136, and it was not carried out by a practising astrologer.
- **What this is and is not.** It is accuracy engineering: a method for finding and fixing errors one technique at a time. It is not evidence that readings are accurate. That needs blind practitioner review, which the seed budget funds.

## 6. What to leave out of the November revision

- **Accuracy claims.** "A bar no competitor has cleared", "chart-grade accuracy", "the only consumer-grade stack" and "institutional-grade" (pp. 2, 10–12, 15, 32). The plan's own risk table rules these out before validation (p. 33).
- **Selling life-event signals to advertisers** (section 8.3, p. 21, and pp. 31–32). It conflicts with the published privacy policy, which says personal information is not used for advertising.
- **The acquisition story** naming Meta, TikTok and others (pp. 31–32).
- **A named persona for the reading voice** (pp. 10, 33, 36), and the engine's internal name.
- **Market counts.** The plan gives 300M and 600M for the same market (cover, pp. 2, 13). Rebuild from reachable segments.
- **Unit economics as targets.** $290 LTV, $20 CAC, 78% margin and a 16 times ratio (pp. 3, 17, 26) have no observations behind them.
- **Dated comparables.** Competitor valuations and revenue figures (p. 14) are unsourced.
- **The health, accident, disease and legal dashboards** (p. 23). The November plan keeps these outside the assistant's remit.

## 7. Two items to check before the revision goes out

- **Encryption.** The June plan says the codebase has encryption of personal data (p. 24). A November plan addresses encryption as work to do. Confirm the current state before repeating the June sentence.
- **Advertising.** On 1 November the published privacy policy said the service does not show advertising. The November subscription notes propose sponsored placements on free public pages. One of the two has to change before launch.
