# Reading charts the Sri Jagannath Center way

Pandit Sanjay Rath is an Odisha-born Jyotish teacher. He presents himself as heir to a Puri family lineage traced to Sri Achyuta Dasa, and he founded the Sri Jagannath Center (SJC) and its teaching arm, the Devaguru Bṛhaspati Center. His school's signature is a Parāśara-plus-Jaimini synthesis. It uses an 8-planet chara karaka scheme with the Atmakaraka's navamsa (Karakamsa) as "the seat of the real self", arudha padas computed in every divisional chart, Narayana dasa as the main sign-based timing system, Tithi Pravesha as the annual chart, and a chart-derived deity framework (Ishta, Dharma, Palana and Guru devata) that steers remedies. For an AI assistant, the most important finding is about provenance. Rath's own published pages, read directly, give the AK and Karakamsa rules, the Narayana dasa starting rule, Shoola dasa, the Tithi Pravesha definition, the deity framework, and a clear ethic: astrology times illness but defers to doctors on diagnosis, remedies are handled with caution, and free will is real but lives in the mind. Almost every step-by-step computational rule (the arudha exceptions, Narayana dasa progression and lengths, argala, varga domains, gemstone rules) comes from his student P.V.R. Narasimha Rao's textbook, not from Rath's own words. Popular claims about his method, such as "start with the Panchanga", "the chart must speak" and "confirm past events before predicting", were not found in any source read. An assistant can therefore follow the school's techniques and ethics faithfully, but must not quote or paraphrase Rath as the author of rules that his students wrote down. It should also leave the many points the record never settled visibly open.

## A self-described lineage carried into a certified teaching institution

Nearly everything known about Rath's background comes from his own site or his students, and that is the main reliability caveat. srath.com says he belongs to a traditional family of astrologers from Bira Balabhadrapur Sasan village in Puri, whose Jyotish line traces to Sri Achyutananda, one of the Pañca Sakhā of Odisha. It says he trained from childhood under his uncle, the late Pt. Kasinath Rath, and that his grandfather Pt. Jagannath Rath was a published astrologer styled "Jyotish Ratna of Orissa" ([srath.com About](https://srath.com/series/about)). A family page places the line in the Atri gotra and says he left government service in 2000 "to continue the family lineage" ([srath.com Rathātreya page](https://srath.com/?p=19)). He graduated in mechanical engineering and worked for the Government of India before turning to Jyotish full time ([srath.com About](https://srath.com/series/about)). He names Brihat Parasara Hora Shastra, the Jaimini Upadesa Sutras, Brihat Jataka and Saravali, along with the Gita, Upanishads and Puranas, as his foundations. He insists he is a strict follower of the seers and is not presenting "his own brand" of astrology. No independent source verifies the lineage. His birth year (1963, Sambalpur) rests on a single Wikipedia mirror seen only through a search snippet. The English Wikipedia page returned a 404 on 2026-11-15 for reasons the research did not establish.

The institution grew in two stages. SJC started informally in 1997, was established in 1998 and was registered as a society in New Delhi in May 2003. It taught through free e-lists, study circles and annual conferences in the guru-shishya model, with the stated aim of restoring Jyotish's dignity against quackery ([srath.com SJC page](https://srath.com/?p=41)). One unconfirmed search summary dated SJC to 2001, and that conflict is unresolved. Long-form paid programmes then moved under the Devaguru Bṛhaspati Center, an SJC subsidiary with a US 501(c)(3) arm ([srath.com About](https://srath.com/about/)). The **Parāśara Jyotiṣa Course is a five-year online-and-contact programme**, with about ten days of Himalayan contact training each March. Rath mentored batch 1, Dr. Andrew Foss batch 2 and Sarbani Rath (his wife) batch 3 ([srath.com PJC page](https://srath.com/parasara-jyoti%E1%B9%A3a-course/)). The Jaimini Scholar Program reportedly first ran 2007–2011 in Almora and Bhimtal, but that detail comes from search summaries, not a page that was read.

His core books are *Crux of Vedic Astrology: Timing of Events* (Sagar; a 1998 edition is catalogued) ([Open Library](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/OL24940905M)), *Narayana Dasa* (Sagar; dated 1998 on one srath.com page and 2001 by a Wikipedia mirror) ([Exotic India](https://www.exoticindiaart.com/book/details/narayana-dasa-nae836/)), and *Vedic Remedies in Astrology* (dated 2000 by a mirror and 2007 by retailers) ([Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/20107720)). The others are a translation of *Maharishi Jaimini's Upadesa Sutras*, *Vimsottari and Udu Dasas* ([Motilal Banarsidass](https://motilalbanarsidass.com/collections/astrology/products/vimsottari-and-udu-dasas-parasaras-key-to-prognostication)), *Brihat Nakshatra* and *Collected Papers in Vedic Astrology* ([srath.com About](https://srath.com/series/about)). None of these book texts were available to the researchers, which is the single largest gap in this report.

The students matter because they wrote down most of the rules. **P.V.R. Narasimha Rao calls Rath "my guru"** in his freely shared textbook *Vedic Astrology: An Integrated Approach*, the most complete written rulebook for the tradition ([Rao-IA](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)). Visti Larsen joined SJC in 2001 "to learn astrology as taught in the lineage of Sri Achyuta Dasa of Puri". He dedicated *Jyotisa Fundamentals: My Master's Words* to Rath ([Exotic India listing](https://www.exoticindiaart.com/book/details/jyotisa-fundamentals-my-master-s-words-idl017/)), and he describes trying to imitate his guru's every word and gesture ([Sri Garuda](https://srigaruda.com/about/)). Sarajit Poddar, writing as an SJC student, credits Rath with making Tithi Pravesha public ([Sanskriti Magazine](https://www.sanskritimagazine.com/principles-of-tithi-pravesh-chakra-in-vedic-astrology/)). No sourced criticism specific to Rath or SJC was found. One forum thread making an unverified allegation was not read and is not relied on.

## Jaimini karakas, arudhas and Narayana dasa form the technical spine

**The Atmakaraka is the centre of the reading.** In Rath's own article, the AK's navamsa sign is the Karakamsa lagna, "the seat of the real self". The AK's sign shows the soul's desire, and its planetary nature shows the soul's spiritual development ([Rath: Atmakaraka & Karakamsa](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/scholar/atmakaraka-karakamsa/)). He gives rules directly. A malefic AK indicates a more advanced soul than a benefic one. The AK brings suffering in its own Vimshottari dasa. **Great achievements come in the Narayana dasa of the AK's sign or of signs aspecting it.** A retrograde AK shows a deep-rooted desire behind the birth, and a combust AK gives spiritual insight. Each planet as AK carries a lesson: humility for the Sun, compassion for the Moon, non-violence for Mars, truthful speech for Mercury, respect for guru and children for Jupiter, clean character for Venus, not causing sorrow for Saturn, and staying guileless though often deceived for Rahu. He also counts from the AK to the navamsa lagna and reads results per house, applying D9 results to ordinary people and reserving D1 results for exceptional figures. With the AK in the 6th or 8th from the navamsa lagna, he prescribes Saturn or Satyanarayana worship, fasting at the full moon, and speaking the truth.

The karaka computation itself is written down by Rao, not Rath. It uses eight karakas, Sun through Saturn plus Rahu, with **Rahu's degrees measured backwards from the end of his sign**. The planets are ranked AK, AmK, BK, MK, PiK, PK, GK and DK, with ties broken by minutes and seconds ([Rao-IA §8.2](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)). Rath argued the 8-versus-7 question at SJC conferences in 2003–04, but his argument was not read. The claim that he uses 8 karakas for living beings and 7 for mundane charts comes only from a critic's search summary.

The arudha rules are also Rao's. To find a bhava arudha, count from the house to its lord, then the same number of signs onward. If the result falls in the 1st or 7th from the house, take the 10th from it instead. Scorpio and Aquarius resolve their co-lords by a fixed strength ladder ([Rao-IA §9.2, §15.5.1](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)). The interpretive frame is what an assistant most needs. **Planets from the lagna show reality. Planets from the Arudha Lagna show the world's perception and the material situation. Graha arudhas show how the native perceives things** ([Rao-IA §9.6](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)). The 11th and 12th from AL show gains and losses. The Upapada (A12) shows marriage, with the 2nd, 7th and 8th from it showing strain or ending. A7 shows associates, and A10 in D10 shows the workplace. SJC class notes put it as "houses are intangible, arudha padas are tangible" ([SJC-Boston notes](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/classes/book2-for-CD.pdf)). Rath's own Shoola dasa article uses the AL directly: death can fall in the AL sign or in signs aspecting the 3rd from AL ([Rath: Shula Dasha](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/dasa/shula-dasha/)).

**Narayana dasa starts from the stronger of the lagna and the 7th, which Rath calls the satya peethas (seats of truth).** He also calls it Padakrama dasa ([srath.com](https://srath.com/page/63/)). Rao supplies the rest of the mechanics. The strength ladder runs from planet count, to Jupiter, Mercury and the lord by rasi drishti, to an exalted occupant, to the parity of the lord's sign, to the lord's advancement. Movable, fixed and dual seeds progress differently. Direction follows the footedness of the 9th, and Saturn in the seed forces forward order while Ketu reverses it. A dasa lasts the count to the lord minus one year, with 12 years when the lord is in its own sign. Antardasas are twelve equal parts. The dasa sign is read as a temporary lagna ([Rao-IA §15.5.2, §18](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)). The triggers are what make it a life-reading tool. The A10 dasa gives career success, the UL dasa can give marriage, the Ghati Lagna dasa gives power, and antardasas aspected by the GL bring promotions. Varga Narayana dasa seeds the D-n chart from the nth house's lord (D10 for career, D9 for marriage, D4 for residence).

Other dashas have assigned jobs in Rao's presentation. Lagna Kendradi covers material prosperity, Sudasa from Sree Lagna covers wealth and power, Drig dasa covers spiritual growth, Kalachakra is the dasa Parasara most respected, and Vimshottari is the general-purpose dasa. **Rath's own Shoola dasa page calls it "amongst the most accurate" for death and health.** It runs 9-year dashas from the stronger of the lagna and 7th for the self, or from paired houses for relatives, and its remedy is the Mrityunjaya mantra with Shiva worship on Mondays ([Rath: Shula Dasha](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/dasa/shula-dasha/)). Rao notes a naming clash: he uses "Niryana Shoola" for a 2nd/8th-based 7-8-9-year dasa, while his guru uses "Shoola dasa" for the lagna/7th 9-year one ([Rao-IA §22–23](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)). Rao also attributes to Rath a "Tripod of Life" for Vimshottari. Mahadasa results are judged from the Sun, antardasa results from the Moon, and pratyantardasa results from the lagna ([Rao-IA §16.5.3](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)). This is Rath's rule as reported by Rao.

## Tithi Pravesha and divisional charts decide what a year and a domain deliver

Rath calls the **Tithi Pravesha "the truest astrological birthday"** and the annual horoscopy of Jyotish. The chart is cast when the Sun is in its natal sign (not at its exact degree) and the Moon is at the exact natal Sun–Moon angle. It is cast for the birthplace, not the current residence. He notes Tajaka as a separate system ([Rath: Tithi Pravesha](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/tithi-pravesa/tithi-pravesha/)). How to read the chart is reported only by students, and they disagree on the year lord. **Poddar takes the weekday lord at the TP moment, counted sunrise to sunrise, as the year lord**, while treating the hora lord as the most important focus factor ([Sanskriti Magazine](https://www.sanskritimagazine.com/principles-of-tithi-pravesh-chakra-in-vedic-astrology/)). **Rao's 2014 paper calls the hora lord "the lord of the year"** ([Rao-TP2014](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/pp_tp.pdf)). Both agree that the natal chart overrides the year chart. Both agree that the TP's vargas are read by topic, with an indication that has to be strong in the matching varga as well as D1, and that the TP lagna's house from the natal lagna (kendra, trikona or dusthana) colours the year. SJC class notes use Tithi Ashtottari dasa for timing inside the year and caution that daily tithi charts lack traditional basis ([SJC-Boston notes](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/classes/book2-for-CD.pdf)). The Tithi Ashtottari calculation itself was not found. Rao's 2014 changes to how the soli-lunar month is defined, and his choice of ayanamsa for vargas, are his own departure, not the school's.

Divisional charts are where promises get confirmed. In Rao's tradition each varga governs a domain: D9 marriage and dharma, D10 career, D7 children, D4 residence, D12 parents, D20 spiritual life, D24 learning, D60 past-life karma. **The method is to pick the varga for the matter and look for links between the relevant houses, karakas and lords inside it, recomputing arudhas and special lagnas there** ([Rao-IA §6.3–6.5](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)). Rath edited the 2002 SJC varga workshop volume *Varga Chakra*, but its text was not available ([Rath: Varga Chakra](https://srath.com/misc/varga-chakra/)). Argala is the "decisive intervention" on a house: the 2nd, 4th and 11th give primary argala and the 5th secondary, obstructed from the 12th, 10th, 3rd and 9th. Rasi drishti, which is sign-based, runs through all the Jaimini strength rules. Hora Lagna shows wealth, Ghati Lagna power and fame, and Sree Lagna drives Sudasa ([Rao-IA §5, §10](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)).

No Rath text sets out a career method step by step. The student material describes a working method. Read the 10th from the lagna, the Moon and the AL. Read D10 for true conduct, A10 in D10 for the workplace, and the D10 artha trikona (2, 6, 10). Use the GL for promotions and the HL for money. Time it with Narayana dasa of A10 or GL signs, D10 Narayana dasa, Sudasa and the year's TP D10 ([Rao-IA §7.3.4, §9.3–9.4, §18.4](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf); [SJC-Boston notes](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/classes/book2-for-CD.pdf)). An SJC workshop title confirms that the school treats Karma, the 10th house, Saturn, Dashamsha and the Amatyakaraka as one cluster, but that confirmation is a search snippet only.

## Remedies flow from the chart's deities, and Rath polices their limits

**Rath's own "Spirituality in Jyotish" page sets out a deity framework read from Jaimini karakas.** The Ishta devata comes from the 12th from Karakamsa, the Dharma devata from the 9th from Karakamsa, and the Palana devata from the 6th from the Amatyakaraka in navamsa. The Guru devata comes from the navamsa sign of the Bhratrikaraka. Ishta, Palana and Guru form a "primary tripod" tied to the Sun, Moon and Jupiter. Each planet maps to a Vishnu avatara with a mantra, and deities shift by sign gender ([Rath: Spirituality in Jyotish](https://srath.com/spirituality/spirituality-in-jyotish/)). This page settles a point the secondary sources left disputed (6th sign versus 6th planet from the AmK, 9th from AK versus 9th from Karakamsa), and Rath's own page should govern. Rao's textbook phrases the Ishta slightly differently: the strongest planet occupying, owning or aspecting the 12th from the AK in D9. It supplies the planet-to-deity table (for example Ketu for Ganesha, Rahu for Durga or Narasimha, Venus for Lakshmi), with refinement by the planets influencing the deity planet ([Rao-IA §34.5](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)).

The practical remedy rules are Rao's. There are four tiers: common-sense precaution first, then gemstones, good deeds and daana, and mantra or deity propitiation. **Gemstones are only for functional benefics**, never for functional malefics or marakas in maraka periods, and for a life area the yogakaraka of that varga is strengthened. Fasting falls on the weekday of the UL lord for marriage and of the rajya-pada lord for career ([Rao-IA §34.1–34.3](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)).

Rath's own words are mostly about restraint. He says jyotisis should "tread very carefully" with remedies, so that their anxiety to help does not create bad karma. He says he is qualified to give only the Gayatri mantra, and that others should receive mantras from their diksha guru ([Rath: Spirituality in Jyotish](https://srath.com/spirituality/spirituality-in-jyotish/)). His remedies book warns astrologers off black magic ([srath.com](https://srath.com/misc/vedic-remedies-in-astrology/)). He sells no gemstones or yantras, and clients may decline a gem recommendation ([srath.com: The Process](https://srath.com/consultation/the-process/)). On health, he holds that **timing illness is the astrologer's strength, while diagnosis is "a weak area for the astrologer"** and medical treatment is far more reliable than astrological remedies ([Rath: Medical Astrology I](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/amateur/medical-astrology-i/)). His karma essay frames sanchita and prarabdha karma and places free will in the mind (mana), bounded by knowledge, resources, purity of purpose and time. It says present karma can change natal indications, including longevity, and that mantra protects the mind (mana + trai). The astrologer's job is to see how the mind will meet experience, not to declare fixed destinies ([Rath: Karma and Freewill](https://srath.com/misc/media/karma-and-freewill/)). He frames prediction as the study of "possible, probable, and preferable futures" ([Rath: About the Future](https://srath.com/consultation/about-future/)).

His tone has two registers. In writing he is technical and directive. He opens with a Sanskrit verse, uses numbered rules and corollaries, and sets assignments asking readers to test a principle over time ([Rath: Hora](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/hora-roots-of-jyotish/)). He tells students that a lapse "will not be tolerated" ([Rath: Scope of Jyotisa](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/scope-of-jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/)). In interview he is conversational and anecdotal. He reaches for everyday analogies (a torch whose battery decides the light), asks questions back to the listener, uses light humour, and offers maxims such as "Every sinner has a future" ([Rath: Finding Guru](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/scholar/finding-guru-and-seeking-knowledge/)). He also admits limits, for example that he cannot always be sure of a match ([Karma and Freewill](https://srath.com/misc/media/karma-and-freewill/)). No source shows how he words death, illness or divorce to a client.

His consultation intake is documented. The birth time must be reliable, and the office will not book a reading without either reliable birth data or a rectification booking. The client fills in a form that records their religion and picks the life areas in advance. The session includes the person's name and any name changes, ends with remedial and spiritual advice, and is recorded for the client to replay ([srath.com: The Process](https://srath.com/consultation/the-process/)).

## How to read in the manner of this school

The table gives a reading order an assistant can follow. Every step is tagged by provenance: **Rath** means his own srath.com page, read directly; **Student** means a student's written rule, mainly Rao, Poddar or SJC class notes; **Synthesis** means the researchers' ordering or inference, which no source states. The ordering itself is synthesis, built from Rath's intake pages and the students' technique rules. When speaking to a user, an assistant should attribute steps to "this tradition" or "the Jaimini/Parāśara method", never to Rath personally. It should not quote him except where his page says so verbatim.

## Conclusion

The school is better documented as an ethic than as an algorithm, at least in Rath's own words. What he wrote himself is the frame. The AK sets the soul's agenda. The stronger of lagna and 7th seeds the timing. The lunar return marks the year. The chart's own deities direct remedy. Free will lives in the mind. The astrologer times illness, never diagnoses it, and never sells. The machinery inside that frame comes overwhelmingly from Rao's textbook. That suits an engineering team, because Rao's rules are explicit enough to implement and test. It also creates a specific risk for an AI assistant: fluent output that sounds like the guru while actually reciting the student. The safe design attributes technique to "the tradition", not the person, and keeps the disputed conventions (year lord, karaka count, Shoola naming) as labelled options, not hidden choices.

The second implication is that the record leaves exactly what a consumer assistant most needs (how to deliver hard news, and whether to confirm the past before predicting) undocumented. Rath's sourced positions supply only guardrails: speak in probable futures, admit uncertainty, defer to medicine, and treat remedies as the person's own optional practice. The positive behaviour has to be the product's own design, stated as such. The most valuable next research step is to obtain *Narayana Dasa*, *Vedic Remedies in Astrology* and *Crux of Vedic Astrology*. Those three texts would move a large share of the student-sourced rows in the reading table into Rath's own column.
