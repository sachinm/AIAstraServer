# Build the You board around one dated season

The best self-insight products open the "You" screen with three things: **one named identity or state, one sentence of guidance, and one time horizon with an end date**. The "why", the contributors and the deep reading sit one tap down. Across astrology, wearables and personality products the same structure keeps returning. The top shows a hero (Co-Star's daily theme, Whoop's Recovery dial, 16Personalities' "INFJ (Advocate)" tagline, Oura's "most important score"). Under it sits a paired strengths/risks layer (CHANI's "gifts" and "challenges", Hogan's normal day versus "under pressure", Gallup's "Navigating blind spots"). Then comes a timing layer that people remember because it ends (The Pattern's dated cycles, Align27's 180-day green/amber/red planner, Rise's named energy windows). The products that fail fall into three groups. Some use blunt or verdict-like copy: Co-Star's "lovingly savage" lines and sleep scores read as a "moral scorecard". Some use jargon as section names: Vedic apps label sections Hora, Panchapakshi, Chandrashtama. Some keep adding paywalls and upsells. On audience, today's apps serve young women well: women 18–49 are the heaviest users in the US, and in India women aged 25–35 asking about marriage are the paying core. They fail men 35+, executives and founders who want decision-grade timing, families who need several charts, the 60+ group, and the very wealthy, who buy discretion and a named human rather than an app. Segmentation in the leading products comes from stated intent and opt-in content, not from rewriting copy by age or gender. A Vedic personal assistant should therefore copy five things. Name sections in plain life language. Pair every gift with its knot. Show every hard stretch as a season with an end date and one action. Ask what brought the user here, and confirm before predicting. Cut the generic essays, the percentile bars and the upsell blocks.

## Three layers repeat across every category: who you are, how you're doing now, and what's coming

Strip away branding and every leading product builds the self screen from the same three layers. **The identity layer** comes first and is never a number. 16Personalities opens with a type code, a nickname and a single tagline, then runs Introduction, Strengths & Weaknesses, Romantic Relationships, Friendships, Parenthood, Career Paths, Workplace Habits and Conclusion ([16Personalities](https://www.16personalities.com/infj-personality)). Insights Discovery anchors identity in a wheel position plus a type name such as "Motivating Inspirer" before any strengths appear ([Colourworks sample](https://www.thecolourworks.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/HollyMiller-26-Motivating-Inspirer-Classic-002.pdf)). PrinciplesYou gives up to three archetypes from 30, mapped on an "Archetype Archipelago" ([PrinciplesYou FAQ](https://support.principlesus.com/principlesyou-results-faq)). In astrology, The Pattern's "Your Pattern" is a slideshow of the birth-chart reading covering destiny, growth, instincts and communication ([Bustle, 2024](https://www.bustle.com/life/pattern-app-review-features-price)). CHANI organises the natal chart planet by planet, each split into gifts and challenges ([sunmoresun, 2024](https://sunmoresun.substack.com/p/chani-app-review)).

**The state layer** answers "how am I right now", and wellness apps have refined it most. Whoop puts three dials (Sleep, Recovery, Strain) on top. The colour bands mean the same thing on every screen: green at 67% and above, red below 33%. One design teardown describes each tile as "a doorway, not a destination" ([925 Studios, 2026](https://www.925studios.co/blog/whoop-design-breakdown)). Oura's Today puts its scores on top, then a highlight insight that changes with the time of day, which Oura compares to a news app's "Top Stories". The contributors live in a separate Vitals tab, each shown against the member's own baseline ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/blog/new-oura-app-experience/)). Co-Star's dashboard does the same in astrological form: a daily theme, Do/Don't lists, and category lines like "Trouble with friends" ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/costar-personalized-astrology)).

**The horizon layer** carries the time dimension. The Pattern has "Your Transits" covering past, present and future, a Time Travel scrubber, and in 2026 a Transit Ring and Upcoming Transits ([App Store, The Pattern](https://apps.apple.com/app/the-pattern/id1071085727)). CHANI layers daily, weekly and yearly readings, the yearly one running month by month across four life areas ([App Store, CHANI](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1532791252)). Oura's My Health holds the slow metrics and weekly, quarterly and yearly reports that list "strengths and areas to improve" ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/blog/new-oura-app-experience/)). BetterUp re-measures its 25-dimension Whole Person Report about every four months ([BetterUp support](https://support.betterup.com/hc/en-us/articles/360049794271-The-Whole-Person-Assessment)). That is the coaching version of a "this year" panel.

Personality and coaching reports keep a consistent order from the identity line onward. After the identity come the strengths, then a risk section framed as blind spots or derailers, then relationships or communication, then development suggestions. Executive products add a values layer and split behaviour into "normal" and "under pressure". Hogan's Leadership Forecast uses three lenses: the "bright side" (HPI), behaviours "that emerge during times of stress" (HDS), and core values and drivers (MVPI) ([Peter Berry](https://peterberry.com.au/leadership-forecast-series/)). These three lenses map closely onto a Vedic You board. The normal-day lens is mind and temperament. The under-pressure lens is struggles and knots. The drivers lens is the soul's aim.

The Vedic incumbents are the exception. Their self screens are tool menus, not narratives. Align27 offers a 180-day planner, Hora and Panchapakshi grids, a Chandrashtama tracker and rituals by life area ([App Store, Align27](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1364184571)). AstroSage lists Kundli, five-level Dasha, Sade Sati, Mangal Dosh, Kaal Sarp, Varshphal and Shadbala ([App Store, AstroSage](https://apps.apple.com/app/id824705526)). None of the Western leaders use technical terms as section names. The gap is plain: **no premium Vedic app names its self screen in life language**.

| Layer | Astrology leaders | Wellness leaders | Personality and coaching | Suggested You board name |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Identity | Your Pattern; CHANI birth chart by planet | n/a | Type + tagline; archetypes; theme ranking | Who you are |
| Paired strengths and risks | CHANI gifts/challenges | Oura "strengths and areas to improve" | Gallup blind spots; Hogan derailers; Insights opposite type | Gifts and knots |
| Under pressure | The Pattern "when you don't feel good" | Low Recovery / Rest Mode | Hogan HDS; Enneagram stress line | What tests you |
| Drivers | n/a | n/a | Hogan MVPI values | What you're here for |
| Now | Co-Star daily theme; Align27 colour days | Whoop dials; Oura Today | BetterUp reflection point | Where you are now |
| Horizon | The Pattern Timing; CHANI yearly | Oura reports; Whoop Health tab | BetterUp 4-month re-measure | Your season / This year |

## Hard news lands when it is paired, conditional and dated

How products word difficult material decides whether a struggling user stays. **Co-Star is the cautionary case.** Its CEO described the notifications as meant to be "conversational, zero bullshit, lovingly savage" ([Yahoo/InStyle, 2019](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/meet-banu-guler-ceo-co-150000063.html)). Lines like "you talk about other people because you don't have your own life" became memes ([Capital FM](https://www.capitalfm.com/internet/co-star-astrology-app-horoscope-meme/)). Critics say this tone clashes with people who come to astrology for comfort ([Inquirer SCOUT](https://lifestyle.inquirer.net/?p=66779)). The Pattern softens bad news by pairing states: a reading of who you are "when you feel good" next to one of who you are when you don't ([Bustle](https://www.bustle.com/life/pattern-app-review-features-price)). Even so, some App Store reviewers want more advice and encouragement ([App Store, The Pattern](https://apps.apple.com/app/the-pattern/id1071085727)). CHANI's gifts-and-challenges framing draws praise for warmth and is rated 4.9 from about 60,000 ratings ([App Store, CHANI](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1532791252)). The lesson is that pairing a hard statement with a kind one is not enough. **Every hard statement also needs something to do about it.**

Personality products have developed tested devices for describing weakness without fatalism. Hogan describes derailers as normal-condition strengths that "under stress or pressure" become career obstacles ([Hogan HDS FAQ](https://www.hoganassessments.com/blog/faq-blog-series-the-hogan-development-survey/)). Gallup calls weak areas "lesser strengths" and says outright that blind spots are a risk, not a promise that "you will in fact trip" ([Gallup](https://www.gallup.com/cliftonstrengths/en/249404/insights-help-understand-people-weaknesses.aspx)). PrinciplesYou says there is "nothing inherently 'good' or 'bad'" about any score and uses "may have a tendency" phrasing ([PrinciplesYou FAQ](https://support.principlesus.com/principlesyou-results-faq)). The Kaplan–Kaiser Leadership Versatility Index rates behaviour as too little, the right amount, or too much, and finds that senior leaders over-rely on the strengths that made them successful ([Training Mag](https://trainingmag.com/?p=11950)). The most transferable pattern for an astrology product is **the gift that becomes a knot under pressure**. A Vedic chart naturally describes a placement's strength and its strain together, so the "gifts and knots" card already suits the material.

One counter-view: Zenger Folkman argue that asking people to watch for a strength becoming "too effective" is "terribly confusing" ([Zenger Folkman, 2019](https://data.zengerfolkman.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/Can-Strengths-Be-Taken-Too-Far_WP-2019.pdf)). The fix is to make the knot concrete (a situation and a behaviour) rather than abstract.

Wellness has added evidence on tone. Sleep doctor Michael Breus warns that a score can become "a digital moral scorecard". In one study, false "slept poorly" feedback made participants perform worse on cognitive tests ([Harper's Bazaar via AOL, 2026](https://www.aol.com/articles/oura-ring-judging-160543237.html)). Oura's answers have been Rest Mode, softer messaging, and an Advisor whose voice the user picks: supportive or accountability-style ([Oura Advisor](https://ouraring.com/blog/oura-advisor)). Whoop is criticised for copy that sounds robotic and full of jargon ([925 Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/whoop-design-breakdown)). Noom shows the opposite failure. It is praised for "compassionate and empathetic language" ([HelpGuide](https://helpguide.org/handbook/healthy-living/noom-review)), but critics argue the soft wording hides calorie restriction ([InsideHook](https://www.insidehook.com/wellness/course-noom-diet)). Both failures matter for astrology. Planet and dosha words in headlines are the astrological version of Whoop's jargon. AstroSage, for example, names Sade Sati and Kaal Sarp outright as prediction categories ([App Store, AstroSage](https://apps.apple.com/app/id824705526)). Vague, upbeat filler is the astrological version of Noom's euphemism.

For someone who arrives with a problem, the evidence points to a four-part sentence:

1. Name the season, not a verdict ("a demanding stretch").
2. Say why in life terms.
3. Give an end date.
4. Give one small action.

Ask "how does this match what you feel?" before the prediction. This matches the psychologist's advice to check in with yourself before reading the score.

**Barnum risk is the last test for tone.** Critics liken 16Personalities and the MBTI to horoscopes "with a sciencey flair", because the descriptions are positive and fit anyone ([Vice](https://vice.com/en/article/the-myers-briggs-personality-test-bullshit)). An astrology app starts out under that suspicion. The products that resist it point to something checkable: norm-group percentiles, behavioural specifics, the gap between self-view and reputation, and explicit "may not apply" caveats ([Hogan Summary sample](https://performanceprograms.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/Hogan-Summary-Report-Sample.pdf)). For a You board, that means each statement names a situation, a behaviour or a time window. Each profile includes at least one kind but unflattering knot. And the user confirms facts before the board makes claims built on them.

## Timing works when a window has a name, a task and an end

Timing is where astrology has an edge over every other category. It is also where the best non-astrology apps already show how to display it. **Rise is the cleanest model.** It predicts a daily curve (morning peak, afternoon dip, evening peak, a one-hour Melatonin Window) and matches each window to a type of work: peaks for demanding tasks, the dip for email and admin ([Rise help](https://help.risescience.com/hc/en-us/articles/6654243671191-What-is-my-Daily-Energy-Energy-Schedule); [Rise blog](https://www.risescience.com/blog/circadian-energy-peak-sales-productivity)). Whoop's 2025 Daily Outlook predicts energy and the best training times, and its Day in Review closes the evening ([Gadgets & Wearables](https://gadgetsandwearables.com/?p=16084720)). Align27 applies the same logic at astrological scale: green, amber and red days, Golden, Productive and Silence "moments", a 180-day planner, calendar sync, and a date-finder that users single out as a favourite ([App Store, Align27](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1364184571)). Moving from hours to weeks gives the shape a Vedic You board needs: **a named window, a verb and a date range**, such as "Stronger from 1 Dec: start new work".

The end date carries the emotional weight. The Pattern's Timing section lists the dates and meanings of the cycles affecting you. One reviewer was told a Neptune transit "will last for several more months" ([Bustle](https://www.bustle.com/life/pattern-app-review-features-price)). One user recalled their first completed cycle as "scarily accurate" and said they felt like a different person the day it ended ([Hilltop Views](https://hilltopviewsonline.com/19784/life-and-arts/love-astrology-but-struggle-with-its-vocab-this-app-might-be-for-you)). That is the retention mechanism to copy: the app predicted an ending, and the ending arrived. No app found in this research uses verbatim "this eases on <date>" wording on screen. That makes it an open opportunity rather than an established convention. It is also why combining The Pattern's dated cycles with Align27's colour scale is new ground.

Horizons need layers, and the layers need restraint. Every mature wellness app separates today, the recent trend against a baseline, and the long term. Apple Health keeps Highlights (current state) apart from Trends (several weeks) ([Apple support](https://support.apple.com/104997)). Whoop goes from tile to week-over-week trend to deep graph ([925 Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/whoop-design-breakdown)). Breus advises judging weekly trends rather than single nights ([AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/oura-ring-judging-160543237.html)), which matters most for anxious, high-achieving users. A You board can copy this directly. "Where you are now" holds the present state. "This season" covers the next four to eight weeks. "This year" holds the arc. A weekly digest suits a busy professional better than daily swings.

Users also want the curve, not only the label. When Garmin replaced its full Body Battery graph with a highs-and-lows view, forum users asked for the old graph back ([Garmin forums](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-mobile-andriod/344436/is-there-a-way-to-go-back-to-the-old-body-battery-graph)). The Pattern added a Transit Ring in 2026 ([App Store, The Pattern](https://apps.apple.com/app/the-pattern/id1071085727)). A horizontal band from now to year end, with each window shaded and labelled in life terms, shows the evidence without exposing the engine behind it. This research found no sources that describe the Transit Ring's visual design.

## Segmentation comes from intent and life stage, not demographic rewrites

None of the products studied rewrite their core copy by gender or age. Personality vendors segment by **report type**: Gallup's manager, leader, sales and student reports ([Gallup](https://www.gallup.com/cliftonstrengths/en/253868/popular-cliftonstrengthsassessment-products.aspx)), MBTI's College report ([Psychometrics](https://www.psychometrics.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/JSample_Step_I_-_Interpretive_Report_College.pdf)), Hogan's High Potential report, and Insights chapters assigned person by person ([Insights](https://support.insights.com/how-to-guides/understanding-and-assigning-chapters-for-insights-discovery-personal-profiles)). Astrology apps bring in life stage through content. The clearest example is CHANI's 7-part Saturn Return course, which reaches people around 27–30 without asking their age ([App Store, CHANI](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1532791252)). Wellness apps use **stated intent plus opt-in**. Oura asks new members to choose an intention such as cycle tracking. It shows cycle phases only when the contraception answer allows it, and "Prefer not to say" quietly hides those features rather than blocking the user ([Oura support](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/51359344232211)). This research found no evidence that Co-Star, The Pattern or CHANI collect pronouns or gender. The one product-level complaint about fit came from a Truity reviewer who called a report "not practical for my stage in life" ([Truity reviews](https://www.truity.com/customer-reviews/enneagram-personality-test?p=15)).

The audience data explains why "what brings you here" beats demographic fields. In the US, **46% of women aged 18–49 consult astrology at least yearly**, against 18–20% belief among men. LGBT adults are the most engaged group at 54% ([Pew Research Center, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/)). In India, AstroTalk's core customers are aged 25–35 and **60% are women**, and about **65% of revenue comes from marriage questions** ([Inc42, Aug 2026](https://inc42.com/features/can-astrotalk-chart-a-future-beyond-astrology/)). AstroYogi reports relationships leading among Gen Z, with career anxiety a top driver ([Upstox, 2025](https://upstox.com/news/upstox-originals/investing/stars-on-demand-how-digital-astrology-is-booming-in-india/article-167792)). Coaching skews older and more senior: clients are most often 35–44 (37%) or 45–54 (32%), and 56% of coaches say their clients are mostly managers or executives ([ICF, 2023](https://coachingfederation.org/app/uploads/2023/11/ExecutiveSummary_2023ICFFutureofCoachingSnapshot.pdf)). Taken together, each life stage brings a different problem. Under 25 the question is identity and "will this pass". From 25 to 35 it is marriage timing, job switches, moves and the first money decisions. From 36 to 45 it is a plateau, marriage strain and burnout. From 46 to 60 it is children's marriages, ageing parents and succession. Over 60 it is health, spiritual practice and the daily panchang. No survey ranks these questions by age band, so treat this map as reasoned rather than measured. What it does show is that **the life stage decides which panel leads**, while one well-written core reading can serve everyone.

Gender should change what the board shows, not how it speaks. Copy should stay in the second person with no gendered pronouns. Compatibility should never assume a heterosexual pair, which matters more given how engaged LGBT users are. Gender-specific panels should appear only by opt-in, on Oura's model. The data also hints at a framing lever. Men use astrology less, yet the coaching market they buy into is 58% women and mostly managers and executives ([Tandem Coach](https://tandemcoach.co/coaching-industry-statistics/)). That suggests "decision support for work" brings men in where "horoscope" does not. The claim that men's questions lean towards career and money is anecdotal and has not been measured.

## Premium buyers pay for decisions, discretion and depth, and today's apps miss most of them

The money in this category sits in decision moments, not daily content. AstroTalk made about ₹1,176 crore in FY25 operating revenue from paid live chats, with an average order of about ₹210 ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/start-up/news/astrotalk-reports-85-revenue-growth-in-fy25-as-tier-i-cities-boost-platform-activity); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/can-astrotalk-chart-a-future-beyond-astrology/)). Repeat consultations drive that business, which points to recurring worry rather than one-off curiosity. Western apps have reach but thin monetisation. Co-Star reached about 4.3M monthly users and was bought by Midjourney in July 2026 ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/private-equity/ai-lab-midjourney-buys-astrology-startup-co-star-plans-new-apps)), while most US users say astrology is "just for fun" (20%) rather than a source of insight (10%) ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/)). Premium self content costs about **$9–15 a month**:

| App | Price | What premium adds |
|---|---|---|
| Co-Star | Pro-Star $8.99/month; "Your Year Ahead" $11.99 and "Advanced Chart: Self" $8.99 sold separately | Depth, year ahead ([App Store, Co-Star](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1264782561)) |
| The Pattern | Go Deeper+ $14.99/month | Time Travel, Romantic Timing and unlimited Bonds ([App Store, The Pattern](https://apps.apple.com/app/the-pattern/id1071085727)) |
| CHANI | $11.99/month | ([App Store, CHANI](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1532791252)) |
| Align27 | $9.99/month; one question $9.99; Panchapakshi $29.99 | Single questions and tools sold separately ([App Store, Align27](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1364184571)) |

Across these apps, premium sells three things: depth (the full reading, personal timing, the year ahead), questions, and relationships.

Premium users punish pressure. A 2025 Co-Star reviewer complains the app is now "largely locked", with the free widget and daily content cut back ([App Store, Co-Star](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1264782561)). Align27 users object that loyal subscribers pay more than new ones. AstroSage users accuse human astrologers of stalling to stretch paid sessions ([App Store, AstroSage](https://apps.apple.com/app/id824705526)). Nebula draws complaints about too many notifications, forced extra payments and hidden cancellation ([mindbodygreen, 2025](https://www.mindbodygreen.com/articles/nebula-review)). Truity users balk at being "asked to pay $30 for the results" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/truity.com?page=5)). **A flat premium price with no upsell on the self screen is itself a feature for the high-earner tier.**

Each segment responds to something different. **Women 25–45** respond to warmth plus specificity. CHANI's personal-feeling weekly readings and The Pattern's sense of being "seen" are the most praised qualities in reviews ([App Store, CHANI](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1532791252); [App Store, The Pattern](https://apps.apple.com/app/the-pattern/id1071085727)). For them, relationship and marriage timing is the paid question. **Young professionals** respond to Rise-style windows tied to tasks and to a weekly brief. **Executives** respond to consequence-led, dense, scored material with one clear action, which is how Hogan, Kaplan–Kaiser and BetterUp are built. Hogan's framing of the gap between what people see "in an interview" and "after prolonged exposure" ([Hogan Summary sample](https://performanceprograms.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/Hogan-Summary-Report-Sample.pdf)) speaks to reputation and career risk, not mood. The executive evidence has limits, though. The wellness signals (Whoop's Coach and Advanced Labs, Oura Advisor, 83% of members rating its answers trustworthy) are vendor-reported ([Oura Advisor](https://ouraring.com/blog/oura-advisor)). No independent study of what executives value in these products turned up.

**High earners and the ultra-wealthy** are the clearest group current apps fail. In the US, belief in astrology falls with income (37% among lower-income adults, 16% among upper-income) ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/)). Use at the top is therefore private rather than mass. Joan Quigley timed Nancy Reagan's schedule for about seven years ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Quigley)). The Ambani wedding date of 12 July 2024 was chosen on the family pujari's advice ([Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/national/why-the-ambanis-chose-july-12-for-anant-ambani-radhika-merchant-wedding-a505)). Bollywood clients ask about release muhurats, title spellings, when to step back and when to speak during a PR crisis. They pay ₹12,300 for a written report or ₹23,100 with a 6–9 minute call, and their advisors stress encrypted channels and secrecy ([THR India, 2025](https://www.hollywoodreporterindia.com/amp/story/features/interviews/bollywood-celebrity-astrologers-how-big-decisions-in-cinema-are-made-after-a-call-to-the-stargazers)). What this tier buys is timing, naming, discretion, a named human, and charts for a whole circle (heirs, partners, counterparties, hires). It is a concierge service, not an app tier. Other groups are underserved too, often in the same ways. Men 35+ want decision-grade windows. The 46–60 group wants family charts. The 60+ group wants the panchang and gentle, reverent tone. Non-English Vedic users have few apps to choose from, though that last point is inferred, since no data on language share was found.

## Concrete screen patterns for a Vedic You board

The evidence points to a specific build. **Above the fold** should hold four things: a hero, one sentence, a band, and a row of chips.

1. The hero is a named state with a tone word, such as Demanding, Steady or Opening.
2. The sentence says why in life terms.
3. The band runs from now to year end, with the current window shaded and its end date printed.
4. The row holds two or three chips.

This follows the direction of the leaders. Oura cut five tabs to three ([9to5Google, 2025](https://9to5google.com/2025/10/20/oura-app-redesign/)). Whoop shows three tiles with detail behind a tap. Headspace rebuilt its Today tab in June 2026 from member feedback ([Headspace help](https://help.headspace.com/hc/en-us/articles/51443842967195-Refreshed-Today-Tab-Experience-June-2026)). The Pattern reorganised its You tab into swipeable collections in May 2026 ([App Store, The Pattern](https://apps.apple.com/app/the-pattern/id1071085727)). That supports modular panels: a carousel with chips and dots on phone, tabs on desktop, no stacked scroll.

Two habits from Whoop are worth copying exactly. Tone colours should mean the same thing on You, Today, Love and Work. And the hero should never carry the contributors. One tap opens "what is driving this", listed as life areas, never planets or houses. Following Oura and Whoop, the hero framing can change by time of day: a morning outlook and an evening review.

**Below the fold**, the panels follow the three lenses, each as a card in the carousel:

| Panel | What it holds | Model it follows |
|---|---|---|
| Who you are | An identity line plus one tagline. No score. | 16Personalities, Insights |
| Gifts and knots | One card per gift, its paired knot, and a three-step strip: use it, watch it, try this week. | Gallup action items, the Kaplan–Kaiser do more / do less |
| What tests you | The under-pressure lens, with a "when well / when strained" toggle. | Hogan HDS, Enneagram health bands |
| What you're here for | The drivers lens. | Hogan MVPI values |
| This season and this year | Dated windows, each a verb plus a range, with a best window and a caution window. | The Pattern Timing, Align27 planner, Rise windows |

The "This year" panel is re-checked on a set schedule, as BetterUp re-measures every four months. Section names stay in plain language. The working names "Who you are", "Gifts and knots", "What tests you", "Where you are now" and "This season" all avoid the Vedic incumbents' jargon.

**What to cut** is just as clear from the evidence. Cut the following:

1. Percentile bars and 0–100 scores for chart-derived traits. They imply false precision, and scores invite the moral-scorecard reaction.
2. Generic essays on love, friendship and parenthood, and long conclusions. These are 16Personalities' filler sections and the main source of Barnum criticism.
3. Dosha and planet names in headlines.
4. Co-Star-style one-line verdicts.
5. Upsell blocks inside the self screen.
6. Any copy that guesses gender.

Keep a way to see the curve, because Garmin's users show that removing real evidence costs trust.

## Conclusion

The cross-category reading changes what a "You" screen is for. Astrology apps treat it as a portrait. Wellness apps treat it as a gauge. Coaching products treat it as a development plan. The best version for a problem-first Vedic assistant combines all three, and it adds the one thing none of them does: **tie a named difficulty the user has stated to a dated window that ends.** No app found in this research publicly links stated intent to timing. The Pattern dates cycles but does not ask what you came for. Oura asks your intention but has no forecast. AstroTalk sells answers to anxious, time-bound questions only through paid human chats. A board that asks first, confirms before predicting, and then says "this eases after mid-December; here is one thing to do now" sits in the space between those products.

The audience evidence also shows where growth lies beyond today's core of young women. A "career and timing counsel" frame could reach men and executives. Family and circle boards with a muhurta calendar would serve the 46–60 group and high-net-worth households. The ultra-wealthy belong in a separate, human-led concierge offer rather than a richer app tier.
