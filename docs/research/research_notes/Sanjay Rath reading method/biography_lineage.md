## 1. Lineage (Puri, Odisha; Sri Achyuta Dasa tradition) and how he describes it

### Takeaway
Rath presents himself as the heir of a hereditary Puri family of astrologers whose Jyotish line goes back to Sri Achyuta Dasa (Achyutananda), one of the Pañca Sakhā of Odisha. He learned from his uncle Pt. Kasinath Rath. His grandfather Pt. Jagannath Rath was a published astrologer titled "Jyotish Ratna of Orissa". Every source for the lineage is Rath's own material or a student's, and no independent verification was found.

### Cited Findings
- He "belongs to a traditional family of astrologers from Bira Balabhadrapur Sasan village of Puri, Orissa" whose lineage traces to Shri Achyuta Das (Sri Achyutananda), one of the Pañca Sakhā, the five Mahāpuruṣa of Odisha. — [srath.com About](https://srath.com/series/about)
- His site's wording: "Our lineage of Vedic astrology comes from the holy feet of Sri Achyutananda." — [srath.com About](https://srath.com/about/)
- He studied Jyotish from a young age under his uncle, the late Pandit Kasinath Rath, "in the traditional parampara". — [srath.com About](https://srath.com/series/about); repeated by student [Vijaya, vijayajyoti.com](https://vijayajyoti.com/?p=347)
- Grandfather: the late Pandit Jagannath Rath, "Jyotish Ratna of Orissa", author of many books on Jyotish. — [srath.com About](https://srath.com/series/about); [vijayajyoti.com](https://vijayajyoti.com/?p=347)
- The "Rathātreya" family page puts the family in the Atri gotra. The direct line it lists is Pt. Somanath Rath, Pt. Ramakrusna Rath, Pt. Jagannath Rath, then Pt. Kashinath Rath ("third son of Pt. Jagannath Rath"), then Pt. Sanjay Rath, "nephew of Pt. Kashinath Rath & son of Umakant Rath". The family is said to have moved from Somanāth (Dwarka) to Kanyakubja about 700 years ago and to have reached Orissa "a few centuries back". The page calls Sri Acyutānanda Das "one of the Pañca sakhā" of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, "considered the reincarnation of Sri Garuḍa", and says the family traces its Jyotiṣa tradition to him. It also says Rath studied at Bishop Cotton Boys School, Bangalore (1980–82), and left government service in 2000 "to continue the family lineage". — [srath.com Rathātreya page](https://srath.com/?p=19) (self-published)
- SJC teaching follows "the jyotish parampara of Sri Achyuta Dasa of Jagannath, Puri". The site places Mahapurush Achyuta Dasa as a disciple of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. — [srath.com, Sri Jagannath Center page](https://srath.com/?p=41)
- He names Brihat Parasara Hora Shastra, Jaimini Upadesa Sutras, Brihat Jataka and Saravali as the foundations of his teaching, plus the Gita, Upanishads and Puranas. He calls himself a strict follower of the ancient seers who does not present "his own brand" of astrology. — [srath.com About](https://srath.com/series/about)
- His student Visti Larsen writes that Pt. Jagannath Rath (the grandfather) favoured Vimsottari Dasa. — [Larsen, *Jyotisa Fundamentals: My Master's Words*, Exotic India listing/preface](https://www.exoticindiaart.com/book/details/jyotisa-fundamentals-my-master-s-words-idl017/)
- Birth: 1963 in Sambalpur, Odisha. — Wikipedia mirror, via search snippet ([cs.odu.edu mirror](https://www.cs.odu.edu/~salam/wsdl/inforet/wikihtml/Sanjay_Rath_7aeb.html)). srath.com gives no birth data. Single-sourced.

### Inferences
- "Achyuta Dasa lineage" works as both a family claim and an institutional brand. SJC teaching is described as transmitting it, and Larsen describes joining SJC "to learn astrology as taught in the lineage of Sri Achyuta Dasa of Puri". The lineage is presented as transmissible through guru-shishya, not only by blood.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-Rath, non-student) source was found that verifies the Achyuta Dasa descent or the details of Pt. Jagannath Rath's titles and books.
- No date of birth beyond "1963" was found, and that year rests on one secondary source.

## 3. Organisations and programmes, with dates

### Takeaway
The Sri Jagannath Center (SJC) is the umbrella. It began informally in 1997, was established in 1998 (possibly first as "Sri Jagannath Vedic Center", SJVC) and was registered as a society in New Delhi in May 2003. The Devaguru Bṛhaspati Center (DBC) is an SJC subsidiary that runs the paid long-form programmes, and Devaguru Brihaspati USA is a 501(c)(3). The Jaimini Scholar Program's first run was 2007–2011 in the Himalayas (dates from a search summary of srath.com, not read directly). The Parāśara Jyotiṣa Course is a five-year online-plus-contact programme that has taken batches yearly since about 2011–2013.

### Cited Findings
- **Sri Jagannath Center (SJC):** "established in 1998 (1997 started informally)" in India; registered as a non-profit society under the Societies Registration Act, New Delhi, since May 2003. It aims to teach jyotish in the tradition of Parasara and Jaimini, plus mantra shastra, numerology, vastu and yoga, and to "restore jyotish's dignity" against quackery. It uses a guru-shishya model with a "panel of jyotish gurus", e-lists (Achyuta Gurukulam, Varahamihira, Vedic Astrology), free online classes and study circles worldwide. — [srath.com, SJC page](https://srath.com/?p=41)
- He "originally founded the Sri Jagannath Vedic Center (SJVC) in 1998 as a platform for open discussion about the teachings of his Jyotish tradition". — Wikipedia mirror, via search snippet ([IPFS mirror](https://ipfs.aleph.im/ipfs/QmXoypizjW3WknFiJnKLwHCnL72vedxjQkDDP1mXWo6uco/wiki/Sanjay_Rath.html))
- SJC held annual conferences: Visti Larsen won "Best Presentation" at SJC's 4th Annual Conference in Mumbai (2005). SJC also ran SJCERC, a graduate and postgraduate programme. — [Larsen book listing](https://www.exoticindiaart.com/book/details/jyotisa-fundamentals-my-master-s-words-idl017/)
- **Devaguru Bṛhaspati Center (DBC)** is "a subsidiary of SJC". It runs the Jaimini Scholar Program, the Parāśara Jyotiṣa Course, the Mantra Śāstra programmes and "Jaimini India". **Devaguru Brihaspati USA** is announced as a 501(c)(3) non-profit, with no founding date given. — [srath.com About](https://srath.com/about/)
- **SoHamsa | DBC courses:** online Jyotish courses taught by him, with classrooms and assistant teachers. — [srath.com About](https://srath.com/series/about). A search summary also said SoHamsa describes its two professional courses as the Jaimini Scholar Program and the Parāśara Jyotiṣa Course (search summary; exact srath.com page not confirmed).
- **Jaimini Scholar Program (JSP):** a search summary of srath.com pages ([JSP category](https://srath.com/category/learn/jaimini-scholar-program), [JSP page](https://srath.com/jaimini-scholar-program/)) gave these details, none of them read directly: "conceptualised and taught in the traditional manner by Pt. Sanjay Rath at Almora and Bhimtal, Himalayas"; a four-part course over five years with 28 principal lessons in Year 1; first run 2007–11, ending in late October 2011; DBC certification to teach, only when learning under another Jaimini Scholar. Visti Larsen as head of the JSP also comes from a search summary (exact page not confirmed).
- **Parāśara Jyotiṣa Course (PJC):** "A five year online and contact program". Lessons are online at devaguru.com, with about 10 days of contact training each March in the Himalayas. The academic year starts at Makara saṅkrānti (Himalaya Batch-II references January 2013). Mentors: Pt. Sanjay Rath for batch 1, Dr. Andrew Foss for batch 2, Sarbani Rath for batch 3. "PJC 2016 Admissions" was the fourth batch. DBC organises it, and a "Parāśara Jyotiṣa Paṇḍita program" is mentioned. — [srath.com PJC page](https://srath.com/parasara-jyoti%E1%B9%A3a-course/). A search summary also said batches have run yearly since 2011 (exact page not confirmed).
- **Mantra Śāstra programmes** are listed under DBC. No founding year was found. — [srath.com About](https://srath.com/about/)
- **Sagittarius Publications** publishes and distributes *The Jyotish Digest* and Vedic astrology books, including Rath's. srath.com does not explicitly say he founded it. — [srath.com About](https://srath.com/series/about). The Wikipedia mirror says he founded *The Jyotish Digest* — via search snippet ([cs.odu.edu mirror](https://www.cs.odu.edu/~salam/wsdl/inforet/wikihtml/Sanjay_Rath_7aeb.html))
- He runs a podcast, "Rath's Jyotish", on Apple Podcasts. — [Apple Podcasts listing](https://geo.itunes.apple.com/ca/podcast/feed/id1505029497?at=11lLuB) (title only; contents not checked)
- srath.com carries posts titled "Sivananda Ashram 15th Annual Vedic Astrology Conference" and "18th Vedic Astrology Conference" (a "SAYF 18th Vedic Astrology Conference 2017" title also appears in its archive). These are search-result titles only, not read. — [srath.com ?p=3105](https://srath.com/?p=3105); [srath.com ?p=5812](https://srath.com/?p=5812); [srath.com/page/14](https://srath.com/page/14/)

### Inferences
- The structure moved from free e-list and conference teaching (SJC, about 1998–2000s) to certified multi-year paid programmes under DBC (JSP from 2007, PJC from about 2011–13) delivered online through devaguru.com and SoHamsa.

## 4. Books and published works

### Takeaway
His core titles are *Maharishi Jaimini's Upadesa Sutras* (translation), *Crux of Vedic Astrology: Timing of Events* (1998), *Vedic Remedies in Astrology* (2000, though some listings say 2007), *Narayana Dasa* (2001), *Vimsottari and Udu Dasas* and *Collected Papers in Vedic Astrology*, mostly from Sagar Publications, plus *Brihat Nakshatra* (Sagittarius, edition 2008). Retailer listings give conflicting years, and first-edition years should be treated as approximate.

### Cited Findings
- ***Crux of Vedic Astrology: Timing of Events***, Sagar Publications, ISBN 9788170822486. Open Library has a 1998 edition with 589 pages, and other listings give 560 or 596 pages, with reprints in 2016, 2020 and 2022. It covers the various houses, introduces divisional charts, and covers Narayana Dasa, Shula Dasa and other tools. — [Open Library](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/OL24940905M); description from Wikipedia mirror via search snippet ([cs.odu.edu mirror](https://www.cs.odu.edu/~salam/wsdl/inforet/wikihtml/Sanjay_Rath_7aeb.html)); listing [Exotic India](https://exoticindia.com/book/details/crux-of-vedic-astrology-timing-of-events-idj605)
- ***Vedic Remedies in Astrology***, Sagar Publications, on remedial measures. The Wikipedia mirror dates it to 2000, while Bagchee (hardcover, 31.12.2007) and Goodreads (first published 1 January 2007) give 2007. The 2007 date is probably a later edition. — [Bagchee](https://www.bagchee.com/books/BB102498/vedic-remedies-in-astrology); [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/20107720); [Exotic India](https://www.exoticindiaart.com/book/details/vedic-remedies-in-astrology-idj596/)
- ***Narayana Dasa***, Sagar Publications: "a definitive explication of Narayana dasa, a Vedic timing technique utilizing the signs of the zodiac". The Wikipedia mirror says 2001, and Exotic India lists a 2019 edition. — [Exotic India](https://www.exoticindiaart.com/book/details/narayana-dasa-nae836/); mirror via search snippet
- ***Maharishi Jaimini's Upadesa Sutras*** (translation and commentary). Also listed as a Sagittarius title, "Maharishi Jaimini Upadesa Sutra". — [srath.com About](https://srath.com/about/); [Motilal Banarsidass listing context](https://motilalbanarsidass.com/collections/astrology/products/vimsottari-and-udu-dasas-parasaras-key-to-prognostication). ## 5. Notable students and what they say

### Takeaway
His best-documented student is Visti Larsen, who joined SJC in 2001, dedicated his first book to Rath and is reportedly head of the Jaimini Scholar Program. Sarbani Rath, his wife, mentors in his programmes. Freedom Cole's profile claims initiation into an Odisha lineage but does not name Rath; the only direct link found is a Scribd transcript of uncertain reliability. Dr. Andrew Foss is documented only as a PJC mentor. Students who write about him use devotional guru terms.

### Cited Findings
- **Visti Larsen**, born 21 November 1981 in Nairobi. He studied Western astrology from 1998, moved to Jyotisa in 2000 and joined SJC in 2001 "to learn astrology as taught in the lineage of Sri Achyuta Dasa of Puri". At his guru's request he became a jyotish guru and began teaching in 2002. His first book, *Jyotisa Fundamentals: My Master's Words* (Sagittarius, 2005), is dedicated to Rath and the lineage and grew from years of travelling with Rath to conferences in the USA, UK and India. — [Exotic India listing/preface](https://www.exoticindiaart.com/book/details/jyotisa-fundamentals-my-master-s-words-idl017/). He is styled "Jyotisha Pandita and Jaimini Scholar" — [momjunction reviewer page](https://www.momjunction.com/reviewer/visti-larsen) — and taught at SJCERC (same listing). His headship of the Jaimini Scholar Program comes from a search summary only.
- **Sarbani Rath**, his wife (spouse relation from a Wikipedia mirror, via search summary), mentored PJC batch 3. — [srath.com PJC page](https://srath.com/parasara-jyoti%E1%B9%A3a-course/)
- **Freedom Cole** is described as a practitioner of Jyotiṣa, Āyurveda and Yoga therapy, "initiated into a traditional Jyotiṣa lineage of the royal astrologers of Odisha". The profile does not name Rath. — [UAC 2026 speaker profile](https://uac2026.sched.com/speaker/freedom_cole.232lgg8j). A Scribd transcript of uncertain reliability has Rath's 2003 talk "Lineage and Avatara" at SJC Vyasa, California, hosted by Freedom T. Cole, and refers to Freedom as one of Rath's students. — [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/680329909/On-Creation-and-Avatars)
- **Dr. Andrew Foss** mentored PJC batch 2. That documents a teaching role in Rath's programme, not studentship. — [srath.com PJC page](https://srath.com/parasara-jyoti%E1%B9%A3a-course/)
- **Vijaya** (London, vijayajyoti.com): "Pandit Sanjay Rath is my Jyotish Guru". She says he "spearheaded dissemination of this traditional and sacred knowledge" worldwide through DBC and SJC. — [vijayajyoti.com](https://vijayajyoti.com/?p=347)
