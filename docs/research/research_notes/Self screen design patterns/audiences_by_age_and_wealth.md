# Audiences for astrology, coaching and self-insight products: by age, life stage, gender and wealth tier

Research date: 2026-11-09. Older material is marked with its year. Quotes are kept under 15 words.
Method note: about 18 searches and fetches. Primary sources are Pew (2024 fieldwork, 2025 report), Inc42 (Aug 2026), Bloomberg (Jul 2026), The Hollywood Reporter India (Sep/Oct 2025), Dazed (Sep 2024), and ICF coach surveys (2023). Most market-size numbers come from paid-report press releases and disagree widely. Treat them as rough.

## Q1. For each segment: top questions, preferred tone, depth, and how timing should be shown

### Takeaway
Hard data exists only at a coarse level. Pew shows belief and use peak among women 18–49 and LGBT adults. AstroTalk/AstroYogi show India's paying core is 25–35, about 60% women, and marriage-led (about 65% of AstroTalk revenue). Coaching clients skew older (35–54) and are mostly managers or executives. Celebrity and business clients ask about timing (release dates, launches), names, career moves and crisis handling. No source gives a ranked "top 5 questions" list for each age band. The per-segment lists below are inferences built on the cited anchors, and they are marked as inferences.

### Cited Findings
**Age and gender anchors (US)**
- 27% of US adults believe in astrology: women 35%, men 18%. By age and sex: women 18–49 43%, women 50+ 27%, men 18–49 20%, men 50+ 16%. Belief barely changed since 2017 (29%). Fieldwork Oct 21–27, 2024, n=9,593. — [Pew Research Center, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/)
- 28% consult astrology or horoscopes at least yearly, including 37% of women and 46% of women 18–49. LGBT adults consult at 54% (LGBT women 63%, LGBT men 40%). — [Pew, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/)
- Most users say it is "just for fun" (20%) rather than "helpful insights" (10%). 17% of adults aged 18–29 say insight. — [Pew, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/)
- Few rely on astrology for "major decisions about your life": 1% "a lot" and 5% "a little". Among subgroups, younger adults are at 13%, Asian Americans 12%, Black Americans 11%, and LGBT adults 21% (at least a little). — [Pew, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/)
- Americans under 65 are more likely than those 65+ to believe. Fortune-teller use is 14% under 30 vs 2% at 65+. Tarot is nearly a quarter at 18–29 vs 3% at 65+. — [Pew, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/)

**Age and gender anchors (India, Vedic)**
- AstroTalk's core customers are aged 25–35, women are 60% of customers, and close to 65% of revenue comes from marriage questions. — [Inc42, 16 Aug 2026](https://inc42.com/features/can-astrotalk-chart-a-future-beyond-astrology/)
- AstroYogi (2023 survey of 500,000): 53.8% of Gen Z sought astrological guidance. Women are 60% of AstroYogi users. Users were mostly 25–35 before skewing younger after COVID. Relationships lead for Gen Z, and "career anxiety" is a top driver. COVID-era health questions are cited without figures. — [Upstox, 2025](https://upstox.com/news/upstox-originals/investing/stars-on-demand-how-digital-astrology-is-booming-in-india/article-167792)

**Under 25: identity and self-diagnosis context**
- In a PLOS ONE study, many top ADHD TikTok videos misalign with clinical criteria. Viewers grew more confident in self-diagnosed ADHD. — [News-Medical, Mar 2025](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20250325/TikTok-fuels-ADHD-self-diagnosis-with-misleading-content-study-shows.aspx)
- A Tebra poll found 30% of Gen Z had self-diagnosed a mental health problem from social media (poll date unconfirmed). A Made of Millions survey says few rely solely on the internet. — [Harvard Petrie-Flom, Apr 2025](https://petrieflom.law.harvard.edu/2025/04/02/dr-tiktok-the-impacts-of-misinformation-on-mental-health-self-diagnosis); [MBRSC](https://mbrsc.aud.edu/MBRSCPost/from-scroll-to-self-diagnosis/)
- Co-Star users were reported as 87% female, average age 24 (figure undated, treat as old). — [Ipsos](https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/how-people-see-their-zodiac-sign)

**36–54: the coaching core**
- ICF 2023: coaches report clients aged 35–44 most often (37%), then 45–54 (32%). Under-35s are about 21%, a mix similar to 2019. — [ICF Future of Coaching Snapshot, 2023](https://coachingfederation.org/app/uploads/2023/11/ExecutiveSummary_2023ICFFutureofCoachingSnapshot.pdf); figures also via [Tandem Coach](https://tandemcoach.co/coaching-industry-statistics/)
- 56% of coaches say their clients are mostly managers (31%) or executives (25%). 57% of clients are sponsored by an employer or another party (2022). — [ICF 2023](https://coachingfederation.org/app/uploads/2023/11/ExecutiveSummary_2023ICFFutureofCoachingSnapshot.pdf)
- About 58% of coaching clients are women (ICF-linked secondary summary). — [Tandem Coach](https://tandemcoach.co/coaching-industry-statistics/)
- 2014: awareness of coaching was highest at ages 25–34 (57%). — [ICF via PR Newswire, 2014](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-international-coach-federation-shows-link-between-age-and-awareness-of-coaching-260252421.html)

**Founders, executives and celebrities: what they ask**
- Business astrologers' clients ask when to start a business and about career direction from the Midheaven. They also ask about hiring and team fit, and about business timing using the company's filing date and time as its "birth". Some astrologers read the charts of job candidates. — [Dazed, Sep 2024](https://www.dazeddigital.com/life-culture/article/64660/1/inside-the-cold-capitalistic-world-of-business-astrology)
- Bollywood clients ask about release dates and muhurats and the timing of trailer drops. They also ask about signing films, name and title spellings, when to take a break, when to speak during a PR crisis, and the timing of social-media comebacks. — [The Hollywood Reporter India, Sep/Oct 2025](https://www.hollywoodreporterindia.com/amp/story/features/interviews/bollywood-celebrity-astrologers-how-big-decisions-in-cinema-are-made-after-a-call-to-the-stargazers)
- Indian companies "from startups to large conglomerates" consult astrologers and numerologists on business decisions. AstroSage AI's CEO says it happens "far more often than people imagine". — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-makers/from-media-planners-to-brand-ceos-astrology-secretly-claims-a-seat-in-the-boardroom-75488.htm)

### Inferences
Persona map. Each card combines the anchors above with general product reasoning; the ranked questions are not survey-ranked.

- **Gender**: Women ask more often overall and lead on relationship and marriage questions. Men use astrology less (Pew 18–20%), and anecdotal sources say their questions skew to career, money and business timing. The coaching data (58% women, but mostly managers and executives) suggests a framing like "decision support for work" may bring men in where "horoscope" does not.
- **People who do not state gender, and LGBT users**: This is the most engaged US subgroup (54% consult, 29% say insight, 21% rely at least a little). Copy and compatibility features must not assume heterosexual pairing or gendered roles.
- **Wealth**: In the US, belief falls with income (37% lower-income vs 16% upper-income). Use by the top tier is therefore concentrated and private rather than mass. In India the pattern differs: elite families openly use muhurta (see Q4).

### Gaps
- I found no survey that ranks question topics by age band, for either Western or Vedic users. The ranked lists above are inferences.
- I found no data on what men vs women ask on Western apps (Co-Star, The Pattern). The "men skew career and money" claim is anecdotal and not quantified.
- I found no data on 60+ usage of astrology apps or on non-English usage shares, beyond Vedic dominance in India (60% of India's app market, per Upstox).
- Pew's age and income breakdowns for *consulting* appear only in charts. The exact figures were not extracted.

## Q2. Market data: demographics, spend per user, revenue, premium drivers

### Takeaway
India's AstroTalk is the clearest real business: about ₹1,176 crore of FY25 operating revenue, built on paid live chats with astrologers (average order about ₹210) and led by marriage questions. Western apps are smaller and opaque. Co-Star has about 4.3M MAU (Sensor Tower) and was bought by Midjourney in July 2026. Total market estimates range from about $1B to $23B depending on definition, so none should be quoted as fact without its source.

### Cited Findings
- AstroTalk FY25: revenue from operations about ₹1,176 cr (FY24 about ₹651 cr) and total income ₹1,214 cr, up 85%. Engagement is up 27%, driven by repeat consultations in Tier-I cities. 20,000+ astrologers. Profit figures conflict: adjusted PBT ₹285 cr vs PAT about ₹33 cr after a one-time charge of about ₹120 cr. — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/start-up/news/astrotalk-reports-85-revenue-growth-in-fy25-as-tier-i-cities-boost-platform-activity); [CXO Digital Pulse](https://www.cxodigitalpulse.com/?p=43618)
- AstroTalk's average order is about ₹210 for consultations and about ₹900 for store orders. Its user base is 4 crore+. — [Inc42, Aug 2026](https://inc42.com/features/can-astrotalk-chart-a-future-beyond-astrology/)
- AstroTalk late 2024: 3 crore registered users, about 4.5M paid and 6M free sessions per month, about ₹70 cr monthly revenue, and an 80% share claimed by the company. Session prices run from ₹99 to ₹1,999. AstroSage: ₹60 cr annual revenue (FY24) and 10M+ installs. India's astrology app market: $163M (2024), projected $1.8B by 2030. Vedic holds 60% of it. — [Upstox, 2025](https://upstox.com/news/upstox-originals/investing/stars-on-demand-how-digital-astrology-is-booming-in-india/article-167792)
- Co-Star: acquired by Midjourney (terms undisclosed). About 4.3M MAU per Sensor Tower. — [Bloomberg Law, 24 Jul 2026](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/private-equity/ai-lab-midjourney-buys-astrology-startup-co-star-plans-new-apps)
- Co-Star: 20M+ downloads (company-reported). Third-party estimators conflict wildly (AppGoblin shows about 130K MAU), so they are unreliable. — [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.costarastrology); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/co-star-astrology-society)
- Allied Market Research: astrology market $12.8B (2021), projected $22.8B by 2031 at a 5.7% CAGR. — [PR Newswire / Allied](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/astrology-market-to-reach-22-8-billion-globally-by-2031-at-5-7-cagr-allied-market-research-301730436.html)
- MarkNtel: astrology app market about $3B (2024), projected $9B by 2030 (about 20% CAGR). — [MarkNtel](https://www.marknteladvisors.com/press-release/astrology-app-market-size)
- Other app-market estimates for 2025 range from $1.16B to $7.11B. Subscriptions are about 46% of app revenue (2025). Reports also disagree on whether North America or Asia-Pacific leads. — [Market Research Forecast](https://www.marketresearchforecast.com/reports/horoscope-and-astrology-apps-11905); [GII / 360iResearch](https://www.giiresearch.com/report/ires1952792-horoscope-astrology-apps-market-by-service-type.html)
- InstaAstro is betting $12M on AI reading of the customer. — [Open Magazine](https://openthemagazine.com/business/kundli-meets-code-instaastros-12-million-bet-on-ai-reading-the-customer)

### Inferences
- **Premium conversion driver in India**: a live human answering a specific, anxious, time-bound question (marriage, job), paid per minute. The business is built on repeat use, which suggests recurring worry rather than one-off curiosity. An AI assistant that is specific to the person, and confirms before it predicts, competes directly with this ₹210 chat.
- **Western driver**: daily identity content and social features (Co-Star) bring big reach but thin monetisation. Usage is "fun" for most (Pew 20% fun vs 10% insight).
- The money sits in decision moments (marriage, career, launches), not in daily horoscopes.

### Gaps
- No reliable revenue or subscriber data was found for The Pattern, Sanctuary or Nebula (OBRIO). Only app-directory estimates exist.
- Grand View Research's astrology figure was not found in search.
- No income data was found for app users. Pew's income data covers belief, not app spend.
- No published per-user spend for Western apps.

## Q3. Which segments are served well, and which are ignored

### Takeaway
Today's apps serve young women well: Western apps through identity and compatibility content, Indian apps through women 25–35 asking about marriage. Underserved groups visible in the data are men (especially 40+), executives and founders who want decision timing, the 60+ group, non-English speakers outside Hindi and English, and the wealthy. These underserved groups currently go to private human astrologers, coaches or numerologists.

### Cited Findings
- Men 50+ have the lowest belief (16%), and 65+ adults the lowest use. — [Pew, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/)
- Market reports describe growth as driven by Gen Z and Millennials, with no segment for older users or men. — [GII](https://www.giiresearch.com/report/ires1952792-horoscope-astrology-apps-market-by-service-type.html)
- Executives get decision support from coaching instead: 56% of coaching clients are managers or executives, and most are sponsored. — [ICF 2023](https://coachingfederation.org/app/uploads/2023/11/ExecutiveSummary_2023ICFFutureofCoachingSnapshot.pdf)
- Celebrities buy written reports and short calls from named human astrologers. Example: Sanjay B. Jumaani charges ₹12,300 for a written report and ₹23,100 for the report plus a 6–9 minute call, with a 2.5–3 week turnaround and no refunds. — [THR India, 2025](https://www.hollywoodreporterindia.com/amp/story/features/interviews/bollywood-celebrity-astrologers-how-big-decisions-in-cinema-are-made-after-a-call-to-the-stargazers)
- Business astrology for companies is still a niche run by individual practitioners. Corporate use for launches and rebrands is predicted, not established (2024). — [Dazed, 2024](https://www.dazeddigital.com/life-culture/article/64660/1/inside-the-cold-capitalistic-world-of-business-astrology)

### Inferences
- **Gap for men 35+ and executives**: they want decision-grade output (a window, a confidence level, a reason), not a mood horoscope. Framing it as "career and timing counsel" fits the coaching data better than "zodiac".
- **Gap for the 46–60 and 60+ groups**: family and multi-person charts (children's marriages, parents' health) and panchang or daily muhurta are served by Indian apps only through human chats, not through structured boards.
- **Gap for non-English users**: Vedic demand is regional-language heavy in India. Most apps are English or Hindi first (inference; no share data found).

### Gaps
- I did not pull or read app-store review corpora, so review-based complaint evidence is missing. A separate review scrape would close this.
- No founder interview was found that admits to ignoring men or older users.

## Q4. How the very wealthy use astrologers, and what a billionaire or family office needs that an app cannot give

### Takeaway
Elite use is long-standing and private. J.P. Morgan's link to Evangeline Adams (about 1900–1930s) and Joan Quigley timing Reagan's schedule (1981–88) are the classic cases. In India, muhurta for weddings and launches is open and routine; the Ambani wedding (July 2024) was dated by the family pandit. What they buy is timing, names, discretion and a named human advisor. Formats are written reports, short private calls and retained family priests or astrologers. No reliable fee data exists for the UHNW tier.

### Cited Findings
- J.P. Morgan had a lifelong interest in astrological prediction, as did three NYSE presidents of the era. — [University at Buffalo, Feb 2026](https://www.buffalo.edu/news/releases/2026/02/tirado-bramen-astrological-speculation.html)
- "Millionaires don't use astrology; billionaires do" is widely credited to Morgan, but the attribution is disputed. — [Llewellyn](https://www.llewellyn.com/encyclopedia/term/John+Pierpont+Morgan); [Deeper Weirder transcript](https://deeperweirder.transistor.fm/episodes/millionaires-don-t-use-astrology-billionaires-do/transcript)
- Evangeline Adams won a 1914 court case over her right to practise astrology. — [Encyclopedia.com](https://encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/adams-evangeline-smith-mrs-george-e-jordon-jr-1859-1933)
- Joan Quigley advised Nancy Reagan for about 7 years on the timing of presidential activities. It was revealed in 1988 by Donald Regan's memoir. The White House said she covered non-political decisions only. — [Wikipedia: Joan Quigley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Quigley)
- Anant Ambani–Radhika Merchant: the wedding muhurat was 12 July 2024, with the reception on 14 July. Pandit Chandrashekhar Sharma, the family's priest and astrologer, oversaw the rituals. Radhika Merchant said the dates were chosen on the family pujari's advice. — [Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/national/why-the-ambanis-chose-july-12-for-anant-ambani-radhika-merchant-wedding-a505); [DNA India](https://www.dnaindia.com/viral/report-meet-mukesh-ambani-nita-ambani-family-panditji-who-officiated-anant-radhika-wedding-his-fees-3116592); [Bollywood Hungama](https://test-stag.bollywoodhungama.com/news/features/radhika-merchant-opens-spiritual-significance-wedding-dates-says-dates-strategically-chosen-advice-family-pujari)
- Wall Street: in 2018 Bloomberg Businessweek profiled Henry Weingarten, who invests client money using astrology. A 2012 report had about 300 traders paying $237 a year for one financial-astrology newsletter. In 2001 an astrology firm claimed 10–15% of fund managers used such services (unverified). — [Bloomberg, 2018](https://brp-prod-bcc.bloomberg.com/news/features/2018-07-27/is-the-key-to-beating-the-market-written-in-the-stars); [Marketplace, 2012](https://marketplace.org/2012/09/19/astrology-guides-some-financial-traders); [Wikipedia: Financial astrology](https://en.Wikipedia.com/wiki/Financial_astrology)
- Bollywood astrologers stress confidentiality. Dr Sohini Sastri says she uses encrypted channels. Jumaani says some questions are so private "even my wife doesn't know." — [THR India, 2025](https://www.hollywoodreporterindia.com/amp/story/features/interviews/bollywood-celebrity-astrologers-how-big-decisions-in-cinema-are-made-after-a-call-to-the-stargazers)
- Name and spelling changes for stars and film titles (Ajay Devgn, *Simmba*, *Sooryavanshi*) are a visible output of numerology and astrology advisors. — [THR India, 2025](https://www.hollywoodreporterindia.com/amp/story/features/interviews/bollywood-celebrity-astrologers-how-big-decisions-in-cinema-are-made-after-a-call-to-the-stargazers)
- One Mumbai astrologer (Sundeep Kochar) analyses birth charts of prospective hires for companies. — [Dazed, 2024](https://www.dazeddigital.com/life-culture/article/64660/1/inside-the-cold-capitalistic-world-of-business-astrology)
- In Nepal, a top astrologer gives business and fiscal-timing readings to wealthy clients (2017). — [SCMP, 2017](https://scmp.com/magazines/style/travel-food/article/2130189/nepals-top-astrologer-can-uncannily-predict-your-fiscal)
- AstroTalk itself timed its store opening by muhurat (Oct 2026). — [Startupfeed](https://startupfeed.in/?p=12266)

### Inferences
What a billionaire or family office needs that a consumer app does not give today:
1. **Confidentiality as a feature**: no cloud-visible names, encrypted or sealed records, deniability, and no social or share surfaces. Elite clients value advisors whose clients are never named.
2. **Human in the loop**: a named, retained advisor who reviews the machine output and answers follow-ups. The machine output is a draft for the advisor, not the final word.
3. **Multi-person charts**: family members (children's marriages, heirs), partners, counterparties in a deal, board members and hires. The family-office version of the "You" board is really a "Family and circle" board.
4. **Event-timing calendar (muhurta)**: deal signing, IPO and listing, launches, weddings, property registration, travel, and court dates, ranked best to avoid, with a reason for each.
5. **A written annual outlook**: a year-ahead report per family member, plus quarterly updates. This mirrors the ₹12,300-style written report scaled up, and the Quigley-style calendar.
6. **Bespoke question types the apps lack**: succession, litigation and opponents, political or regulatory favour, health of the patriarch or matriarch, naming (companies, products, children), and property. Some of these, such as "enemies" and litigation, carry ethical and legal risk. They need careful wording and a "never claim certainty" stance.
7. **Format**: private call, written report, retained advisor. Price is not the barrier; trust and discretion are.

For the product's "You" board:
- **Build for the mass tier**: Today and You (identity), Love (partner and marriage timing), and Work (career window), with dates shown as windows.
- **High-earner and HNW add-ons**: a multi-year arc view, a family and circle board, a muhurta calendar, and an exportable written annual outlook.
- **UHNW**: not an app tier. It is a concierge service (human astrologer plus engine, private deployment). It should be scoped separately.

### Gaps
- I found no verifiable fee data for UHNW, family-office or Wall Street astrologers. Concierge pricing is unconfirmed, and the DNA India and Lokmat "fee" headlines did not show a figure in the fetched snippets.
- I found no FT, WSJ, Vanity Fair, Mint or Forbes India pieces on Adani or other named industrialists in this pass. Bejan Daruwalla's client list was not researched.
- I did not verify any claim of a hedge fund using Vedic astrology. Indian IPO "listing muhurta" evidence comes only from astrology vendors (for example Tempora and GaneshaSpeaks), not from company disclosures.
