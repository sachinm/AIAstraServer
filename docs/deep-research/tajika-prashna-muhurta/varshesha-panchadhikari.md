# Varshesha: the year lord from the five office-holders (varṣeśa, pañcādhikārin)

> The year lord (varṣeśa) is the one planet that "rules" the person's year. It is chosen from five office-holders
> (pañcādhikārin). Its strength and condition decide the overall quality of the year, and each of the seven planets
> has its own year-lord results (HR ch. 5 §11). Every Tajika school uses it. The candidate list is stable across texts.
> The tie-breaking order is disputed, so implementations differ.

## 2. When it applies

Every annual chart. The same method, with a sixth candidate added, gives the month lord (`masa-pravesha.md`).

## 3. Inputs required

Annual chart (lagna, all planets in D1 and the dignity vargas), day or night at the revolution, Muntha sign, natal
lagna sign, the pañcavargīya strength of each candidate (`harsha-bala-pancha-vargiya-bala.md`), and the Tajika aspects
to the varṣa lagna (`tajika-aspects-deeptamsha.md`).

## 4. Calculation

**Five candidates [TEXT] (Samarasiṃha; others agree):**

1. Lord of the natal lagna sign (janma-lagneśa).
2. Lord of the varṣa lagna (varṣa-lagneśa).
3. Lord of the Muntha sign (munthahā-pati).
4. Triplicity lord (trirāśi-pati) of the varṣa lagna, from the day or night table.
5. Day revolution: lord of the sign the Sun occupies in the annual chart. Night revolution: lord of the Moon's sign
   (dina-rātri-pati).

**Triplicity lords: two tables [TEXT]**

Table A (Tejaḥsiṃha / Maṇittha; Samarasiṃha calls it the Yavana view):

| Varṣa lagna | Ar | Ta | Ge | Cn | Le | Vi | Li | Sc | Sg | Cp | Aq | Pi |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Day | Su | Ve | Sa | Ve | Ju | Mo | Me | Ma | Sa | Ma | Ju | Mo |
| Night | Ju | Mo | Me | Ma | Su | Ve | Sa | Ve | Sa | Ma | Ju | Mo |

Table B (Samarasiṃha / Muktāvalī ṭippaṇī 1.12) gives the classical Dorothean triplicity lords by element, with a
third "constant" lord. Fire: day Su, night Ju, constant Sa. Earth: Ve / Mo / Ma. Air: Sa / Me / Ju. Water: Ve / Ma / Mo.
Choose the stronger of the day (or night) lord and the constant lord (ṭippaṇī 1.13).
The summariser marked the attribution of Table A to one source as inferred, so it is unverified at verse level.

**Selection [TEXT]:**

1. Keep the candidates that cast any Tajika aspect on the varṣa lagna (Muktāvalī ṭ. 4.3–4; Yādava 7.3–4).
2. Of these, the strongest (by pañcavargīya bala) is the year lord. A weak candidate that aspects beats a strong one that does not.
3. Ties: by greater aspect strength (Vāmana; Tājikakaustubha), or by the number of offices held (Yādava 7.4; Tājikasāra 99).
   The Kaustubha puts strength between aspect and offices. The Tājikatilaka puts offices before strength.
4. If no candidate aspects the lagna, sources differ: the varṣa-lagna lord (Muktāvalī ṭ. 4.9); the planet that aspected
   that sign in the nativity, else the Muntha lord (TBh 1.37, Gaṇeśa); the Muntha lord (Yādava); the varṣa-lagna lord
   (Tuka). TBh's commentary resolves this by taking the strongest of the three by ṣaḍbala, or the Muntha lord if they are equal.
   The Kaustubha's extreme view: no year lord, predict death.
5. [TEXT] Year-lord strength grades (Balabhadra): pañcavargīya ≤6 weak, >6 middling, >12 full.

## 5. Interpretation rules

- [TEXT] A strong year lord that is well placed and aspected by benefics gives a prosperous year in the matters of
  the houses it rules and occupies. A weak or afflicted one (combust, in 6/8/12, in hostile aspect with malefics)
  gives a difficult year (HR ch. 5 §10–11; per-planet results not fetched).
- [TEXT] Each planet as year lord has its own signification (Sun: authority, government; Moon: mind, public, travel; etc.,
  HR ch. 5 §11.1–11.7, not fetched; wording to be taken from there).
- [MODERN] Many modern readers use the year lord as the "theme planet" of the year and cross-check it with the natal
  dasha lord. If the two are the same or friendly, the year delivers the dasha's promise.

## 6. Variants and disputes

| Point | Positions |
|---|---|
| Triplicity table | A (Yavana / Tejaḥsiṃha) vs. B (Samarasiṃha, with a constant lord) |
| Aspect requirement | All the texts quoted require an aspect on the lagna. Strength alone never qualifies |
| Ties | Aspect → strength → offices (Kaustubha) vs. aspect → offices → strength (Tilaka) |
| No aspecting candidate | Four different answers (above) |
| Day/night of what | The revolution moment (the texts speak of a day or night revolution) |

## 7. Worked micro-example

Night revolution with varṣa lagna Capricorn: candidates are Saturn (varṣa-lagna lord); natal lagna Cancer, so the
Moon; Muntha Taurus, so Venus; triplicity (Table A, night, Capricorn) Mars; the Moon in Leo (night), so the Sun. Which
of them aspect Capricorn? Say only Venus (from Virgo, the 9th: trine, friendly) and Mars (from Aries, the 4th:
hostile). Both aspect, so the stronger by pañcavargīya wins. If Venus has 11.2 and Mars 7.5, Venus is year lord with
middling strength.

## 8. Design notes for AI Astra

- Engine (served): `annual.tajaka.year_lord` from `tajaka.lord_of_the_year`. Inspected 2026-11-18
  (`AIAstra_Radha/src/jhora/horoscope/transit/tajaka.py` lines 449–533):
  - Candidates: matches the five (Sun/Moon-sign lord, natal-lagna lord, Muntha lord, varṣa-lagna lord, triplicity
    lord via `const.tri_rasi_day/nighttime_lords`, which is **Table A**). Rahu or Ketu as sign lord is mapped to Saturn or Mars.
  - **Selection differs from the texts.** (a) It first keeps only candidates with a *benefic* (friendly) aspect on
    the lagna and returns one if only one remains. Only when none has a benefic aspect does it look at malefic
    aspects. The texts admit any aspect and then choose by strength. (b) When several qualify, it takes the
    pañcavargīya maximum over **all** candidates (`pvbc = [pvb[c] for c in candidates]`, line 492), not over the
    shortlist. So it can choose a planet that does not aspect the lagna, which every text forbids. (c) If that maximum
    is ≤10 it returns candidate 1 (the Sun/Moon-sign lord), which no quoted text does. (d) Its instant is the mean year
    (see `varsha-pravesha.md`).
  - **Engine addition (fix):** select by aspect → strength among the aspecting candidates → tie rule (configurable:
    Kaustubha or Tilaka) → no-aspect fallback (configurable, default Muktāvalī: varṣa-lagna lord). Output the full
    working: `candidates[{planet, offices[], aspects_lagna, aspect_kind, aspect_strength, pvb}]`, `rule_path`, and
    `strength_grade`. Then a reader can show "why this planet".
- Serves: the annual reader, the Self board year card, and `general.annual` (which already asks for the year lord).
  `general.annual.md` notes the TP year lord (vāreśa) is a different, non-Tajika thing. Keep both, labelled.
- Weight: the most important single Tajika factor for the quality of the year. It is still subordinate to natal dasha.
