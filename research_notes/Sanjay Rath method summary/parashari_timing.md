# Sanjay Rath: Parashari timing methods (source-cited notes)

Retrieved 2026-11-17 by web search and page fetch: about 17 research calls, including one verification fetch and one failed PDF read.

**Label key**
- **Rath's own text**: a page on srath.com (or a page carrying Rath's byline) that I fetched. Fetched content was read through a summarizing model, so statements below are paraphrases unless a short phrase is in quotation marks, and none was checked against the page source.
- **Secondary account**: anything else, including bookseller listings, Scribd uploads, blogs and co-author papers. Items marked "search summary" or "search snippet" were seen only in search results and were not opened. Items with no URL that I could pin are listed under Gaps.

**Dates**: "November 18, 2026" appears in the site header of every srath.com page I fetched. It is not a publication date.

**Conflicts**
1. Tithi Pravesha basis: resolved from Rath's own page. The page defines the annual moment as the Sun transiting the natal sign, with the Moon at the birth angle from the Sun. An earlier, truncated excerpt had read "solar return" as describing Tithi Pravesha. The full page uses "solar return" only for a contrasting Tajika method. The co-author account and secondary explainers agree on the Sun-Moon basis.
2. Kalachakra vs Kaala: one secondary source calls Kalachakra sign-based. A co-author's Scribd piece calls "Kaala dasa" a planet-based 120-year system distinct from the sign-based Chakra dasa (both in Gaps; no URL pinned). The sources do not agree that these are one system.
3. Ashtottari applicability: three secondary sources (a Parashara summary, Mansagari, and a Yogini source citing Rudrayamal) give different conditions, and the Yogini source reverses the others (see Q1 Gaps and Q4).
4. Narayana and Ashtakavarga: a Scribd excerpt says Rath advises an Ashtakavarga cross-check (see Gaps; no URL pinned). Rath's own Narayana page shows only a heading for Ashtakavarga.
5. Narayana book date: Rath's page gives 1998. Search summaries report other dates (see Gaps).

## Which dasha systems does Rath explicitly recommend for timing life events, and in what priority?

### Takeaway
No retrieved text by Rath ranks dasha systems for timing life events. Each srath.com page describes one system with its own applicability rule and stated purpose. The explicit timing uses found are: Vimshottari-based lines for longevity and death-related windows, Mula dasa for curses and karmic effects, and Shula for physical-body events. Narayana, Kalachakra, Yogini, Chaturaseeti Sama and Tithi Ashtottari have no retrieved Rath rule text.

### Cited Findings

#### Vimshottari (120-year), with Rath's longevity lines
- The "Vimshottari Dasha #2" article builds Moon-based Vimshottari lines from the 5th, 4th and 8th nakshatras counted from the birth nakshatra (named Utpanna, Kshema and Adhana). It compares their end dates with the Janma Vimshottari end dates, and states the lines are always calculated from the Moon's longitude. [Rath's own text: Vimshottari Dasha #2](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/dasa/vimshottari-dasha-2/)
- Trigger in that article: close or coinciding end dates, in particular the closing portion (sesham) of a period coinciding across lines. Coincidence in two or more lines is described as the stronger signal. One worked example narrows a window by taking the shorter period. The article's stated subject is illness, accidents and death, not general life events. [Rath's own text: Vimshottari Dasha #2](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/dasa/vimshottari-dasha-2/)
- Further death-related triggers in the article: lords of the 2nd, 6th and 8th houses, a "Rudra" lord (the stronger of the 2nd or 8th lord), the longitude of Gulika, the last degree of a sign (rasi-sandhi), and the first, third, fifth and seventh dasas in the sequence. The article ties these to infant, teenage, middle and full life respectively. [Rath's own text: Vimshottari Dasha #2](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/dasa/vimshottari-dasha-2/)
- The page is marked as a continuation. The base Vimshottari rules are in a Part 1 that I did not retrieve. [Rath's own text: Vimshottari Dasha #2](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/dasa/vimshottari-dasha-2/)

#### Shula dasa (longevity-type)
- Rath's dasa category excerpt says Shula can be used to time events of the physical body, and that the Moon is stressed for health. The full article was not opened. [Rath's own text: srath.com dasa category excerpt](https://srath.com/category/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/dasa)

#### Mula dasa (Moola dasa)
- The article is credited on the page as "(c) Sanjay Rath (1999)". [Rath's own text: Mula Dasa](https://srath.com/?p=6254)
- Start: the Lagna or another kendra containing planets, whichever is stronger. The sequence then moves to the other kendras with planets, followed by planets in panapara and apoklima signs. An odd Lagna makes panapara follow the kendras, and an even Lagna makes apoklima follow. Saturn in the Lagna makes panapara follow irrespective of odd or even, and Ketu in the Lagna makes apoklima follow. [Rath's own text: Mula Dasa](https://srath.com/?p=6254)
- Period per planet: count signs from the planet's sign to its mulatrikona sign, subtract one, and deduct the result from the planet's Vimshottari years. Add one year if the planet is exalted and subtract one if debilitated. Rahu and Ketu are reckoned to Aquarius and Scorpio, as in Narayana dasa. A second cycle takes the balance up to 120 years. [Rath's own text: Mula Dasa](https://srath.com/?p=6254)
- Stated use: timing curses, hardships and other adverse events, the period in which a curse or blessing comes into play, and links to past-life karma. [Rath's own text: Mula Dasa](https://srath.com/?p=6254)

#### Dvisaptati Sama dasa (72-year cycle, 144 years over two cycles)
- Applicability: the lagna lord is in the 7th house, or the 7th lord is in lagna, or both. [Rath's own text: Dvisaptati Sama Dasa](https://srath.com/?p=75)
- Eight planets, Sun to Rahu, in weekday order, nine years each: 72 years per cycle, 144 years over two cycles. [Rath's own text: Dvisaptati Sama Dasa](https://srath.com/?p=75)
- Start: count from Moola nakshatra (19) to the birth nakshatra, remove multiples of eight, and count the remainder from the Sun. The balance at birth comes from the Moon's longitude in the birth nakshatra. [Rath's own text: Dvisaptati Sama Dasa](https://srath.com/?p=75)
- Dictum in the article: dasas tend to produce adverse results toward their closing sub-period. [Rath's own text: Dvisaptati Sama Dasa](https://srath.com/?p=75)

#### Narayana dasa (Rath's book; 1998 per the page)
- Starts from the stronger of the Lagna or the 7th house, which the page calls the "seat of truth". The page says the rules for deciding strength are culled from BPHS and the Jaimini Sutras, and notes that the sequence of those rules differs among sources. [Rath's own text: Narayana Dasha](https://srath.com/misc/naraya%e1%b9%87a-dasa/)
- The page's contents list includes order of dasa, period of dasa, effect of house, dasa rasi as lagna, effect of dasa pravesh chakra, timing marriage, effect of transit, and ashtakavarga, plus varga and altered variants. Rule text under these headings was not retrieved. [Rath's own text: Narayana Dasha](https://srath.com/misc/naraya%e1%b9%87a-dasa/)

#### Kaala dasa (as named in the request)

#### Kalachakra (a related name; listed because it is the nearest Rath material)
- Rath has a presentation titled "Kälacakra daśä" (a Scribd upload, attributed to Rath in its title; not opened). [Scribd presentation](https://www.scribd.com/presentation/620260194/KCD-SanjayRath)
- A book titled "Crux of Vedic Astrology: Timing of Events" is described in a search summary as Rath's, covering Kalachakra calculation and disputed motion rules. Only the title was seen. [issuu listing](https://issuu.com/abhimeranaam/docs/crux-of-vedic-astrology-timing-of-e)
- Secondary (search summary; not opened): Kalachakra is sign-based. The order depends on the Moon's nakshatra-pada, and sign periods come from planetary year values (Sun 5, Moon 21, Mars 7, Mercury 9, Jupiter 10, Venus 16, Saturn 4) applied through each sign's lord. A savya or apasavya birth changes which signs mark the body and life. [Satyori: Kalachakra Dasha](https://satyori.com/jyotish/dasha/kalachakra/)

## How does he use annual charts (varsha, Tithi Pravesha) to confirm or refine dasha timing?

### Takeaway
Rath's own srath.com page defines the Tithi Pravesha annual moment as the Sun transiting the natal sign, with the Moon at the birth angle from the Sun. On a separate srath.com page, he applies dasha sub-periods to events inside an annual chart. No retrieved text states a rule for how the annual chart confirms or refines a dasha, or how the annual chart is read against the natal chart beyond using the birth angle as a timing match.

### Cited Findings
- The Tithi Pravesha page is credited by byline to Sanjay Rath and P.V.R. Narasimha Rao. It defines the annual birth moment as the moment the Sun transits its natal sign, with the Moon at the same displacement angle from the Sun as at birth. The chart is cast for the birthplace's coordinates, regardless of current residence. The page says the chart throws light on events of the coming year. The natal comparison is used for timing: the chart is cast when the Sun-to-Moon angle matches the natal chart. The page names no dasha running within the year. [Rath's own text: Tithi Pravesha](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/tithi-pravesa/tithi-pravesha/)
- The page uses "solar return" only in a description of a Tajika annual method, in contrast to Tithi Pravesha. [Rath's own text: Tithi Pravesha](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/tithi-pravesa/tithi-pravesha/)
- The srath.com India Tithi Pravesh 2008 page applies dasha periods to India's 2008 events, including a Ketu antardasha in Venus dasha, then Moon and Rahu pratyantara dashas. One interpretive sentence points to the Moon's pratyantara as the third lord of the chart. The excerpt does not say how the annual chart was cast, and no byline is visible. [Rath's own text, byline not shown: India Tithi Pravesh 2008](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/mundane/india-tithi-pravesh-2008/)
- Secondary (Scribd "Dasa Pravesh Chakra" upload; not opened; attributed to Rath only in a search summary): a new rashi-dasa period starts when the Sun reaches its natal longitude. Authorship not verified. [Scribd: Dasa Pravesh Chakra](https://www.scribd.com/document/448743437/Dasa-Pravesh-Chakra)
- Secondary (Sanskrit Magazine; search summary): the Tithi Pravesh chakra was kept within a tradition until Rath made it known. [Sanskrit Magazine](https://www.sanskritimagazine.com/principles-of-tithi-pravesh-chakra-in-vedic-astrology/)

### Inferences
- Rath's published example places dasha periods inside an annual chart. This suggests the annual chart is used as a frame and the dasha as the finer pointer. Based on one example only.
- Rath's own page and the co-author account (search summary) both describe a Sun-Moon angle basis, so the earlier solar-return reading of Tithi Pravesha is not supported. The solar-return method is a separate, contrasting method on the same page.

### Gaps
- No Rath rule found for confirming or refining dasha timing by an annual chart.
- The Tithi Pravesha page does not state how the annual chart is read against the natal chart beyond the angle match, and names no dasha running within the year.
- Secondary, no URL pinned: a general explainer (search summary) says Tithi Pravesha tracks the return of the Sun-Moon relationship, the same tithi in the same solar month, and not the Sun's return as in Varshaphal.
- Secondary, no URL pinned: Narasimha Rao's 2014 account (search summary; not opened) restates the chart as cast when the Moon returns to the birth Sun-Moon angle within the same soli-lunar month.
- Secondary, no URL pinned: a Scribd upload says the material appeared in Jyotish Digest, May 2002, under Rao and Rath. Not opened.
- Varshaphal (solar annual chart): no Rath text found. Tajaka material appeared in search results (vedicastrologer.org pp_tajaka.pdf) and was not opened.
- The vedicastrologer.org PDF (pp_tp.pdf, Narasimha Rao) could not be read. The fetch returned binary PDF content, and a local read failed because pdftoppm is not installed. Its byline, any mention of Tithi Ashtottari, and any event-timing rule are unverified.
- No Rath text retrieved on transit timing. The Narayana contents list "effect of transit" as a heading only.

## What rules does he publish for judging whether a period activates a house or significator?

### Takeaway
The retrieved Rath texts give applicability conditions for choosing a dasa and a stated purpose for each system. They do not give a rule for when a running period activates a house or significator. The rule-like statements about a period's effect concern closing sub-periods and coinciding endings, framed around harm and endings. No primary activation rule was found.

### Cited Findings
- Dvisaptati Sama: applicability via the lagna lord in the 7th house, the 7th lord in lagna, or both. Dictum: dasas tend to produce adverse results toward their closing sub-period. [Rath's own text: Dvisaptati Sama Dasa](https://srath.com/?p=75)
- Vimshottari Dasha #2: coinciding closing portions (sesham) across lines flag illness, accidents or death, and a shorter period narrows the window. The article does not test houses or significators in these rules. [Rath's own text: Vimshottari Dasha #2](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/dasa/vimshottari-dasha-2/)
- Mula dasa: sign-strength rules order the dasa sequence. Planets in a sign make it stronger than an empty sign. Exaltation, mulatrikona and own sign are considered. Dual signs are stronger than fixed, and fixed stronger than movable. A planet with a higher degree strengthens its sign. Sources are cited to Jaimini's Upadesa Sutra. These rules order the sequence and are not house-activation rules. [Rath's own text: Mula Dasa](https://srath.com/?p=6254)
- Mula dasa: the stated effect is that the period is when a curse or blessing comes into play. The page gives no house or significator test. [Rath's own text: Mula Dasa](https://srath.com/?p=6254)
- Narayana: "effect of house" is a contents heading only. Its text was not retrieved. [Rath's own text: Narayana Dasha](https://srath.com/misc/naraya%e1%b9%87a-dasa/)
- Shula: states a scope (events of the physical body) and gives no activation rule. [Rath's own text: srath.com dasa category excerpt](https://srath.com/category/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/dasa)

### Inferences
- In the retrieved texts, activation rests on a dasa's structure (its start, sequence, closing portion and coinciding ends) rather than on house-by-house activation tables. This is an inference from the pages retrieved.

### Gaps
- No primary Rath rule found for whether a running period activates a house, lord, karaka or significator. The most likely places are the Narayana rule text, Vimshottari Part 1, and the Kalachakra motion rules. None were retrieved.
- Secondary, no URL pinned: a Scribd snippet says a new rashi-dasa period begins when the Sun reaches its natal longitude. Attributed in a search summary to Rath's Dasa Pravesh Chakra notes. See the Dasa Pravesh Chakra link under the second key question.

## Which of these are his own method vs tradition he cites?

### Takeaway
Rath's own pages cite classical sources in a few places. Narayana rules are said to be culled from BPHS and the Jaimini Sutras. Mula dasa cites Jaimini's Upadesa Sutra for sign strength. The Tithi Pravesha page places a Tajika annual method, which it contrasts with Tithi Pravesha. The Dvisaptati Sama and Vimshottari Dasha #2 pages name no source in the retrieved text. No retrieved text establishes which parts are Rath's own construction.

### Cited Findings
- Narayana: the rules for the stronger sign are said to be culled from BPHS and the Jaimini Sutras. The page notes that the sequence of those rules differs among sources. [Rath's own text: Narayana Dasha](https://srath.com/misc/naraya%e1%b9%87a-dasa/)
- Mula dasa: sign-strength sources are cited to Jaimini's Upadesa Sutra. Rahu and Ketu are reckoned as in Narayana dasa, a cross-reference between two of Rath's own systems. [Rath's own text: Mula Dasa](https://srath.com/?p=6254)
- Tithi Pravesha: the first fetch of the page read the method as Tajika in origin, with a possible borrowing from Yavana or Tajika contacts. The second fetch found that the page describes a solar-return Tajika method as the contrast, and did not confirm that Tithi Pravesha is itself presented as Tajika. Unresolved. [Rath's own text: Tithi Pravesha](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/tithi-pravesa/tithi-pravesha/)
- Dvisaptati Sama and Vimshottari Dasha #2: no named tradition appears in the retrieved text. [Rath's own text: Dvisaptati Sama Dasa](https://srath.com/?p=75); [Rath's own text: Vimshottari Dasha #2](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/dasa/vimshottari-dasha-2/)
- Secondary (Sanskrit Magazine; search summary): the Tithi Pravesh chakra was kept within a tradition until Rath made it known. [Sanskrit Magazine](https://www.sanskritimagazine.com/principles-of-tithi-pravesh-chakra-in-vedic-astrology/)
- Secondary (Yogini source; search snippet): Yogini and Ashtottari conditions cite classical texts (Rudrayamal, Mansagari and Parashara summaries), and the sources disagree. See conflict 3 above. [Vedicastro: Yogini Dasha](https://vedicastro.com/yogini1.aspx)

### Inferences
- Rath's pages cite classical texts for specific rules (Narayana, Mula), but not for the period lengths in Dvisaptati Sama or the lines in Vimshottari Dasha #2. Dvisaptati Sama is the clearest case in the retrieved set of rules stated without a cited source.

### Gaps
- Whether Dvisaptati Sama and Vimshottari Dasha #2 derive from a named tradition is unknown from the retrieved text. Vimshottari Part 1 was not retrieved.
- No Rath text attributes Yogini, Chaturaseeti Sama or Tithi Ashtottari to him. None is claimed here.
- Whether Tithi Pravesha is itself a Tajika method is unresolved (see the Tithi Pravesha bullet above).
