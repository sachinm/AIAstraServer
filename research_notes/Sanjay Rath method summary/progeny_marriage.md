## 1. Children: rules for number and timing, and the use of D7

### Takeaway
Rath's book gives a rule-based children framework. The fifth house and Jupiter are the significators. The fifth lord and other planets are read from Jupiter for the number and nature of children. The Saptamsa (D7) decides pregnancy: the first child comes from the D7 fifth house, later pregnancies every third house, each child's sex is read from the lord of the pregnancy house, and birth timing uses the dasa and antardasa linked to the D7 lagna lord. The Scribd note's rules are mostly consistent with this text. Two of its rules (a navamsa-gain count and a shared-sign survival rule) were not found in the text read.

VIII sect. 8.1, p.166](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)
- **Rath's own text:** For matters of children, the fifth lord and planets are read from Jupiter. Planets strong from Jupiter indicate the number and nature of children, and the fifth lord's position from Jupiter indicates the extent of gains from children. Saturn brings loss or deprivation of children. Mars can cause loss of children and domestic disharmony. [Crux, Ch. - **Rath's own text:** Jupiter in the fifth: five sons, of whom three survive. In Pisces, few children. In Sagittarius, children with difficulty. In Cancer or Aquarius, children are denied, on the principle that a natural significator placed in its own house damages its significations. [Crux, sect. 8.5.1(e), p.171](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)

- **Rath's own text:** The D7 "holds the key" to pregnancy, and the final result is pronounced only after the D7 chart is examined. Where the fifth lord is exalted and Jupiter is strong in a quadrant, children may be denied. A remedial method using a "Santana Tithi" is given. [Crux, sect. 8.5.2, p.172](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)

If the fifth is also conjoined or aspected by Saturn and Mandi (a sub-planet), the text reads the adoption as due to denial of the native's own children. If the upapada or seventh house or lord associates with Rahu, a child may arise from a marriage to a widow who had children from an earlier marriage. - **Rath's own text:** Denial of children may come from curses of past births, listed in the classical Brihat Parasara Hora Shastra with remedies. The book gives conditions for each curse type, such as Rahu in Pisces or Sagittarius, Jupiter afflicted by Mars and Saturn, or the ninth lord in the eighth. Infertility is indicated if the Sun falls in the 2nd, 6th, 8th or 12th from these padas, or if Venus and Mercury together conjoin them. If either of the two pada sets (the Sun-based and the lagna-based) is undamaged, childbirth is delayed rather than denied. Later pregnancies are read every third house. For an odd D7 lagna, pregnancies fall in the 5th, 7th, 9th and 11th. For an even lagna, they fall in the 9th, 7th, 5th and 3rd. [Crux, sect. 8.6.1, p.175](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)
- **Rath's own text (sex of child):** The sex of each child is read from the lord of the sign ruling the pregnancy house. If that lord is exalted or conjoins male planets (Sun, Rahu, Jupiter, Mars), the child is male. If it is debilitated or conjoins female planets (Moon, Ketu, Venus), the child is female. Saturn and Mercury are called eunuch planets, and the author says he has found Saturn to indicate a female child and Mercury a male. If neither applies, the sign's nature decides. The child's lord in the seventh from the D7 lagna means the child opposes the parent. In a quadrant, parent and child cooperate. A benefic in the 3rd or 6th may bring defeat or loss to the parent. In the 12th, the child lives abroad. The marriages of children are read from the upapada of the child's lord. The same method applies to the drekkana lagna for co-borns. [Crux, sect. 8.6.1, p.176](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)
- **Rath's own text:** The controlling house for children is the fifth in male charts and the ninth in female charts. The sixth (male) or tenth (female) is the killer house. If a child's lord is in those houses, or conjoins their lords, the pregnancy may be lost or the child may die. [Crux, sect. 8.6.1, pp.176-177](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)
- **Rath's own text (timing of births):** The D7 lagna lord is key to all births. The Vimshottari dasa planet must give benefic argala to the D7 lagna lord, or obstruct papa argala, for a birth to occur in its period. The antardasa planet should relate to the child's lord or sit in a trine, 7th or 12th from it. A pratyantara planet in a trine to the D7 lagna contributes the rising sign at birth. For a double check, the book suggests independent Narayana dasas from the sign of the seventh lord (rasi chart) placed in D7. [Crux, sect. 8.6.2, pp.177-178](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)

The example then describes one issue surviving under a Venus and Ketu condition. This is worked-example reasoning, not a general survival rule. [Crux, worked example in Ch. ### Inferences
- In the sections read, Rath's children rules are categorical (children are denied under stated conditions; the barrenness rule is called overriding). These are testable claims. No independent test of them was found.
- The Scribd note appears to summarise this book. Its page citation matches the book's page 169, so that attribution is well supported. Its navamsa count and shared-sign survival rule should be treated as unverified until checked against the rest of the book.
- Birth timing in this book is dasa-based, with D7 and argala checks. The sections read do not use navamsa gains for timing.
- The site's D7 chart readings of public figures use the same sign and count logic as sect. 8.6.1. They are illustrations, not tests.

## 2. Marriage: timing and partner characteristics

### Takeaway
Rath's book sets out marriage rules in several chapters, not one. They use the upapada, the seventh house and its lord, the eighth from the upapada in a conditional second-marriage case, Jupiter and Saturn progressions for timing, and a Vivaha saham formula. His article on the Darapada and Upapada (fetched) gives generic placement rules for Venus, Ketu and Rahu in the upapada and the A7, with hedged wording and a caution that the upapada and seventh house alone cannot settle marriage. The seventh-house chapter of the book is not in the extract, so the book's full seventh-house and D9 rules for partner traits remain unread.

### Cited Findings
- **Rath's own text (timing method, Ch. I):** Auspicious events such as marriage are timed by progressing from Jupiter's sign or its seventh house, at one year per sign. Inauspicious events are timed with Saturn at two and a half years per sign. A worked example times a marriage by Saturn progressions over natal Venus and cites the Sudarshan Chakra for the year. [Crux, Ch. I timing passage, page not fixed](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)
- **Rath's own text (Vivaha saham, Ch. I table):** The marriage saham formula is Venus minus Saturn plus ascendant, with a reversed form beside it. [Crux, Ch. I saham table, p.14](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)
- **Rath's own text (Ch. II, p.37):** In a worked example on co-borns, Jupiter is named the spouse significator for a female chart. [Crux, Ch. II, p.37](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)
- **Rath's own text (Ch. II, p.37, inside one chart example):** The eighth house and its lord are described as the second (maraka) from the seventh, the house of the spouse. The example treats affliction to them as indicating accidents or dangers to the spouse, and says to confirm by placing both planets in dusthanas from the Navamsa lagna. This is reasoning inside a single chart example, not a general rule. [Crux, Ch. II, p.37](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)
- **Rath's own text (Ch. V, p.79):** The second house from the upapada, or from the seventh, is read for the sustenance of marriage. The passage gives Jupiter as the natural significator of "this house". The reference is ambiguous in the scan, so the Jupiter link is not assigned to the upapada-second house alone. [Crux, Ch. V, p.79](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)
- **Rath's own text (Ch. V sect. 5.3.4, page not fixed):** Wealth comes from activities whose arudha padas fall in the second house. The example given is the upapada in the second house, which the text says brings wealth from the spouse and marriage into a wealthy family. [Crux, Ch. V sect. 5.3.4](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)
- **Rath's own text (Ch. VI, p.111):** An argala of the third lord on the seventh gives benefit from the spouse. [Crux, Ch. VI, p.111](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)
- **Rath's own text (Ch. VI sect. 6.5, p.112):** The seventh lord in the third indicates a spouse of doubtful character. The eighth lord in the third indicates more than one attachment, because the eighth house rules the breaking of marriage. [Crux, Ch. VI sect. 6.5, p.112](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)
- **Rath's own text (Ch. VII, p.139):** Details about the spouse are examined from the upapada. Venus is the sthira karaka for wife and in-laws, per Jaimini. Spouse-related shoola dasas begin from the first or seventh house, whichever is stronger, and run nine years each. [Crux, Ch. VII, p.139](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)
- **Rath's own text (Ch. VII, p.143, conditional):** Where a strong earlier emotional bond exists, the eighth from the upapada is treated as the upapada for a second marriage. The author says he reads marriage using a criterion from the Mahabharata (a year of mental and physical union), and the rule is illustrated by a pair of charts. [Crux, Ch. VII, p.143](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)
- **Rath's own text (Ch. VIII sect. 8.1, p.166):** Love and affection are read from Venus and the Navamsa (D9). The chapter-level list of divisional charts in Ch. II also names the spouse with D9. [Crux, Ch. VIII sect. 8.1, p.166](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)
- **Rath's own text (worked example, pp.183-184):** A worked example describes a delayed marriage. Earlier sub-periods did not produce it despite eligible candidates, and it came in a Moon-Venus sub-period, with Venus in the upapada and linked to a Sudarshan Chakra sign. The example is retrospective. [Crux, worked example, pp.183-184](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)
- **Rath's own text (srath.com article "Darapada and Upapada", fetched; condensed by a small model):** Venus signifies marriage and the Darapada (A7), and Ketu signifies renunciation. Venus in the 12th from lagna or Arudha lagna may mean losing a spouse. Ketu in the 12th may mean delayed marriage or denial of marital bliss. Venus in the 6th from AL may mean no marriage or separation. Ketu in the 7th or A7, with Venus in the 12th or UL, may mean two spouses. Venus in the 7th or A7, with Ketu or Rahu in the 12th or UL, may mean losing a spouse, remarrying, or renouncing. Renunciation is said to be promised only if Venus is in a dusthana from AL, the article's firmest wording. Harmony between the 7th and 12th houses and their arudhas, especially from karakamsa, is said to benefit the native and can support spiritual growth through a spouse. The A7 is said to govern sexual desire and whether it is fulfilled or renounced. [srath.com, Darapada and Upapada](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/scholar/darapada-and-upapada/)
- **Rath's own text (his website, search snippet only, not fetched):** A chart reading of a public figure identifies Mars as the chara darakaraka and ties a wedding to a Mars dasa with Jupiter antardasa. The example is retrospective. [srath.com, Franklin Roosevelt reading](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/dasa/franklin-delano-roosevelt/)
- **Secondary account (publisher listing; search snippet only, not fetched):** *Timing of Marriage with Tested Techniques* is described as combining classical Parashari and Jaimini material with Rath's Tithi Pravesha method and K.N. Rao's techniques. The retrieved listing does not confirm the author. [Motilal Banarsidass listing](https://www.motilalbanarsidass.com/products/timing-of-marriage-with-tested-techniques)
- **Secondary account (listing contents; search snippet only, not fetched):** The listed contents include Jaimini Sutras marriage timing, the Vivaha Sahama, Tithi Pravesha, and transiting planets with the Darakaraka. [Exotic India listing](https://www.exoticindia.com/book/details/timing-of-marriage-with-tested-techniques-NAO509/)
- **General, not Rath:** A Vedicfeed article says the planet with the lowest degree is the Darakaraka, and its period often brings marriage. The article does not attribute this to Rath. [Vedicfeed, "When Will I Get Married?"](https://vedicfeed.com/when-will-i-get-married/)
- **Rath's own text (website, not on marriage):** The site lists four classical works as foundation texts and lists the Jaimini Upadesa Sutras among books attributed to him. It does not discuss marriage. [srath.com, about page](https://srath.com/series/about)

### Inferences
- Rath's marriage rules are placement rules for the upapada, seventh and eighth, plus timing by progression and dasa. The book's illustrative marriage examples are retrospective, so they show the method fitting known events, not how often it works.
- The Darapada article's chart examples (drawn from classical and modern religious figures) are presented as illustrations. The article gives no birth data and no validation, and reports no failed case. Its hedging ("can", "may", "shows") is the most explicit limit found on marriage claims.
- The Darakaraka appears in the book as the chara darakaraka (DK) inside chart examples. The rule that selects it is not stated in the sections read, so the Vedicfeed "lowest degree" convention cannot be attributed to Rath.
- The Motilal listing's marriage book appears to be a separate volume. The book read here covers marriage only in passages across chapters.

### Gaps
- Seventh-house chapter: not in the extract. The extract ends at about printed p.249, and the chapter starts at about p.251. The book's own seventh-house rules are therefore unread.
- D9 rules for partner characteristics, beyond the divisional list and the love-and-affection passage: not read in full.
- The rule for selecting the Darakaraka, and the derivation of the upapada: not stated in the sections read.
- A search summary described a chart reading in which Rahu, as Darakaraka, denies marriage. The fetched article does not contain it, and the page was not identified. It is not cited.
- Ch. V and Ch. VII marriage passages: page numbers not fixed for some points.
- *Timing of Marriage with Tested Techniques* (listing above): not read.
- Scribd upload "Navamsa Sanjay Rath" (https://www.scribd.com/document/356674100/Navamsa-Sanjay-Rath): listed in search, not fetched. Authorship is unverified, and it is a lead for D9 material only.

## 3. Disclaimers and stated limits on prediction

### Takeaway
Rath's text states several limits. They concern the method's completeness, the scope of remedies, medical referral, and the need for a D7 check. None of them states a probability or a confidence level for a prediction. His site's disclaimer (fetched) says astrology is not a substitute for licensed professional advice, that interpretations can differ between astrologers, and that remedies carry no guarantee of accuracy, reliability or results. It describes no verification of predictions, and it refers birth data only to a separate privacy policy.

### Cited Findings
- **Rath's own text (preface):** The author says explanations may at places be inadequate, and invites constructive criticism from readers. [Crux, Preface](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)
- **Rath's own text (Introduction, p.1):** Overlooking the riders of Vimshottari dasa is said to produce incorrect predictions and remedies that are off target. [Crux, Ch. I, p.1](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)
- **Rath's own text (sect. 8.2, p.168):** The full range of remedial measures for the fifth house is said to lie beyond the scope of the book. [Crux, sect. 8.2, p.168](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)
- **Rath's own text (sect. 8.4, pp.169-170):** Cautions that many astrologers prescribe mantras too freely, and that a poorly matched propitiation could endanger children. Sets rules for choosing a mantra. [Crux, sect. 8.4, pp.169-170](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)
- **Rath's own text (sect. 8.5.2, p.172):** The final result for a children question is pronounced only after the D7 chart is examined. [Crux, sect. 8.5.2, p.172](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)
