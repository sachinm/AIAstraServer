Where a point is already there, this note gives a one-line cross-reference.

**Tier key**
- **(A) Rath's own text**: a srath.com page or Rath book whose byline names Rath. Co-credited pages are marked as such.
- **(B) Students and SJC teachers**: P.V.R. Narasimha Rao, Visti Larsen, Sarajit Poddar, and others who credit Rath's teaching.
- **(C) Third party**: general sites with no stated tie to Rath.

**Reading status**
- **Opened** means the page was fetched and read through a summarising model, so every statement here is a paraphrase.
- **Snippet** means the page was seen only in search results.
- **Search summary, unpinned** means the search tool's own summary made the claim and no specific page was tied to it. These are leads, not findings, and they appear only under Gaps.

## Transits (gochara): reference point, techniques used, and ranking

### Takeaway
No retrieved Rath text sets out a general transit doctrine. That covers the reference point (Moon, lagna, AL or natal planets), vedha, ashtakavarga, Sade Sati, double transit, Kota chakra and tara bala. The only Rath-site transit reasoning actually read is a mundane article. It judges a transiting planet by counting malefic conjunctions, aspects and hemming around it, with no dasha or ashtakavarga. No ranking among transit methods was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Malefic hit count on a transiting significator.** A srath.com mundane article, "Curse of Jupiter in Sagittarius" (opened; byline not shown in the fetch, so tier A is not confirmed), reads world events from transiting Jupiter as the protector:
  - It treats Sagittarius as the natural house of protection.
  - It counts each malefic (Sun, Saturn, Mars, Rahu, Ketu) that conjoins or aspects Jupiter as a hit, and gives a percentage, about four of five described as an 80% malefic hit. The fetch inferred the formula; the page does not state it.
  - It adds a hit for papa kartari (malefics hemming Jupiter on both sides) and for a Mars–Ketu combination.
  - It counts Rahu's aspect (drishti) on Jupiter as a hit; the fetch did not say whether this is graha or rashi drishti.
  - It positions other planets counted from Jupiter, not from the Moon or lagna.
  - It calls Jupiter strong "at the tattva level" without defining the term.
  - It does not use vedha, ashtakavarga, Sarvatobhadra, dasha or an annual chart.
  - [srath.com, Curse of Jupiter in Sagittarius](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/mundane/curse-of-jupiter-in-sagittarius/)
- **Nodes' transit in an annual reading.** The India Tithi Pravesh 2008 page (opened; byline conflict below) compares transiting Rahu in Capricorn and Ketu in Cancer with the 1971 configuration as a supporting check. It does little other transit analysis. [srath.com, India Tithi Pravesh 2008](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/mundane/india-tithi-pravesh-2008/)
- **Kota chakra.** A srath.com page on Rani Rasmani (opened; byline not shown) says only that the author was working on the Kota chakra when the chart came up. It gives no Kota rules: no kota swami, kota pala, stambha or prakara entries. [srath.com, Rani Rasmani #1](https://srath.com/celebrity/india/rani-rasmani-1/)
- **Narayana dasa headings.** The page lists "effect of transit", "ashtakavarga" and "dasa pravesh chakra" as contents headings only, with no rule text; this is already recorded in parashari_timing.md. [srath.com, Narayana Dasha](https://srath.com/misc/naraya%e1%b9%87a-dasa/)
- **Sarvatobhadra chakra (C, opened).** The one Scribd upload opened shows no author (uploader "manus2005") and no body text. [Scribd, Sarvatobhadra Chakra](https://www.scribd.com/document/235266774/Sarvatobhadra-Chakra). The grid-and-vedha description is listed under Gaps because no page was pinned to it.

### Inferences
- In the one transit-heavy Rath-site article read, the method is graha-centred. A significator planet is judged by malefic contact, using conjunction, drishti of unstated type and papa kartari, counted from that planet. It is not judged by house position from the Moon. That article is mundane, and extending the method to natal charts is not established.
- Rath's transit doctrine is probably carried in books, courses or the unread Narayana and *Crux* sections, not on srath.com's public pages. Of the 101 pages of `?s=transit` results, the first page shows mostly mundane and news posts.

A second unpinned summary described the chakra as a 9x9 grid in which transiting planets cast vedha onto birth panchanga elements, such as the birth nakshatra.

## Annual charts: Tithi Pravesha rules, Tajika use, and annual versus natal

### Takeaway
Rath's own co-credited Tithi Pravesha page gives only the casting rule: the Sun in its natal sign, the Moon at the birth angle from the Sun, cast for the birthplace. This was already recorded. The reading rules come from tier B:
- **Poddar**: the weekday lord is the year lord, the hora lord is the most important factor, the TP lagna is read against the natal lagna, and the natal chart overrides the TP chart.
- **Narasimha Rao**: the hora lord is the lord of the year, per a snippet.

The sources conflict on which planet is the year lord, and on which dasha runs inside the year. No Rath text on Tajika Varshaphala, Muntha or Mudda dasa was found.

### Cited Findings
- **(A, co-credited, opened) Rath and P.V.R. Narasimha Rao's introduction.**
  - It casts the chart at the moment the Sun is in its natal sign and the Moon's angle from the Sun matches birth, using the birthplace coordinates.
  - It calls this the truest astrological birthday.
  - It gives no reading rules: no year lord, hora lord, houses or dasha within the year.
  - It discusses Tajika only as background. It mentions that some scholars see Tajika as non-Vedic and that Nilakantha and B.V. Raman accepted it. It says Tajika features such as Itthasala and Easarpha are not in Parashara's available teachings.
  - [srath.com, Tithi Pravesha](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/tithi-pravesa/tithi-pravesha/)
- **(srath.com, byline conflict, opened) India Tithi Pravesh 2008.** This is the only worked annual example on srath.com.
  - It runs Vimshottari dasa started from the Moon inside the annual chart, down to antardasha and pratyantara. Examples are the Ketu antardasha in Venus dasa, read as maraka, and the Moon pratyantara, read as the chart's 3rd lord.
  - It compares the 2008 chart with the 1971 chart for repeating configurations: Saturn in the 7th and the Mars–Saturn Yama yoga.
  - It also uses digbala, parivartana, marana karaka sthana, the Arudha lagna and the Atmakaraka.
  - It names no year lord or hora lord.
  - The posting path suggests December 2010.
  - **Byline conflict**: the direct fetch on 2026-11-18 reported "Sanjay Rath" as author. The category-page fetch and the earlier report both found no byline shown. Treat as unresolved; do not upgrade to tier A.
  - [srath.com, India Tithi Pravesh 2008](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/mundane/india-tithi-pravesh-2008/)
- **(B, opened) Sarajit Poddar, "Principles of Tithi Pravesh Chakra".** He credits the method to SJC tradition, made public by Rath. His rules:
  - The year starts when the Sun is back in its sign and the birth tithi recurs. He does not state the casting place.
  - **Year lord** = the weekday lord at the start of the tithi year. The weekday runs sunrise to sunrise, so a birth before sunrise counts as the previous weekday. A year lord that is a functional benefic for the natal lagna and well placed gives a good year. The year lord activates its natal yogas.
  - **TP lagna from the natal lagna**: a kendra gives happiness, a trikona gives prosperity, and a dusthana or badhaka house gives obstacles. The TP lagna lord shows success or failure in undertakings.
  - **Hora lord**: called the most important of the three factors. It sets the year's focus through its ownership, placement and yogas, refined in the vargas.
  - **Annual versus natal**: the natal chart has overriding influence, and the TP chart cannot be read on its own.
  - The same principles extend to Masa (month) and Dina (day) pravesha charts.
  - He mentions no dasha within the year.
  - [Sanskriti Magazine, Poddar](https://www.sanskritimagazine.com/principles-of-tithi-pravesh-chakra-in-vedic-astrology/)
- **(B, snippet only) Narasimha Rao, "Re-defining Tithi Pravesha Chart" (2014).**
  - The TP chart is cast when the Moon is at the birth angle from the Sun, within the same soli-lunar month as birth.
  - The hora lord at the TP moment gets special importance and is called the lord of the year.
  - [vedicastrologer.org, pp_tp.pdf](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/pp_tp.pdf) (unread PDF; snippet only)
- **(B, snippet) Jyotish Digest, May 2002.** A Scribd copy, "Tithi Pravesh-5", is described in search results as a Jyotish Digest article co-written by Rao and Rath, with the Sun–Moon angle definition. [Scribd, Tithi Pravesh-5](https://www.scribd.com/document/347971271/Tithi-Pravesh-5)
- **(C, search summary, page not pinned) Tithi lord as year lord.** A search summary attributes to dekhopanchang the rule that the tithi lord at the TP moment is the year lord. It named the site but not the page; [dekhopanchang, tithi pravesha](https://dekhopanchang.com/en/learn/tithi-pravesha) is a likely page, unconfirmed. Nothing ties the rule to Rath.
- **Year-lord conflict, three positions**:
  1. Weekday lord, with the hora lord as the most important separate factor (Poddar, B, opened).
  2. Hora lord as lord of the year (Rao, B, snippet).
  3. Tithi lord (dekhopanchang, C).

  No tier A text settles it.
- **Prior note**: the earlier report already records the Tithi Pravesha definition, the birthplace rule and that no confirming rule was found. Those points are not restated here.

### Inferences
- Taken together, tier B, plus the single srath.com example, suggests this reading order:
  1. Natal promise.
  2. TP lagna relative to the natal lagna.
  3. The year lord or hora lord's natal yogas.
  4. A dasha run inside the year to the pratyantara level, to place the event.

  This sequence is assembled from Poddar and the 2008 example. No source states it as a rule.
- The India 2008 page runs Moon-started Vimshottari in the annual chart. That conflicts with the unpinned claim that the TP dasha is Tithi Ashtottari (see Gaps). It is possible that both were used at different times or for different charts, mundane versus natal.

### Gaps
- **Dasha within the TP year**: the search summary, unpinned, says the dasha for the TP chakra is Tithi Ashtottari. No page was tied to that claim, and the conflict with the 2008 example is unresolved. The Tithi Ashtottari sequence, start nakshatra and starting lord were not found.
- **Houses to read in the TP chart**: Poddar says to read it like a normal rasi chart. No specific houses were found.
- **Tajika Varshaphala** (Muntha, Mudda dasa, Varsheshwara or Panchadhikari): no Rath text found. His TP page treats Tajika only as a contrast. Whether he uses Tajika at all is unknown.
- **Annual overriding or confirming natal dasha**: no tier A rule found. The only stated rule is Poddar's tier B one: the natal chart overrides, and the TP chart is not read alone.
- **Sarbani Rath**: no Tithi Pravesha writing found. The search returned only an unrelated Sankranti page.
- **Unread leads**: `pp_tp.pdf`; Scribd "Tithi-Pravesha-Chart-Analysis" (https://www.scribd.com/document/348629674/Tithi-Pravesha-Chart-Analysis); Scribd "Tithi-Pravesh-8" (https://www.scribd.com/document/347971275/Tithi-Pravesh-8). Scribd landing pages returned no body text in two attempts.

## Multi-method agreement: "dasha promises, transit triggers", triple confirmation, and picking the month, week or day

### Takeaway
No retrieved Rath text states a rule that dasha, transit and annual chart must agree before an event is predicted. None gives a minimum number of agreeing methods, and none gives a month, week or day procedure. The only agreement-type rules found are within one method: Vimshottari Dasha #2's coinciding end dates across Moon-based lines, already recorded. Elsewhere there is Poddar's tier B statement that the TP chart is subordinate to the natal chart, and the Masa and Dina pravesha extension for finer timing.

### Cited Findings
- **(A, prior)** Vimshottari Dasha #2: end dates that coincide across two or more Moon-based Vimshottari lines are the stronger signal, and the shorter period narrows the window. This is agreement within one dasha family. See parashari_timing.md. [srath.com, Vimshottari Dasha #2](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/dasa/vimshottari-dasha-2/)
- **(A, prior)** *Crux* children chapter: it suggests independent Narayana dashas as a double check on a Vimshottari-based birth timing. This is the nearest Rath statement of a two-method cross-check. See `reports/Sanjay Rath method summary.md`, children section, sect. 8.6.2.
- **(B, opened)** Poddar: the natal chart has overriding influence, and the TP chart cannot be read independently of it. Masa and Dina pravesha charts follow the same principles, which gives a month and day layer. [Sanskriti Magazine, Poddar](https://www.sanskritimagazine.com/principles-of-tithi-pravesh-chakra-in-vedic-astrology/)
- **(srath.com, byline conflict)** The India 2008 example layers three things: a repeating configuration from an earlier chart, a Vimshottari pratyantara inside the annual chart, and the nodes' transit. It does not state an agreement rule. [srath.com, India Tithi Pravesh 2008](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/mundane/india-tithi-pravesh-2008/)
- **(A, prior)** The *Crux* introduction says that ignoring the riders of Vimshottari dasha gives wrong predictions. This is a caution, not an agreement rule. See `reports/Sanjay Rath method summary.md`, "Limits Rath states himself".

### Inferences
- The structure found is a hierarchy, not a vote: natal promise first, then dasha, then the annual chart placing it in a year, then finer sub-periods or Masa and Dina charts, with transits as supporting checks. This is an inference from tier B Poddar and one srath.com example. It is not a Rath rule.

### Gaps
- No Rath text was found stating "dasha promises, transit triggers".
- None was found giving a triple-confirmation rule, a required number of agreeing systems, or what to do when methods disagree.
- None was found giving a procedure for selecting the month, week or day, beyond the tier B mention of Masa and Dina pravesha and the earlier-recorded pratyantara use.
- No order of application among transits, TP and Sudarshana was found in a tier A source.

## Muhurta and prashna as confirmation tools

### Takeaway
Rath's tradition teaches prashna. A course by his student Visti Larsen teaches Prashna-kendra and Prashna-arudha "as per" Rath's teachings, and one srath.com page uses a prashna chart for nashta jataka, reconstructing a lost birth time. No retrieved text presents prashna as a confirmation layer for natal timing. No Prashna Marga or Kerala rules attributed to Rath were found, and no Rath muhurta rules were found.

### Cited Findings
- **(B, opened)** Rama Academy course "Jyotish Prashna", taught by Visti Larsen, a Rath student who holds the Jaimini Scholar title.
  - It covers two topics, Prashna-kendra and Prashna-arudha, both per Rath's teachings.
  - Prashna-kendra: drawing the prashna chart, finding problems and remedies (prashna parihara), and reading it with the natal chart.
  - Prashna-arudha: the nature of the question, when to accept it, the outcome, and what to tell the querent.
  - The curriculum lists calculating the arudha, arudha bhava, chhatra, prashna yoga and Nashta Jataka.
  - The page states no rule text.
  - [Rama Academy, Prashna-kendra and Prashna-arudha](https://rama-academy.teachable.com/p/prashna-kendra-prashna-arudha)
- **(srath.com, byline not confirmed, opened)** Rani Rasmani #1 uses a prashna chart to reconstruct the birth time of someone born in 1793. It checks the prashna against the birth date and tithi, Jupiter's sign and the navamsa lagna. It expects Mandi in a kendra from the navamsa lagna to confirm a nashta jataka. [srath.com, Rani Rasmani #1](https://srath.com/celebrity/india/rani-rasmani-1/)
- **(srath.com site search, negative result)** srath.com `?s=prashna` returned no page with prashna rules in its excerpts. [srath.com search, prashna](https://srath.com/?s=prashna)

### Inferences
- In this lineage, prashna appears in two roles: answering a question in its own right (Prashna-arudha) and rectifying or reconstructing birth data (nashta jataka). Using it as a third confirmation of a natal prediction is not shown.

### Gaps
- Not found:
  - how the prashna arudha is computed;
  - Prashna Marga or Kerala methods (ashtamangala, arudha by object);
  - any rule that prashna must agree with natal dasha;
  - any Rath muhurta rule.

  The course videos and slides may hold these, but they were not accessible.
- No source found for Freedom Cole's or Sarbani Rath's material on these topics.
