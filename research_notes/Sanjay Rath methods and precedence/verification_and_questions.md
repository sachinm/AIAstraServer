## Q1. Rectification: which methods, and in what order?

### Takeaway
In his own book, Rath sets the order explicitly. First, use the visible facts (appearance) to confirm the rising sign. Then cut the range step by step with varga lagnas, each tested against a fact the client confirms: D3 for siblings, D9 for the spouse, D7 for children. Then use the vighati count to confirm sex. Fine-tuning with Kunda comes last. Tattva and Kunda are finishing tools, not the first step. His senior student PVR Narasimha Rao goes further: he says Kunda and Pranapada fail the twins test, so known events are the only reliable method.

### Cited Findings
- **(A) The stated order.** Before the divisional charts are judged, the birth time should first be rectified from the Navamsa, the Dwadasamsa and other key vargas. It is then fine-tuned with the vighati rule of the Jaimini Sutras. — *Crux* Ch. II sect. 8, p.34 ([PDF](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf))
- **(A) Step 0, physique fixes the rising sign.** In Chart 7 the reported window was 1:30 to 2:00 AM. He accepted the Aquarius rising sign because the native's average height, slim build and dark complexion fit it. — *Crux* p.36
- **(A) Step 1, siblings come first.** He calls verifying "existing relatives" the first step in chart correction. Because the time window spanned two drekkanas, "the question of co-borns was taken up". — *Crux* p.36
  - He reads elder siblings from the 11th house from the Arudha Lagna and younger siblings from the 3rd. Odd or even AL sets the counting direction. Venus in the 11th from AL meant the native was eldest and any elder sibling would have died. **Confirmed by the native.** — *Crux* p.36
  - In the 3rd house and its lord: many younger siblings, and at least one short-lived. **Confirmed.** — *Crux* p.36
  - He tested two candidate drekkana (D3) lagnas, Gemini and Libra, against the fate of the first younger sibling. The Libra D3 lagna implied early death of the next sibling, and the immediately younger sibling had died shortly after marriage. He then read the sex of each later sibling in sequence from successive lords. The native confirmed the sequence, which narrowed the lagna to Aquarius 20° to 28°06'. — *Crux* pp.36-37
- **(A) Step 2, spouse fixes the navamsa lagna.** The native volunteered that his wife had died after two children. Rath took the Upapada and the 8th lord, the 2nd from the 7th, as indicators of danger to the spouse. He then picked the navamsa lagna (Aries, Taurus or Gemini) that put Venus and Mercury in dusthanas and Mars and Ketu in the 7th. That was Taurus. He added a character check: Saturn in the D9 lagna means a person who earns by their own skill. **The native confirmed this.** The range became Aquarius 23°20' to 26°40'. — *Crux* pp.37-38
- **(A) Step 3, children fix the saptamsa lagna.** Two D7 lagnas, Cancer and Leo, were possible in that range. Both gave a son first, possibly with ailments, and the first child was a disabled son. The sex of the second child separated them: Cancer pointed to a son, Leo to a daughter. The second child was a daughter, so the D7 lagna was Leo (Aquarius 25°43' to 26°40'). — *Crux* p.38
- **(A) Step 4, cross-checks from the second marriage.** He read the second marriage from the 8th from the Upapada and the 8th from the 7th. **Confirmed.** He read the children of the second wife from the 8th from the D7 5th house, using a "Manduka gati" (frog-jump) count: son, daughter, then a short-tempered son. "All this was confirmed by the amazed native." — *Crux* pp.38-39
- **(A) Sex check (vighati / tattva-type rule).** Convert the birth time from sunrise or sunset into vighatis and divide by nine. Round any fraction up. Count the remainder from the Sun in weekday order. The planet reached "confirms the sex as male, female or eunuch". — *Crux* pp.35-36
- **(A) Kunda fine-tuning comes last.** Multiply the ascendant longitude by 81 and remove multiples of 360°. The result should fall in a trine to the janma nakshatra (natal Moon's star) or the nama nakshatra (name star). In Chart 7 the Moon was in Rohini. The minimum lagna gave a Kunda in Pushya, but it needed to be in Hasta, a Moon-ruled star trine to Rohini. So he added 40' to the cusp, at about 10' per nakshatra over four stars. This gave a rectified time of 1:54 AM IST. — *Crux* p.39
- **(A) The final proof is prediction.** He calls a chart's predictive value "the final test of a corrected chart". In Chart 7 the father's death, which the chart had anticipated, came after seven days in a coma, and that confirmed the chart. — *Crux* p.39
- **(A) Rectifying a chart with no birth data through prashna.** For a historical figure with no recorded time, he used a prashna to choose the lagna and the navamsa lagna from three candidates. He then tested the chart against her marriage age, the husband's profile, the number and sex of her children, and the year of the husband's death, timed by dasha. — [srath.com, Rani Rasmani Jataka](https://srath.com/celebrity/india/rani-rasmani-jataka/)
- **(A) The service menu.** srath.com offers separate products for birth-time correction (vague time), determination (only date and place known), date-and-time determination (only month and place known) and lost data (only the year known). Clients are asked to take the time from hospital records or the mother. — [srath.com, The Process](https://srath.com/consultation/the-process/)
- **(B) Visti Larsen (Rath's student), Tattva and Kunda.** Larsen's article on Tattwa and birth-time rectification, which names Rath as guide, gives these rules:
  - Kunda equals the lagna × 81, so it moves 81 times faster than the lagna.
  - It should fall in a trine or the 7th from the rasi lagna.
  - A lagna correction of more than about 3° also changes the D60 and D10 lagnas, which must be re-checked.
  - Kunda "cannot be used independently" of varga rectification.
  - The source is a search snippet of the Scribd copy. The full text did not load, and a reviewer notes the worked arithmetic is inconsistent. — [Scribd, Tattwa and Birthtime Rectification](https://es.scribd.com/doc/86362659/tattva2-1). Larsen's own site also has a "Tattva, Antara Tattva, Mahapurusha Yoga and Kunda" article. Only its summary loaded. — [srigaruda.com](https://srigaruda.com/tattva-antara-tattva-mahapurusha-yoga-and-kunda/)
- **(B) Visti Larsen, name-based rectification.** Larsen answered a reader without asking for any life events. He derived a name sign by Katapayadi and adjusted the time so the lagna degree linked to it. He then checked the result against the D60 lagna, the Hora chakra for the name's first syllable, and Ketu's dispositor. — [srigaruda.com, Name & rectification](https://srigaruda.com/name-a-rectification/)
- **(B) PVR Narasimha Rao (Rath's student), dissent on formula methods.** He calls three methods "reasonable": Tattva siddhanta, Pranapada lagna in navamsa, and Kunda. He then says Pranapada and Kunda "fail the acid test of twins", and that Tattva siddhanta is beyond the scope of his book. In his view the only correct way is to find the nearby time that explains the native's "nature, credentials, attitude and aptitude" and known past events. — *Vedic Astrology: An Integrated Approach*, Ch. 32.1, pp.421-422 ([vedicastrologer.org PDF](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf))
- **(B) PVR, client-reported times.** In his experience, birth times reported by clients are "seldom accurate". He lists the causes: an unadjusted clock, a time noted down late, a parent's faulty memory, and differing definitions of "birth". — Ch. 32.1, p.421 (same PDF)
- **(B) PVR's practical order.** Fix the D9 or D10 lagna first, then move to higher vargas such as D20, D24 and Kalachakra. Be ready to go back to the D9. If the person is married, use the marriage to fix the D9. If not, use their sense of duty, basic skills and way of dealing with others. His worked example runs:
  - D4: moved abroad in Jupiter dasha
  - D9: temperament (intellectual and mild versus dynamic and artistic)
  - D10: career as a software engineer, read from the D10 AL
  - D24: studied computer science in Jupiter dasha

  — Ch. 32.3-32.4, pp.427-431 (same PDF)
- **(B) PVR on sensitivity.** The lagna moves about 1° every 4 minutes, so the D10 lagna changes sign every 12 minutes and the D24 lagna every 5 minutes. A planet sitting on a varga border has to be tested both ways. A 2-minute error shifts Vimshottari dates by about (n×m)/4 days, and Kalachakra dates by (n×m) days. — Ch. 32.2, pp.422-426 (same PDF)
- **(C)** A third-party Jagannatha Hora guide says traditional practice expects the Kunda to align with the birth nakshatra and the lagna lord's nakshatra. This was a search snippet only, and it is not an SJC source. — [jagannathhora.com BTR guide](https://jagannathhora.com/is-your-birth-time-correct-the-complete-diy-rectification-btr-guide-for-jagannatha-hora/)

### Inferences
- **What Rath ranks first.** In practice, Rath's first method is event-based varga rectification led by family facts: siblings by D3, then spouse by D9, then children by D7. The appearance check on the rising sign comes before it. Kunda is explicitly a "fine tuning" step and comes last. The sex check is a sanity check. This is a sequence, not a single winning technique.
- **Where Rath and PVR differ.** They agree that known events decide the time. They disagree on Kunda as a final tool: Rath uses it in Crux, and PVR rejects it on the twins argument.
- **What this means for an assistant.** Facts first, and no rectification by formula alone. Any automatic Kunda or tattva result should be treated as a weak tiebreaker.
- **What was not found.** No Rath primary text was found that ranks D60 or D150 (nadi-amsa) as a rectification tool. On the Moon's nakshatra pada, what was found is the Kunda link: a trine to the janma nakshatra or nama nakshatra. No separate pada rule was found.

### Gaps
- No Rath primary text was found on Pranapada rectification, D60 or D150 nadi-amsa rectification, or D12-based parent checks as a rectification step. The D12 is listed among the first rectification vargas (Crux p.34) but has no worked example in that section.
- The full Visti Larsen Tattva article could not be loaded, so the Tattva rules here rest on a snippet.
- *Collected Papers in Vedic Astrology* was not accessible.

## Q2. Which life events does he ask for, and how does each map to the chart?

### Takeaway
The events Rath asks for in his own worked cases are family structure and family deaths. These cover elder and younger siblings (count, order, sex and early deaths), the spouse (death or misfortune, and a second marriage), children (sex, order, health), and the father's death. He also asks about the person's character and way of earning. Each event is tested in a named varga, with the 3rd or 11th counted from the Arudha Lagna, and timed by Vimshottari or Narayana dasha. PVR adds moving abroad (D4), career (D10) and education (D24).

### Cited Findings
- **(A) Siblings.** Older and younger siblings are read from the 11th and 3rd counted from the Arudha Lagna, with the direction set by an odd or even AL. Then the D3 lagna, the 3rd and 11th lords and the Bhratrukaraka are added. — *Crux* pp.36-37
- **(A) A second sibling case in his book.** In Chart 35 he correctly placed the native as the youngest, with six elder siblings, from the AL. He then read each elder sibling's sex in order from the D3, including twins. To confirm the reading he offered a detail about the eldest sister: emotional, rash, and a troubled marriage. "This was confirmed." — *Crux* Ch. VI (Third House), pp.119-120
- **(A) Spouse.** He reads danger to the spouse from the Upapada, its 2nd house and lord, and the 8th lord as the 2nd from the 7th. The navamsa must agree, through dusthana placement from the D9 lagna and the Darakaraka relative to the 7th. A second marriage is read from the 8th from the UL and the 8th from the 7th. — *Crux* pp.37-38
- **(A) Children.** He reads the sex and order of children in the D7. The first child is taken from the 5th lord, the second from the 7th lord, and the count runs zodiacal or reverse by the sign. Children by a second spouse are read from the 8th from the D7 5th. — *Crux* pp.38-39
- **(A) Before speaking of children.** He confirms physical capacity first, using the Deha method: the arudha padas of the 3rd and 9th from the lagna and from the Sun. — *Crux* 8.5.5. Already in prior notes, so it is not expanded here.
- **(A) Father's death.** In the D12, a parent's death is read from the 3rd and 10th houses, and he timed one by Vimshottari: "Father died in Venus dasa Saturn antardasa." Narayana dasha also tracks the loss of both parents, father in the 13th year and mother two years later. — *Crux* Ch. IX (Sixth House), pp.213-214, Hitler example
- **(A) Which dasha to use.** A nakshatra dasha such as Vimshottari pinpoints an event, but its occurrence "will require confirmation from a Rasi dasa" such as Narayana. — *Crux* Introduction, p.3
- **(A) Character and livelihood.** He uses them as evidence: "earns by own skill" from Saturn in the D9 lagna, confirmed by the native. — *Crux* p.38
- **(A) srath.com intake.** The intake includes a study of the name and of any name changes, and "a detailed study of the past is made" to support the forecast. — [srath.com, The Process](https://srath.com/consultation/the-process/)
- **(B) PVR's event set.** PVR uses moving abroad (D4, timed by dasha), marriage (D9), career and how others see the person at work (D10 and its AL), and education (D24, A4, timed by dasha). — PVR Ch. 32.3-32.4, pp.427-431 ([PDF](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf))

Each question below is tied to the chart factor it tests. **A** means the question appears in Rath's own worked cases. **B** means it comes from PVR or Larsen. **Inference** means it extends the stated rule.

### Inferences
- **The order of questions in his worked case is fixed.** He starts with siblings, which are low-sensitivity and well known to the person. He then moves to the spouse and then to children. Each answer halves the candidate range before the next question.
- **Each question is a discriminator.** It is asked because two candidate varga lagnas predict different answers, not to gather a general history. An assistant can copy this: ask only what separates the remaining candidates.
- **Question 12 needs care.** In Rath's case the father's death was the final predictive proof (Crux p.39). That makes it a forecast that came true, not something to say to a client cold. This rests on the case text only.

### Gaps
- No Rath text was found that asks about job changes, house moves or the end of education as rectification events. Those come from PVR, tier B.
- No list of required events was found on any srath.com intake form. The "Vedic Form" itself was not read.

## Q3. Consultation approach: past first, question first, ethics

### Takeaway
Rath's published process is client-led. The person sends birth data and a form choosing the life matters to cover, and "the reading will go in the direction in which you will lead". The past is studied in detail before forecasting. In his book cases the past is confirmed with the person aloud ("This was confirmed"), so verification is a dialogue step and not a silent calculation. No Rath statement was found on how to word death or health forecasts, apart from items already covered in prior notes: the fertility check, the medical referral and the disclaimer.

### Cited Findings
- **(A) Intake requirements.** The client must send birth data before booking, take the birth time from a hospital record or the mother, and give their religion. They also say whether they object to gemstone advice, and fill in a form choosing the topics. "A detailed study of the past is made". Calls are recorded and the recordings are kept for 14 days. — [srath.com, The Process](https://srath.com/consultation/the-process/)
- **(A) What the services cover.** Jyotish is described as examining "the past, present and future trends", using horoscopy, prashna, numerology and muhurta. Mantra, prayer and puja are offered to soften negative trends. — [srath.com, About Your Future](https://srath.com/consultation/about-future/)
- **(A) A live dialogue in the book.** He offered a specific detail about a sibling "to confirm the readings" and recorded the confirmation. He then says the same method lets the astrologer confirm details of each sibling's life and predict their well-being. — *Crux* p.120
- **(A) The same pattern in the rectification case.** In Chart 7 he states an indication, records the native's answer, and only then moves on. The forecast (the father's death) comes after the chart is fixed. — *Crux* pp.36-39
- **(A) Remedies wait for verification.** Before naming a curse and its remedy, the astrologer should confirm the chart is physically capable of children. — *Crux* 8.5.5, p.~173 (prior notes)
- **(C) Ethics and death.** The search found no Rath or SJC statement on refusing questions or on handling death predictions. Third-party ethics pages hold that predicting death is unethical, but they are not tied to Rath. — e.g. [jagannathhora.com ethics code](https://jagannathhora.com/ethical-astrologers-code-guidelines/) (not opened; snippet only)

### Inferences
- **His method implies past first.** Rath's method implies "read and confirm the past, then predict". The confirmation is both a technical step that fixes the varga lagnas and a trust step: "amazed native" (Crux p.39).
- **This fits the assistant's rule.** It supports the project rule that the assistant confirms before it predicts, and that its questions follow from known facts.

### Gaps
- No first-hand student account of a live Rath consultation was found. One Medium post about a guru questioning a disciple returned 403, and it is not confirmed to be about Rath.
- No Rath statement was found on how to word health or death forecasts, how to avoid causing fear, or when to refuse a question. The prior notes record the medical referral (Crux 8.5.5(A)) and the disclaimer.

## Q4. How the question itself is used: prashna, time of query, stated versus indicated concern

### Takeaway
Rath uses prashna to recover a chart when the birth data is lost, and he lists prashna among his services. In *Crux* he cites Jataka Parijata's option of starting Vimshottari from the Moon's star at the time of the query. He then rejects it for natal work in favour of the stronger of the lagna and the Moon. No Rath text was found on prashna arudha or on comparing the client's stated concern with the chart's indication.

### Cited Findings
- **(A) Starting star for Vimshottari.** Jataka Parijata (XVIII.33-35) lists four candidates: the lagna star, the Moon star, the name-initial star, and the Moon star "at query". — *Crux* Introduction, p.4-5
- **(A) His own ruling.** For natal work, Vimshottari should start from the stronger of the lagna and the Moon, "ignoring" the utpanna, name and query stars. — *Crux* Introduction, p.~6
- **(A) Prashna to recover a lost chart.** "After the praśna we were able to get the rāśi and some varga charts" for a figure with no recorded time. The result was then tested against her life events. — [srath.com, Rani Rasmani Jataka](https://srath.com/celebrity/india/rani-rasmani-jataka/)
- **(A) Prashna with a devotional device.** He describes seven verses written on folded slips of paper, used during a prashna chart after prayer. — [srath.com, Sapta Sloki Gita](https://srath.com/spirituality/jagannath-vi%e1%b9%a3%e1%b9%87u/sapta-sloki-gita/) (seen only as a search-page excerpt)
- **(A) Prashna as a service.** Prashna is listed as one of the jyotish services. — [srath.com, About Your Future](https://srath.com/consultation/about-future/)

### Inferences
- **For an assistant.** The moment of a question is a legitimate fallback chart when birth data is missing, in Rath's practice. It does not replace the natal chart for timing when the natal chart is known.

### Gaps
- Nothing was found on prashna arudha, on judging the questioner's intent, or on resolving a mismatch between the stated concern and the chart's indication. A dedicated Prashna Marga commentary by Rath or SJC was not found.
- The search engine did not index srath.com or srigaruda.com well. Deeper site pages (prashna series pages 2-5, the Vedic Form) were not read.
