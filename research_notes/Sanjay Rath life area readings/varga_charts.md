## What does Rath say each varga signifies, and how are vargas grouped by level of consciousness or harmonic cycle?

### Takeaway
Rath groups vargas into five 12-chart harmonic cycles, each a deeper plane of consciousness: D1–D12 physical, D13–D24 conscious, D25–D36 sub-conscious, D37–D48 super-conscious, D49–D60 supra-conscious. Within each cycle only a few charts are used, each with one life area (D2 wealth, D3 siblings, D4 property, D7 children, D9 spouse, D10 work, D12 parents, D16 vehicles and happiness, D20 spirituality, D24 learning, D27 strengths and weaknesses, D30 evils, D40 maternal karma, D45 paternal karma, D60 past-life karma).

### Cited Findings
- Harmonic rule: a division number repeats every twelve, so a chart's cycle is found by dividing N by 12 (examples given: 16 = 12+4, 40 = 36+4, so D16 and D40 are higher octaves of D4) — [Rath's own text] [Principles of Divisional Charts, section on harmonics](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/principles-of-divisional-charts/)
- Five cycles (his Table 4) — [Rath's own text] [same article](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/principles-of-divisional-charts/):
  | Cycle | Plane | Charts | Charts actually used | Focus |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Primary | Physical | D1–D12 | D1–D12 | body, wealth, siblings, property, authority, children, spouse, work, parents |
  | Secondary (1st harmonic) | Conscious | D13–D24 | D16, D20, D24 | mental happiness, spirituality, learning |
  | Tertiary (2nd harmonic) | Sub-conscious | D25–D36 | D27, D30 | strengths and weaknesses; evils |
  | Quaternary (3rd harmonic) | Super-conscious | D37–D48 | D40, D45 | matrilineal and patrilineal karma |
  | Pentenary (4th harmonic) | Supra-conscious | D49–D60 | D60 | karma of past births |
- Higher vargas beyond D60 are named (D72, D81 Nadiamsa-type names, D108 Astottaramsa, D144, D150, D300) but no signification is given in that article — [Rath's own text] [same article](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/principles-of-divisional-charts/)
- Per-chart significations and alternate names — [Rath's own text] [same article, significations table](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/principles-of-divisional-charts/):
  - D1 Rasi (Bhagana / Bha chakra): the physical body.
  - D2 Hora: material wealth.
  - D3 Drekkana: siblings (co-borns).
  - D4 Chaturthamsa (also Tureeyamsa): immovable property / fixed assets.
  - D5 Panchamsa: authority or power over others.
  - D6 Shashtamsa (Kaulaka): named, no area given in that article.
  - D7 Saptamsa: children.
  - D8 Ashtamsa: named, no area given.
  - D9 Navamsa (also Dharmamsa): spouse.
  - D10 Dasamsa (also Swargamsa): work, profession, actions in society.
  - D11 Rudramsa (also Labhamsa): named, no area given there.
  - D12 Dwadasamsa (also Suryamsa): parents.
  - D16 Shodasamsa (also Kalamsa): mental happiness, luxuries, vehicles.
  - D20 Vimsamsa: spirituality, worship, occult study.
  - D24 Chaturvimsamsa (Siddhamsa / Bhamsa): learning and education.
  - D27 Nakshatramsa: mental and physical strengths and weaknesses (examples: strong Moon in a kendra shows mental strength; weak Mercury may show speech trouble).
  - D30 Trimsamsa: "all evils" (misfortune, troubles), the focus Parasara sets for this group.
  - D40 Khavedamsa: matrilineal karma (mother's line).
  - D45 Akshavedamsa: patrilineal karma (father's line).
  - D60 Shashtyamsa: karma of past births; given the highest weight among the sixteen (Shodasavarga) by Parasara.
- Nomenclature: vargas are named five ways — by division number, by a reference number (e.g. Nakshatramsa = 27), by use (e.g. Siddhamsa for learning), by multiple traditional names (Shodasamsa = Kalamsa), or by "D-number" as in P.V.R. Narasimha Rao's convention — [Rath's own text] [same article, nomenclature section](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/principles-of-divisional-charts/)
- D60 has Saṅjñā (consort of the Sun) as its overlord; mentioned in passing in a mythology article — [Rath's own text] [Yama Deva, srath.com](https://srath.com/arista/yama-deva)
- Rath taught a workshop on critical bhavas for wealth in D1, D9 and D60 together (BAVA 18th conference, 2016), so D60 is used by him for wealth judgement too, not only past-life karma — [Rath's own text, workshop listing] [BAVA 18th International Conference](https://srath.com/learn/workshops/bava-18th-international-conference/)

- Navamsa versus Dasamsa for career, from a Rath-attributed lecture summary: D9 shows skills and the nature of the profession, D10 shows success and failure in it; the strength of the relevant planet in D1, D9 and D10 points to the gainful line of work — [secondary, unverified Scribd upload titled "Navamsa Sanjay Rath"; byline not checked] [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/356674100/Navamsa-Sanjay-Rath)

### Inferences
- The harmonic scheme implies an octave reading: D16 is a higher octave of D4 (property becomes vehicles and comforts), D40 a higher octave of D4 too (home becomes matrilineal inheritance), D20 of D8, D24 of D12 (parents become teachers / learning), D45 of D9 (spouse / dharma becomes paternal lineage). Rath's article states the 12-cycle arithmetic; the octave meanings are an inference from it, not quoted.
- For a product, the first cycle (D1–D12) maps to concrete life boards (money, siblings, home, children, spouse, work, parents); D16/D20/D24 to inner-life themes; D27/D30 to strengths and troubles; D40/D45/D60 to deep karma and should be presented carefully, since D60 needs very accurate birth time (general consequence of a 30-arc-minute division).

### Gaps

- Could not read the full text of "Varga Chakra" or "Crux of Vedic Astrology"; chapter contents beyond titles are not available online legitimately.

## What step-by-step rule does he give for reading any divisional chart?

### Takeaway
Rath's rule set: cast the varga by mapping each amsa to a sign; read the varga from its own lagna; find the karya rasi (the house of the chart's subject, e.g. the 10th in D10) and check that the D1 lord of that house is well placed in the varga; find the karyesha (significators of the activity) and use argala to see which ones flourish or fail as dashas change; also check the Moon's dispositor (Subhapati) across vargas for health and longevity.

### Cited Findings
- Casting: split each sign into N equal parts; each part maps to a fixed sign of the D-N chart, and a planet or lagna in that part is placed in that sign. Worked D3 example: first 10° to the sign itself, second 10° to the 5th from it, last 10° to the 9th from it (lagna 14° Pisces → Cancer; Saturn 27° Capricorn → Virgo) — [Rath's own text] [Principles of Divisional Charts, "dividing a sign"](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/principles-of-divisional-charts/)
- Karya rasi: the house that is the focus of the chart's activity (example: 10th house in D10). Its lord in the Rasi chart should be well placed in the varga — [Rath's own text] [same article, technical terms](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/principles-of-divisional-charts/)
- Karyesha: the significator(s) of the activity; can be several planets and varies by chart. Rath's example is a person who is both politician and lawyer: argala on the karyesha decides which role thrives or fails as the dasas change — [Rath's own text] [same article](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/principles-of-divisional-charts/)
- Karakas are of three kinds: natural (naisargika), variable (chara) and fixed (sthira); basics such as rasi drishti, graha drishti and argala are deferred to standard texts and apply in vargas as in D1 — [Rath's own text] [same article](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/principles-of-divisional-charts/)
- Subhapati (dispositor of the Moon): its dignity across the vargas shows health and longevity. If it joins lagna or the Atmakaraka it forms Kevala yoga; if it also links to Hora Lagna or Ghatika Lagna it becomes Kevala Yogada; with both further links, Kevala Mahayogada — [Rath's own text] [same article](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/principles-of-divisional-charts/)
- Yoga in vargas is formed the same way as in D1: conjunction, mutual aspect, sign or nakshatra exchange, or mutual placement in each other's signs — [Rath's own text] [same article](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/principles-of-divisional-charts/)
- SJC student application of the karyesha rule to D2: the 2nd lord of the Rasi chart is the karyesha of the hora chart; its placement and strength in the hora chart is a key (not sole) factor for wealth; houses are also read from the hora chart's Arudha Lagna (perception of finances) and from arudha padas (actual events), with the 12th pada (UL) showing real expenditure — [secondary, P.V.R. Narasimha Rao, SJC, class at SJC East Coast Conference, Edison NJ, 31 Aug 2003; reposted by a Rath student blog] [Using Kashinatha Hora Chart](https://jyotish-blog.blogspot.com/2008/06/using-kashinatha-hora-chart.html)
- Same SJC school: the Rasi is only a special case of a divisional chart; all chart principles apply in every varga, but each varga is consulted only for its own matters — [secondary, P.V.R. Narasimha Rao, "Vedic Astrology: An Integrated Approach", as summarised in search results] [PDF listing](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/vedic-astrology-an-integrated-approach2.pdf)
- Varga Chakra contains SJC chapters on arudha in divisional charts, special lagnas in D-charts and divisional dignities, showing the school reads arudhas, special lagnas (Hora Lagna, Ghatika Lagna etc.) and varga dignity inside each varga — [Rath's own site, chapter list] [srath.com Varga Chakra](https://srath.com/?p=58)

### Inferences
- A usable procedure from these sources: (1) build the varga; (2) take the varga lagna and its lord; (3) mark the karya bhava (2nd in D2, 3rd in D3, 4th in D4, 5th in D7, 7th in D9, 10th in D10, 9th/4th in D12 by parent, etc.); (4) place the D1 lord of that house in the varga and judge its dignity; (5) identify karyesha (D1 house lord, natural karaka, chara karaka) and their argala; (6) read arudhas for how the matter is seen; (7) time with a dasha run from the karyesha's varga sign (see dasha section). Steps 3 and 5 for charts other than D10/D2 are an extrapolation of Rath's stated D10 example, not quoted per chart.

### Gaps
- No public Rath text found listing the karya bhava for every varga explicitly (only the D10 example).
- No public source found on Rath's stance on whether a natural karaka in the varga lagna harms that chart's matter; secondary web sources disagree.

## Which calculation variants does he prefer for D2, D3, D4 and others?

### Takeaway
The public record shows Rath's school treats the standard Parasara D2 (Sun/Moon halves, Leo/Cancer only) as Parasara's wealth hora, rejects Parivritti-dvaya hora as a wealth chart, and promotes the Kashinatha hora from the Rath family tradition as the hora that shows wealth. For D3 his own worked example uses the Parasara drekkana (1st, 5th, 9th). No public source was found stating his preference for D4 or other variants.

### Cited Findings
- D2 base: Rath's article describes hora as the zodiac's solar half (Leo–Capricorn) and lunar half (Cancer–Aquarius), with the Sun and Moon owning Leo and Cancer and the other planets assigned by proximity — [Rath's own text] [Principles of Divisional Charts, hora section](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/principles-of-divisional-charts/)
- D3: worked example uses Parasara drekkana (sign, 5th, 9th) — [Rath's own text] [same article](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/principles-of-divisional-charts/)
- Rath wrote the "Hora Chakra" chapter of Varga Chakra (contents not public) — [Rath's own site] [srath.com Varga Chakra](https://srath.com/?p=58)
- Parasara hora as described by SJC: first half of odd signs and second half of even signs are Sun's hora (Leo), the rest Moon's hora (Cancer) — [secondary, P.V.R. Narasimha Rao] [Using Kashinatha Hora Chart](https://jyotish-blog.blogspot.com/2008/06/using-kashinatha-hora-chart.html)
- Parivritti-dvaya hora (cyclic: each half maps in zodiac order) is rejected as Parasara's wealth chart because it has no link to the luminaries; in this school it shows family support for one's activities, not wealth — [secondary, P.V.R. Narasimha Rao] [Using Kashinatha Hora Chart](https://jyotish-blog.blogspot.com/2008/06/using-kashinatha-hora-chart.html); search-result summary of the same author's [hora.pdf](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/hora.pdf) (PDF text not readable by the fetch tool)
- Kashinatha hora: attributed to the tradition of Sri Achyuta Dasa and named after Pt. Kashinath Rath (a secondary web source calls him Sanjay Rath's uncle; not verified). Rule: signs are day-strong (Leo, Virgo, Libra, Scorpio, Aquarius, Pisces — Sun group) or night-strong (Aries, Taurus, Gemini, Cancer, Sagittarius, Capricorn — Moon group). A planet in the Sun's hora goes to the day-strong sign owned by its sign lord; in the Moon's hora, to the night-strong sign owned by its sign lord. Examples: Jupiter 25°16' Taurus → Libra; Mars 18°53' Sagittarius → Sagittarius. This chart shows wealth, resources at one's disposal, gains, losses, transactions — [secondary, P.V.R. Narasimha Rao, 2003] [Using Kashinatha Hora Chart](https://jyotish-blog.blogspot.com/2008/06/using-kashinatha-hora-chart.html)
- Rao's same article lists parivritti (cyclic) charts as D2 (in its PD form), D3, D4, D5 and D9, and notes non-Parasari drekkana and chaturthamsa variants exist; drekkana is read as the initiative behind one's actions — [secondary, P.V.R. Narasimha Rao] [Using Kashinatha Hora Chart](https://jyotish-blog.blogspot.com/2008/06/using-kashinatha-hora-chart.html)
- In "Vedic Astrology: An Integrated Approach", Rao says variants of D2, D3, D8 and D11 are not covered in the book; elsewhere he reads D3 as initiative behind karma and D4 as the direction (gati) behind karma — [secondary, search-result summary of Rao's book and handouts] [Integrated Approach PDF listing](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/vedic-astrology-an-integrated-approach2.pdf)
- Varga Chakra includes a Drekkana chapter (G.K. Goel), a Kaulaka/Shatamsa chapter, a Turyamsa (D4) chapter (Visti Larsen) and a Suryamsa (D12) chapter, which is where SJC variants would be discussed — [Rath's own site, chapter list] [srath.com Varga Chakra](https://srath.com/?p=58)

### Inferences
- For wealth, a Rath-school reading uses the Kashinatha hora, not the Parivritti-dvaya hora; the plain Parasara Leo/Cancer hora is kept but is low resolution (all planets in two signs).
- Since the Rath article's D3 worked example is Parasara drekkana and he files siblings under D3, the default D3 for siblings in this school is Parasara's.

### Gaps
- No public, verifiable Rath text found on the Jagannatha, Somanatha or Parivritti drekkana variants or which he prefers for which question; searches returned nothing relevant. Do not encode a preference without the book.
- No public source found on Rath's D4 variant (Parasara kendra mapping vs other), D7 even-sign rule, D10 even-sign start, or D16/D20/D24/D30 variants. Rath's own Kashinatha hora mapping is reported only by Rao, not in Rath's own public text.
- Rao's hora.pdf could not be read (binary PDF).

## How does he use vargas with dasha timing?

### Takeaway
Rath's own public text says only that argala shows which karyesha flourish or fail as the dasas change. His school (as taught by Rao) times a varga's matter with a sign dasa (Narayana dasa) started from the karyesha's sign in that varga, e.g. Hora Narayana dasa from the D2 position of the D1 2nd lord, and reads transits from the varga's Moon and lagna.

### Cited Findings
- Dasas activate karyeshas, and argala on them decides which bloom or decline as dasas change — [Rath's own text] [Principles of Divisional Charts](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/principles-of-divisional-charts/)
- Crux of Vedic Astrology introduces divisional charts together with Narayana and Shula dasas (timing book) — [Rath's own site, book description] [srath.com Books](https://srath.com/?p=152)
- Hora Narayana dasa: start from the hora-chart sign occupied by the D1 2nd lord, treated as lagna; judge each dasa by its sign, its paaka (sign holding the dasa sign's lord) and bhoga (the dasa sign's arudha pada); split each dasa into thirds, with results falling early or late by whether the sign is shirshodaya, prishthodaya or ubhayodaya; credits Rath's "Narayana Dasa" book — [secondary, P.V.R. Narasimha Rao, 2003] [Using Kashinatha Hora Chart](https://jyotish-blog.blogspot.com/2008/06/using-kashinatha-hora-chart.html)
- Varga Chakra includes "Transits from divisional Moon and lagna" (Rao), so the school times varga matters by transits counted from varga lagna and varga Moon — [Rath's own site, chapter list] [srath.com Varga Chakra](https://srath.com/?p=58)
- An SJC presentation pairs Narayana dasa with Vimsottari dasa and the D10 10th house for career timing — [secondary, search-result summary of a Scribd SJC slide deck; byline not verified] [Dasamsa: Loss and Gain in Career](https://es.scribd.com/presentation/229619583/Dasamsa-Loss-and-Gain-in-Career)

### Inferences
- Generalising the Hora Narayana dasa rule: for a varga DN with karya bhava H, a Rath-school timing is Narayana dasa of DN started from the DN sign of the D1 lord of H (e.g. D10 dasa from the D10 sign of the D1 10th lord). This is an extension of the D2 example, not a quoted rule for every varga.
- Vimsottari (or other nakshatra dasa) lords would be judged in the relevant varga for the matter in question — the karyesha principle supports this but no Rath text found states it step by step.

### Gaps
- No public Rath text found giving a step-by-step varga-dasha method for D10, D9, D7 etc.; his "Narayana Dasa" (2001) and "Crux" books likely hold it but are not publicly readable.
- No source verified on how Rath weights a varga against D1 when a dasha promises a result in one but denies it in the other.
