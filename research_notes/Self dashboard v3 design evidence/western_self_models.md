# Western models of the self: domains, demand, product sectioning, forward framing, Barnum, safety limits

Notes for a "Self" dashboard card catalogue. Researched 2026-11-09. Searches ran this session are marked by their URLs.
Items marked **[BK]** come from the researcher's background knowledge of well-known primary sources and were NOT
re-fetched this session; the report writer should treat them as standard textbook facts that still need a link check
before publishing.

## 1. What domains do established models use?

### Takeaway
Every major Western model converges on roughly the same 8 to 12 domains: body (activity, sleep, nutrition), emotion
and mood, mind (intellectual), social relationships, purpose and meaning, mastery and achievement, autonomy, growth,
and context (environment, finances, work). Personality models (Big Five / HEXACO) add a separate "who you are" layer,
and self-concept research adds the "ideal self vs actual self" and "how others see you" layers.

### Cited Findings
- **WHO (1948 constitution):** health is "a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely
  the absence of disease or infirmity" — three domains: physical, mental, social. [BK] — [WHO Constitution](https://www.who.int/about/governance/constitution)
- **Biopsychosocial model (Engel 1977, *Science* 196:129-136):** illness and health come from biological,
  psychological and social factors acting together. [BK] — [Engel 1977, doi 10.1126/science.847460](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.847460)
- **Eight Dimensions of Wellness (Swarbrick 2006, adopted by SAMHSA):** emotional, environmental, financial,
  intellectual, occupational, physical, social, spiritual; presented as interconnected. Physical covers "physical
  activity, diet, sleep, and nutrition". SAMHSA's initiative was aimed at people with mental health and substance use
  disorders (quality of life, cardiovascular health, early mortality). — [SAMHSA guide sma16-4957](https://store.samhsa.gov:443/sites/default/files/sma16-4957.pdf); [Kent State 8 dimensions](https://www.kent.edu/recwell/8-dimensions-well-being); [SAMHSA 8 Dimensions PSA (archived)](https://cybercemetery.unt.edu/archive/oilspill/20121007163159/http:/www.promoteacceptance.samhsa.gov/10by10/PDF/PSA_8Dimensions.pdf)
- **Ryff's six dimensions of psychological well-being (1989; Ryff & Keyes 1995):** self-acceptance, positive
  relations with others, autonomy, environmental mastery, purpose in life, personal growth. Critique: some evidence
  the six are not sufficiently distinct after correcting for measurement error. — [Psychology Today summary](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/theory-knowledge/201405/six-domains-psychological-well-being); [MIDUS Ryff paper](https://midus.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/1334.pdf)
- **PERMA (Seligman 2011):** Positive emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning, Accomplishment. "V" (vitality:
  sleep, activity, nutrition) is a later practitioner addition, not part of Seligman's original five. — [Mappalicious on PERMA vs Ryff](https://mappalicious.com/tag/mastery/)
- **Self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan):** well-being follows from satisfying three basic needs: autonomy
  (volition), competence (efficacy), relatedness (connection). Mapping to Ryff is approximate (autonomy = autonomy,
  relatedness ≈ positive relations, competence ≈ environmental mastery). — [PMC article on SDT needs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/4241300)
- **Big Five / HEXACO:** Big Five = Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism (emotional
  stability). HEXACO adds Honesty-Humility as a sixth factor (Ashton & Lee). These are the best-validated trait
  models; type-based tools (MBTI-style) have weaker psychometric support. [BK] — [HEXACO official site](https://hexaco.org/)
- **Self-discrepancy theory (Higgins 1987):** actual self vs ideal self (gap linked to dejection/sadness) vs ought
  self (gap linked to agitation/anxiety). Supports an "ideal self / who you want to become" card distinct from
  "who you are now". [BK] — [Higgins 1987, Psych Review, doi 10.1037/0033-295X.94.3.319](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.94.3.319)
- **Reflected appraisal / Johari window:** Cooley's "looking-glass self" (1902) and Luft & Ingham's Johari window
  (1955: open, blind, hidden, unknown areas) frame "how others see you" as a separate domain. [BK] (Johari is a
  heuristic, not a validated measure.)
- **Self-Other Knowledge Asymmetry (SOKA, Vazire 2010, JPSP):** the self knows low-observability traits better
  (anxiety, sadness, thoughts, neuroticism); close others know highly evaluative traits better (intelligence,
  attractiveness, funny, selfish); for observable neutral traits (talkative) self and close others are about equally
  accurate. — [WashU news release](https://source.washu.edu/2010/03/others-may-know-us-better-than-we-know-ourselves-study-finds/); [APS Observer](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/observer/knowing-others-and-being-known)

### Inferences
- A defensible card catalogue maps cleanly onto these models:
  - Body and fitness, Energy, Sleep → WHO physical, SAMHSA physical, PERMA-V vitality.
  - Mental sharpness / Growth and learning → SAMHSA intellectual, Ryff personal growth, Big Five openness.
  - Mental health and mood → WHO mental, SAMHSA emotional, PERMA positive emotion.
  - What bothers you / stress triggers and What restores you / peace → SAMHSA emotional + spiritual; coping literature.
  - Play and entertainment → PERMA engagement (flow); not a named domain in most models (it is the weakest-grounded card).
  - Drive and ambition → PERMA accomplishment, SDT competence, Ryff environmental mastery/purpose, SAMHSA occupational.
  - Personality and character → Big Five / HEXACO (Honesty-Humility gives a real "character" axis).
  - How others see you → reflected appraisal, Johari, SOKA. SOKA suggests this card should focus on evaluative and
    observable traits, and that a self-reported answer is a weak source here.
- Commonly present in the models but missing from the proposed card list: relationships/social, meaning/purpose,
  autonomy, finances, environment/home. Purpose/meaning appears in Ryff, PERMA and SAMHSA (spiritual) and is the most
  conspicuous gap.

### Gaps
- Did not re-fetch WHO, Engel, Higgins, HEXACO primary texts this session.
- No formal source found that ranks which model is "best" for a consumer taxonomy; the mapping above is my synthesis.

## 2. Which self domains do people most seek help with?

### Takeaway
Physical health/exercise, happiness/mood, eating and money dominate resolutions; sleep, stress/anxiety and mindfulness
dominate wellness-app demand. "Better health" is the top wellness dimension in every market McKinsey surveyed.

### Cited Findings
- **YouGov US, 2026 resolutions** (n=1,104, Dec 9-11 2025, MoE ~4 pts): exercising more 25%, being happy 23%,
  eating healthier 22%, saving more money 21%, improving physical health 21% (top five of 25 options). 39% of
  planners say "very likely" to keep them, 50% "somewhat likely". — [YouGov 2026](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/53789-americans-new-years-resolutions-2026-poll)
- **YouGov US, 2025 resolutions** (Dec 9-11 2024): saving money 26%, physical health 22%, exercise 22%, being happy
  22%. Only 28% of Americans said they made a 2025 resolution; of those, 19% said they stuck to them entirely. — [YouGov 2025](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/51144-what-are-americans-new-years-resolutions-for-2025)
- **YouGov global 2025:** health, wealth and family time top priorities. — [YouGov business](https://business.yougov.com/content/51276-2025-new-year-resolutions-health-wealth-and-family-time-top-global-priorities)
- **McKinsey Future of Wellness:** six dimensions (health, fitness, nutrition, appearance, sleep, mindfulness); "better
  health" is the most important dimension and highest spend in every market; 79% say wellness is important, 42% a top
  priority (earlier edition); 2025 edition surveyed 9,000+ consumers; consumers increasingly want products spanning
  several dimensions. — [McKinsey 2025](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/our-insights/future-of-wellness-trends-2025); [McKinsey $1.5T wellness market](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/our-insights/feeling-good-the-future-of-the-1-5-trillion-wellness-market)
- **Wellness apps:** sleep disturbance was a top reason to install Calm; Sleep Stories and meditations are the most
  popular components (self-selected subscriber sample). — [JMIR mHealth 2019 Calm survey](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2019/11/e15648)
- Mindfulness, mood tracking and relaxation were the most popular mental-health-app categories (BMC Public Health
  2024). Headspace and Calm accounted for 96% of daily active mental-health-app users (2023 study). Retention is weak
  across the category. — [JMIR Mental Health 2023](https://mental.jmir.org/2023/1/e43565/PDF); [BMC Public Health 2024 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/2037d8eb75d7425daf481e0e5efa2132)

### Inferences
- Ranked by demand, the strongest cards are: body/fitness, mood/happiness, sleep, stress, eating, money. Energy and
  focus ride on sleep/stress demand. Personality, how-others-see-you and play are curiosity/identity cards, not
  top "help me" cards.
- Low follow-through (19% fully kept) argues for small, dated plans rather than big goals (see §4).

### Gaps
- No Pew data on resolutions found. No reliable survey ranking "focus/mental sharpness" or "play" as help-seeking
  categories. Self-improvement market segment sizes (Statista etc.) not verified this session.

## 3. How do leading consumer products section the self?

### Takeaway
Wearables section the body into a few composite scores (readiness/recovery, sleep, activity/strain, stress/resilience);
mind apps section by need state (sleep, stress, focus, anxiety); personality products section by traits or strengths.
None of them merges these into one "self" view, which is the gap a Self dashboard occupies.

### Cited Findings
- **Oura:** Readiness Score 0-100 from seven daily contributors spanning sleep, activity and body stress (HRV,
  resting HR, body temperature, respiratory rate, plus Sleep Balance, Activity Balance, HRV Balance over ~2 weeks).
  85+ optimal, 70-84 good, <70 "pay attention". Readiness also feeds a Resilience level. Top-level areas: Readiness,
  Sleep, Activity (plus Stress/Resilience). — [Oura readiness](https://ouraring.com/blog/readiness-score/)
- **Whoop:** three scores, Strain (0-21), Sleep, Recovery (0-100%); Recovery and Oura Readiness both lean heavily on
  overnight HRV and HR. — [Rouvy comparison](https://rouvy.com/en/blog/whoop-or-oura) (third-party blog). Whoop also
  ships a Stress Monitor. [BK]
- **Apple Health:** browse categories include Activity, Body Measurements, Cycle Tracking, Hearing, Heart, Medications,
  Mental Wellbeing (State of Mind logging), Mobility, Nutrition, Respiratory, Sleep, Symptoms, Vitals. [BK, not
  verified this session] — [Apple Health](https://www.apple.com/health/)
- **Headspace / Calm:** content organised by need: sleep, stress, anxiety, focus, movement. **Finch:** self-care pet
  app with mood check-ins, goals, journaling. [BK]
- **16Personalities:** MBTI-like four letters plus an "Assertive/Turbulent" identity axis; sections Strengths &
  Weaknesses, Romantic Relationships, Friendships, Parenthood, Career Paths, Workplace Habits. **CliftonStrengths
  (Gallup):** 34 themes in four domains: Executing, Influencing, Relationship Building, Strategic Thinking. [BK] —
  [16Personalities](https://www.16personalities.com/); [CliftonStrengths domains](https://www.gallup.com/cliftonstrengths/en/253736/cliftonstrengths-domains.aspx)

### Inferences
- The "Energy" card matches the industry pattern of a single readiness/recovery composite; the "What bothers you /
  restores you" pair matches Oura's stress vs resilience split.
- Personality products present strengths before weaknesses and section by life arena (love, work, friends) — a pattern
  the "Personality and character" and "How others see you" cards can follow.

### Gaps
- Apple Health, Whoop, Headspace, Calm, Finch, 16Personalities and CliftonStrengths category lists were not fetched
  this session; confirm against current product docs. No independent validation of Oura/Whoop scores reviewed.

## 4. Evidence for forward-looking coping and development framing (over prediction)

### Takeaway
Planning tools (if-then plans, WOOP) have reliable but smaller-than-advertised effects (d ≈ 0.3, lower after bias
correction); temporal landmarks raise motivation to start; growth-mindset interventions are weak. Frame cards as
"plan for the next window" with concrete if-then steps, not as forecasts.

### Cited Findings
- **Implementation intentions (if-then plans):** Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, 94 tests, d = 0.65. 2024 update (Sheeran,
  Listrom & Gollwitzer), 642 tests: d = 0.36 overall (95% CI 0.33-0.40), d = 0.27 for behaviour; bias-corrected
  d = 0.35 (trim-and-fill) to d = 0.15 (robust Bayesian). Substance-use meta-analysis 2020: g = 0.31. **Status:**
  robust direction, effect shrinks with more studies; quote d ≈ 0.27 for behaviour, not 0.65. — [Gollwitzer summary (Konstanz)](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/2e749bfb-8533-437c-8203-7e788c910c5f); [secondary summary of 2024 update](https://goalsandprogress.com/implementation-intentions-gollwitzer-how-to/) (2024 figures taken from secondary source; verify in the primary paper)
- **Mental contrasting with implementation intentions (MCII / WOOP):** Wang, Wang & Gai 2021 meta-analysis, 21 studies,
  24 effects, N = 15,907, g = 0.336; publication bias means true effect may be smaller; moderated by intervention
  style. Cross & Sheffield 2019 (health behaviour): g = 0.28 (95% CI 0.14-0.42). — [Frontiers in Psychology 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202/full)
- **Fresh-start effect (Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014, *Management Science* 60(10):2563-2582):** temporal landmarks (new
  week, month, year, semester, birthdays, holidays) open new "mental accounting periods" that relegate past failures;
  Google searches for "diet", gym visits and goal commitments rise after landmarks; a third study used non-health
  goals. Field/archival data, i.e. correlational. A later finding (Dai 2018) that resets can demotivate people on a
  winning streak was seen only in a secondary source. — [Dai et al. 2014 PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Dai_Fresh_Start_2014_Mgmt_Sci.pdf); [CHIBE summary](https://chibe.upenn.edu/news/the-fresh-start-effect/)
- **Growth mindset:** Sisk et al. 2018 (*Psychological Science*): mindset-achievement k = 273, N = 365,915; interventions
  k = 43, N = 57,155; both effects weak; reported that 37/43 intervention effects were not significantly different from
  zero. Macnamara & Burgoyne 2022 (*Psych Bulletin*): positive results "rare and possibly spurious". Burnette et al.
  2022 found a positive effect. **Status: contested; do not rely on mindset messaging.** — [Sisk et al. 2018 PDF](https://englelab.gatech.edu/articles/2018/Sisk,%20Burgoyne%20et%20al.%20(2018)%20-%20Mindset%20and%20Academic%20Achievement.pdf); [Macnamara & Burgoyne 2022 PDF](https://englelab.gatech.edu/articles/2022/Macnamara%20and%20Burgoyne%20(2022)%20-%20Do%20Growth%20Mindset%20Interventions%20Impact%20Students%E2%80%99%20Academic%20Achievement.pdf)
- **Stress inoculation training (Meichenbaum):** three phases, conceptualisation, skill acquisition/rehearsal,
  application/follow-through; Saunders et al. 1996 meta-analysis found it reduced performance anxiety and state
  anxiety. **Coping planning** (Sniehotta et al. 2005): planning how to handle anticipated obstacles adds to action
  planning for exercise maintenance. [BK, effect sizes not re-verified]

### Inferences
- The strongest evidence-backed card shape is: name the coming window (week/month/birthday = fresh start), name one
  wish, the likely obstacle (from the user's known triggers), and an if-then plan. That is WOOP + fresh-start in one.
- Goal horizons of 3/6/12 months: I found no study establishing those specific horizons as optimal; the evidence
  supports dated, landmark-anchored windows generally.
- Coping framing ("when this pressure comes, do X") is supported by SIT and coping planning; prediction framing has no
  supporting evidence in this literature.

### Gaps
- Primary 2024 Sheeran/Listrom/Gollwitzer paper not opened. No evidence found for optimal 3/6/12-month horizons.
  Dai 2018 "reset demotivates winners" unverified.

## 5. Barnum / Forer effect and how specificity plus confirmation counters it

### Takeaway
People rate vague, positive, "personalised for you" descriptions as highly accurate (Forer 1949). Acceptance rises
with favourability, perceived personalisation and evaluator authority, so a product's sense of "it knows me" is not
evidence of accuracy. Specific, falsifiable statements that the user can confirm or reject, scored per statement,
are the main counter.

### Cited Findings
- Forer (1949) gave students the same generic profile; mean accuracy rating ~4.3/5. [BK] — [Barnum effect (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnum_effect)
- Dickson & Kelly 1985 review: acceptance depends on relevance and favourability of the profile, and partly on the
  assessment type. — [Dickson & Kelly 1985 review PDF](https://cortecs.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/the-barnum-effect-in-personality-assessment-a-review-of-the-literature.pdf)
- Acceptance is higher when subjects believe the analysis is personalised, trust the evaluator's authority, and the
  profile is mostly positive; Snyder & Larson 1972 showed labelling a generic reading "for you" raises acceptance.
  — [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnum_effect); [Dickson & Kelly 1985](https://cortecs.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/the-barnum-effect-in-personality-assessment-a-review-of-the-literature.pdf)
- 2025 small study (~60 participants): skepticism, openness and paranormal belief together explained only ~11% of
  variance in Barnum ratings (individual differences matter little). — [IJIP 2025](https://ijip.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/18.01.337.20251303.pdf)
- Measurement: a 2014 Big-Five-feedback study recommends rating component statements, not only the whole description,
  because overall acceptance hides which items are generic. — [Psichologija (Vilnius) 2014](https://www.journals.vu.lt/psichologija/article/view/4893)
- Astrology-specific: Carlson 1985 (*Nature*) double-blind test found participants could not pick their own
  astrological profile above chance. [BK]

### Inferences
- Counter-measures for the product: (1) statements must be specific and falsifiable (a named trait, a named situation,
  a date window); (2) the user confirms or rejects each statement, and rejections change later cards; (3) avoid
  mostly-flattering profiles: include a stated weakness or cost; (4) record per-statement agree rates and compare with
  a generic baseline profile to measure Barnum share. This fits the repo's "confirm before it predicts" rule.
- High user satisfaction ratings cannot be used as validity evidence for cards.

### Gaps
- No formal meta-analysis of the Barnum effect found. No study found that directly tests "confirm-each-statement" UX
  as a Barnum counter; the recommendation is inferred from the measurement literature.

## 6. Safety limits: what consumer apps must not claim

### Takeaway
In the US, a product stays outside FDA device regulation only if it is low-risk and claims only general wellness
(sustaining healthy lifestyle, stress management, sleep, fitness), not diagnosis, treatment, cure or mitigation of a
disease or condition (including anxiety disorders, depression, insomnia as a disorder). The FTC polices
deceptive health claims and, more actively, health-data sharing.

### Cited Findings
- FDA "General Wellness: Policy for Low Risk Devices" revised final guidance issued 2026-01-06 (replaces 2019). Core
  policy unchanged: no device regulation for products intended solely for general wellness that are low risk.
  New: some noninvasive products estimating physiologic parameters (e.g. blood pressure) can qualify; invasive
  products (pierce skin/mucosa) excluded. Feb 11 2026 CDRH town hall: a product may use wellness claims yet fail the
  policy if its technology is not low risk. — [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/insights/articles/fda-updates-general-wellness-and-clinical-decision-support-guidance-documents); [Faegre Drinker](https://faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/1/key-updates-in-fdas-2026-general-wellness-and-clinical-decision-support-software-guidance); [Troutman 2026](https://www.troutman.com/insights/fdas-2026-guidance-on-general-wellness-devices-policy-for-low-risk-devices/); primary text at [FDA guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/90652/download) (not opened)
- General wellness claims fall into two categories: (1) maintaining or encouraging a general state of health
  (weight, fitness, sleep, relaxation/stress management, mental acuity, self-esteem) with no disease reference; (2)
  healthy lifestyle choices that may help reduce risk of or live well with chronic diseases where that link is well
  accepted. Claims to treat or diagnose a specific disease, e.g. "treats anxiety disorder", make it a device. [BK from
  the 2016/2019 guidance text; check 2026 wording]
- FTC enforcement: BetterHelp (2023, $7.8M) for deceptive privacy promises and sharing health data with advertisers,
  including a misleading HIPAA seal; the FTC treated even email addresses as health information because they revealed
  people sought mental health help. Cerebral (2024, $7M) for data sharing and deceptive cancellation. Exposure also
  under the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule. — [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/ftc-orders-virtual-mental-health-platform-to-pay-7-8-million-to-resolve-data-disclosure-allegations/); [WSGR on Cerebral/Monument](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/ftc-announces-proposed-settlement-agreements-with-two-digital-health-companies-for-disclosing-consumers-health-information-to-third-party-advertisers-among-other-violations.html)
- FTC "Health Products Compliance Guidance" (Dec 2022) requires competent and reliable scientific evidence for health
  benefit claims, generally randomized controlled trials. [BK] — [FTC guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance)

### Inferences
- Card and copy rules: use "mood", "stress", "rest", "energy", "focus" in a wellness sense; never name or imply a
  disorder (depression, anxiety disorder, insomnia, ADHD), never claim to diagnose, treat, or predict a health event;
  no efficacy claims ("reduces anxiety by X%") without trial evidence.
- Standard disclaimer pattern seen across wellness apps: "for general wellness and self-reflection; not medical
  advice, diagnosis or treatment; if you are in crisis call or text 988 (US) or local emergency services." A mood
  card should carry a crisis route (988 in US). [BK]
- Mood and stress data counts as health information for FTC purposes: do not share with advertisers; privacy promises
  must match practice.
- Astrology-derived claims about health would sit outside any evidence base, which raises FTC substantiation risk if
  framed as health benefit.

### Gaps
- FDA 2026 guidance text not opened directly; summary from law-firm commentary. Non-US regimes (EU MDR, UK MHRA
  software-as-medical-device rules, India CDSCO) not covered. Current FTC policy direction under the present
  administration not verified beyond 2024 actions.
