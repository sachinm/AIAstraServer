# Build Self around periods, not luck

The evidence supports most of the proposed Self v3 direction, with one important correction. A **user-built card dashboard only works as "smart default plus edit"**: most people never change defaults, so the eight cards the assistant picks from the person's chart *are* the product, and the "+" tab, show/hide sections and reordering are secondary tools for a minority who care. Removing the crammed summary strip in favour of calm, wide, text-capped carousel cards is well supported by Apple's layout guidance, reading research and carousel usability studies, provided the carousel uses labelled chips, a peek of the next card and a position count rather than bare dots. On framing, a dated "how you get through the next 3, 6 and 12 months, or build yourself if they are easy" board beats "find the lucky times": population data shows almost nobody uses astrology to drive decisions, while temporal landmarks and if-then planning have measurable (if modest) effects, and the category leader in Western astrology moved to month-by-month periods in 2026. The domain list holds up against Western wellbeing models and Indian frameworks, but it is missing relationships and purpose/meaning, "play" is its weakest card, and chakra-planet links have no classical Jyotish source. The lead segment is Indian and diaspora adults aged 25 to 40 in a hard marriage or career stretch. Health wording must stay in general-wellness language in both the US and India: no disease names, no diagnosis, no cure or guaranteed outcome from a remedy, and no longevity prediction. Several specifics, including the number 8, the 3/6/12-month horizons and many verse references, have no direct evidence and are flagged below.

## Defaults decide what most users see; editing serves a vocal minority

The strongest finding on customization is that **the generic experience must stand on its own**. Nielsen Norman Group's guidance is blunt: users often don't customize even when the tools are simple, tweaking is "only a secondary task, at best," and products must not leave people on an empty screen because they skipped setup ([NN/g: 7 Tips for Successful Customization](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/customization/)). NN/g also separates customization (user-configured) from personalization (system-configured) and finds that personalization suits people who "do not know exactly what they need," which describes most users of an astrology assistant facing cards named for dashas or arudhas ([NN/g: Customization vs. Personalization](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/customization-personalization/)). The often-quoted figure that **fewer than 5% of users changed any setting** comes from a 2011 Microsoft Word study with a self-selected sample, so it is directionally useful but old and desktop-only ([UIE](https://archive.uie.com/brainsparks/2011/09/14/do-users-change-their-settings/)).

That does not argue for a fixed layout. Every major wellness dashboard examined ships a designer-chosen default and an edit mode, and several added editing after complaints. WHOOP's "My Dashboard" gained a pencil, show/hide toggles and drag-to-reorder after community posts said Home could not be customized ([WHOOP](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/thelocker/the-all-new-whoop-home-screen/); [WHOOP community](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/can-i-customize-my-home-screen/570)). Apple Health uses Edit beside "Pinned" plus a pin star on each data page ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-ca/HT203037)); Apple Fitness has "Edit Summary" ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/see-your-activity-summary-iph4c34a8a95/ios)); Garmin's "Edit My Day" offers drag handles, hiding and **"Restore Dismissed Cards"** ([Garmin forums](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-mobile-ios/116591/choose-or-rearrange-the-my-day-widgets)); and Oura's shortcut row **keeps a minimum of three** ([Oura Support](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/360058599753-How-to-Use-the-Oura-App)). Samsung Health users complained in 2024 that the home was filled with promoted content they could not remove ([Samsung community](https://us.community.samsung.com:443/t5/Samsung-Apps-and-Services/Samsung-Health-Home-remove-Interests-and-allow-us-to-customize/m-p/2950901)). The shared pattern is: default set, one Edit entry point placed with the content, toggles, drag handles, Done. A "+" tab at the end of the carousel fits NN/g's advice to put customization next to the content it changes. No competitor astrology app was found with a user-customizable layout, so this would be a differentiator, not a category norm ([astrology landscape notes, CHANI App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1532791252)).

Psychology gives conditional support. The IKEA effect is real (people bid **$1.46 vs $0.59** for things they had finished building) but **vanishes when the build is not completed** ([Norton, Mochon & Ariely 2012](https://dash.harvard.edu/handle/1/12136084)), so a picker must end in a visibly finished dashboard. Self-determination theory backs offering autonomy, while warning that engagement is not the same as wellbeing ([Peters, Calvo & Ryan 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5985470)). Choice overload is contested: one meta-analysis of 50 experiments found a mean effect near zero ([Scheibehenne et al. 2010](https://scheibehenne.com/ScheibehenneGreifenederTodd2010.pdf)), another found it significant when sets are complex and preferences uncertain ([Chernev et al. 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057740814000916)). Both moderators apply here, which argues for a small, grouped catalogue with a plain one-line benefit per card. Pinterest's required interest picker (at least five topics, per secondary sources) is the best-documented "pick your interests" precedent ([Omega Point](https://omegapoint.systems/growth-studies/pinterest-interest-picker)); NN/g accepts a short setup question only if it has a Skip ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/customization/)).

For **show/hide sections inside a card**, progressive disclosure is the better-supported pattern: sensible default sections, "more" behind a disclosure, and a light per-card toggle for people who want it ([NN/g: Progressive Disclosure](https://nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)). The resulting design is a picker with eight cards pre-selected from the chart, one-tap accept, Skip that lands on the same default, a minimum card count, and "Reset to recommended." **No study tests eight as the right number**; it is plausible, not validated.

## A calm, wide carousel beats the summary strip if navigation is labelled

Apple's Human Interface Guidelines (updated September 2026) tell designers to "order content by relative importance," group with negative space, and use progressive disclosure because "an interface with too much content and too many choices makes it harder to find information quickly" ([Apple HIG: Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)). A dense top strip runs against all three, and against calm-technology principles of requiring "the smallest possible amount of attention" and informing without overwhelming ([calmtech.com](https://calmtech.com/)). Whitespace is not only aesthetic: margins around text **improved both reading speed and comprehension** in a four-layout comparison ([Chaparro et al. 2004](https://portfolio.erau.edu/en/publications/reading-online-text-a-comparison-of-four-white-space-layouts/)). The popular "whitespace raises comprehension 20%" figure has no traceable source and should not be repeated.

"Wide" needs a qualifier. Cards can be wide as containers, but text lines should be capped around **50 to 75 characters** (WCAG AAA allows up to 80) ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/line-length-readability)), which is what Apple's readable content guide enforces on wide screens ([Apple: readableContentGuide](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiview/readablecontentguide)). On an iPad that means two columns or text beside a visual inside one card, not full-bleed paragraphs. Studies on line length disagree (40 to 100+ characters), so this is a range, not a rule.

Carousels earn their bad reputation as auto-rotating banners: **46% of e-commerce homepage carousels have UX issues**, and many users miss dot indicators ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/homepage-carousel)). User-driven carousels work when they support swipe, show a **peek of the next item**, show position and count, give large arrows as backup, and use recognisable labels instead of dots ([NN/g: Designing Effective Carousels](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/designing-effective-carousels/)). NN/g advises **five or fewer frames** and reaching the last item in **3 to 4 swipes**, otherwise use a list ([NN/g: Mobile Carousels](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-carousels/)). Eight cards sits beyond that limit, so labelled chips that jump straight to a card, a "3 of 8" indicator and an overview grid are required, not optional. The strip's at-a-glance job moves into one headline line per card. Two caveats: **no tablet-specific carousel research exists** (guidance is extrapolated from phone and desktop), and chips versus dots has never been tested head-to-head.

## Self should span body to purpose, using Indian lenses as tradition, not diagnosis

### Western models confirm most proposed cards and expose two gaps

Every mainstream model converges on similar domains. The WHO definition splits health into physical, mental and social ([WHO Constitution](https://www.who.int/about/governance/constitution)); SAMHSA's eight dimensions add emotional, environmental, financial, intellectual, occupational and spiritual, with physical covering "activity, diet, sleep, and nutrition" ([SAMHSA](https://store.samhsa.gov:443/sites/default/files/sma16-4957.pdf)); Ryff's psychological wellbeing names self-acceptance, positive relations, autonomy, environmental mastery, purpose and growth ([MIDUS](https://midus.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/1334.pdf)); and self-determination theory reduces it to autonomy, competence and relatedness ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/4241300)). Mapping the proposed cards: body, fitness, energy and sleep sit on physical/vitality; mental sharpness on intellectual and growth; mood on emotional; drive on accomplishment, competence and mastery; personality on the Big Five and HEXACO, whose Honesty-Humility factor gives a genuine "character" axis ([HEXACO](https://hexaco.org/)). **"Play" is the weakest-grounded card**, a partial fit to PERMA's engagement and not a named domain in most models. **Relationships and purpose/meaning are conspicuously missing** from the proposed list even though they appear in Ryff, PERMA and SAMHSA; purpose is in the request, but relationships are not.

"How others see you" has better backing than expected. Vazire's self-other knowledge asymmetry finds the self knows inner traits (anxiety, sadness) better, while close others know evaluative ones (intelligence, attractiveness, selfishness) better ([WashU](https://source.washu.edu/2010/03/others-may-know-us-better-than-we-know-ourselves-study-finds/)). That card should therefore focus on observable and evaluative traits and treat the user's self-report as weak evidence. Self-discrepancy theory supports a separate "who you want to become" card distinct from "who you are now" ([Higgins 1987](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.94.3.319)).

Demand ranks these cards unevenly. The top 2026 US resolutions were exercising more (25%), being happy (23%), eating healthier (22%), saving money (21%) and physical health (21%) ([YouGov 2026](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/53789-americans-new-years-resolutions-2026-poll)); "better health" is the most important wellness dimension in every market McKinsey surveyed ([McKinsey 2025](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/our-insights/future-of-wellness-trends-2025)); sleep was a top reason to install Calm ([JMIR mHealth 2019](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2019/11/e15648)). Body, mood, sleep and stress are "help me" cards; personality, image and play are curiosity cards. Wearables show the industry pattern for energy: Oura folds seven contributors into one 0-100 Readiness score with a separate stress and resilience view ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/blog/readiness-score/)), which maps neatly onto a "what bothers you / what restores you" pair.

### Indian frameworks supply native structure, with uneven evidence

**Panchakosha** (body, breath/energy, mind, intellect, bliss, from Taittiriya Upanishad 2.1-5) gives the cleanest native "layers of you" scaffold ([Wikipedia: Kosha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosha)) and is mainstream enough that NCERT uses "Panchakosha Vikas" in child-development policy ([NCERT](https://ejournals.ncert.gov.in/index.php/tpt/article/download/4998/4756/9216)). The **purusharthas** (dharma, artha, kama, moksha) map onto Work, Love and inner-peace boards. **Prakriti** is the most personal-feeling frame and has some genomic support (a 2015 study grouped 262 high-dominance individuals into Vata/Pitta/Kapha by SNPs "irrespective of their ancestry") ([PMC4625161](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4625161/)), but reviews call this evidence "preliminary," small and partly proponent-authored ([PMC3149388](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3149388)). It needs a questionnaire; a chart can only hint. Gunas, sadhana and dinacharya fit routine and state-of-mind cards, but their definitions were not source-checked in this pass.

On classical Jyotish, the **dhatu card is the best-sourced health-adjacent card**: BPHS 3.31 assigns Sun to bone, **Moon to blood (rakta)**, Mars to marrow, Mercury to skin, Jupiter to fat, Venus to semen and Saturn to sinew ([BPHS 3.31](https://enjoylearningsanskrit.com/scriptures/parashara/chapter-3/verse-31)). Some later schemes give blood to Mars, so the blood card must cite which tradition it follows ([bharatiyajyotish](https://bharatiyajyotishmantrasaadhana.blogspot.com/2025/09/the-seven-dhatus-and-their-planetary.html?m=1)). Planet-to-dosha links found online could not be tied to verse numbers ([dekhopanchang](https://dekhopanchang.com/en/learn/ayurveda-jyotish)). **Chakra-to-planet mappings have no classical Jyotish source**; popular versions contradict each other and trace to Western esoteric writing and modern synthesis ([Karmastro](https://karmastro-site.pages.dev/en/blog/chakras-astrology-7-planets-energy-centers-correspondence); [Wikisource](https://kwx.liehendr.com/content/wikisource_en_all_maxi_2022-09/A/Page%3AA_Collection_of_Esoteric_Writings.djvu/268)). A chakra card must be dropped or labelled "modern association." **Ishta devata** is a strong, culturally native card derived via Jaimini (12th from karakamsha in the navamsa), but tie-break orders and even the meaning of karakamsha are disputed, so the engine must state the rule it used ([vedicmarga](https://vedicmarga.com/ishta-devata-calculator/); [sutramritam](https://sutramritam.blogspot.com/2008/12/krishna-mishra-navamsa-karakamsa-not.html)). Remedies are the biggest commercial pull (Astrotalk's store earned **Rs 140 crore in 2025** across 1.6 million orders) ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/news/astrotalks-e-commerce-vertical-posts-rs-140-cr-revenue-in-2025-hits-rs-200-cr-arr-11004745)), but they carry the most legal and trust risk, covered below.

### Vimshottari leads for everyone; conditional dashas only cross-check

Vimshottari is the default for every chart. BPHS gates each conditional nakshatra dasha on a chart condition: Shatabdika when the lagna is vargottama, Chaturashiti-sama when the 10th lord is in the 10th, Dwisaptati-sama when the lagna lord is in the 1st or 7th, Panchottari when both lagna and D12 lagna are Cancer (about one chart in 144) ([Saravali: Special Nakshatra Dasas](https://saravali.github.io/astrology/dasa_nakshatra.html)). Conditions for Ashtottari, Shodashottari and Dwadashottari **differ between translators** ([Pijan quoting Santhanam](https://www.barbarapijan.com/bpa/VimshottariDasha/1Vimshottari_dasha_BPHS_dashaSystems47.htm); [Satyori](https://satyori.com/jyotish/dasha/shodashottari/)), and one Dwadashottari reading collides with Shatabdika's condition. The same Saravali source says conditional dashas "should only be used in combination with" Vimshottari, and PVR Narasimha Rao reportedly calls Parashara's conditions necessary but not sufficient (seen only via a search snippet of [his paper](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/pp_cond_dasas.pdf)). The product rule follows: Vimshottari as the headline timing, qualifying conditional dashas shown only as "also applies to you."

**Narayana dasa** starts from the stronger of the lagna or 7th ([srath.com](https://srath.com/page/63/)); the common teaching that it reads events and environment per varga was **not verified**. **Tithi Pravesha**, cast yearly when the Moon-Sun angle returns to its birth value, is judged like a natal chart for one year ([PVR: pp_tp.pdf](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/pp_tp.pdf), via snippet), and is a more native "your year" card than a solar return. Which dasha runs inside it (Tithi Ashtottari, per a Rao/Rath transcript) rests on secondary Scribd uploads ([Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/347971271/Tithi-Pravesh-5)).

## Coping periods beat lucky times, and dated windows make them actionable

The best population data undercuts a "find the lucky times" lead. In Pew's 2024 survey, **20% of US adults use astrology mostly for fun and 10% for insight, but only 1% rely on it "a lot" for major decisions** and 5% "a little" ([Pew 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/)). Secondary reports of the psychology literature link turning to astrology with stress and uncertainty, and suggest personal spiritual practice may support a sense of control rather than fatalism ([The Conversation via Inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/how-paranormal-beliefs-help-people-cope-in-uncertain-times); [Discover](https://www.discovermagazine.com/why-are-people-so-into-astrology-all-of-a-sudden-42786)). The job users hire the product for is getting through an uncertain stretch with some control. The steelman for lucky times is real but narrower: Indian use (muhurta, marriage matching) is plausibly more decision-oriented, though **no India-specific motive survey was found**, and the remedy-and-timing segment is exactly where fear-based upselling concentrates.

Planning research gives the "get through or build yourself" framing an evidence base that prediction lacks. If-then implementation intentions work, but less than the famous figure suggests: the 2006 meta-analysis reported d = 0.65, while a 2024 update across 642 tests gives **d = 0.27 for behaviour**, and as low as 0.15 after bias correction (2024 figures from a [secondary summary](https://goalsandprogress.com/implementation-intentions-gollwitzer-how-to/); [Gollwitzer, Konstanz](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/2e749bfb-8533-437c-8203-7e788c910c5f)). Mental contrasting with implementation intentions (WOOP) shows **g = 0.34 across 21 studies**, again with likely publication bias ([Wang et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202/full)). Growth-mindset messaging is contested and should not be relied on ([Sisk et al. 2018](https://englelab.gatech.edu/articles/2018/Sisk,%20Burgoyne%20et%20al.%20(2018)%20-%20Mindset%20and%20Academic%20Achievement.pdf); [Macnamara & Burgoyne 2022](https://englelab.gatech.edu/articles/2022/Macnamara%20and%20Burgoyne%20(2022)%20-%20Do%20Growth%20Mindset%20Interventions%20Impact%20Students%E2%80%99%20Academic%20Achievement.pdf)). Stress inoculation and coping planning support "when this pressure comes, do X," though those effect sizes were not re-verified.

Date-period-led guidance rests on the **fresh-start effect**: searches for "diet," gym visits and goal commitments rise after temporal landmarks such as a new week, month, year or birthday ([Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Dai_Fresh_Start_2014_Mgmt_Sci.pdf)), and framing a reminder date as a fresh start made people choose it **3.5 times as often** (26% vs 7%) ([APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/?p=118615)). The 2014 data are correlational, and a claim that resets demotivate people already on a streak is unverified. Products have converged on periods: Whoop's monthly assessment looks back six months ([Whoop](https://www.whoop.com/thelocker/monthly-performance-assessment)), and CHANI's 2026 update replaced quarterly summaries with **month-by-month breakdowns and four life-area annual horoscopes** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id1532791252)). A dasha change, a Tithi Pravesha or a birthday becomes a personal landmark: "now to 15 Feb," "this eases from 12 Mar." **No study establishes 3, 6 and 12 months as optimal horizons**; the evidence supports dated, landmark-anchored windows in general, so those three are a defensible product choice, not a finding.

Because a dated, specific reading feels personal, it is also prone to the Barnum effect: people rate vague, flattering, "for you" profiles as highly accurate, and acceptance rises with favourability and perceived personalization ([Dickson & Kelly 1985](https://cortecs.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/the-barnum-effect-in-personality-assessment-a-review-of-the-literature.pdf)). The counter is specific, falsifiable statements confirmed or rejected one by one, which a 2014 measurement study recommends over rating whole descriptions ([Psichologija 2014](https://www.journals.vu.lt/psichologija/article/view/4893)). That matches the product's "confirm before it predicts" rule. **No study directly tests confirm-each-statement UX** as a Barnum counter, and user satisfaction cannot stand in for validity.

## Indian and diaspora adults in a hard stretch are the lead segment

India's paying market is people asking a specific question of a person. Astrotalk, the leader, grew operating revenue from **Rs 283 crore (FY23) to Rs 1,176 crore (FY25)**; its core buyers are 25 to 35, **60% are women**, and **marriage questions bring in about 65% of revenue** ([Inc42, Aug 2026](https://inc42.com/features/can-astrotalk-chart-a-future-beyond-astrology/)). International revenue was about 20% of operating revenue in FY25 and, by an unverified CEO claim, 37% in FY26, with the US above 60% of that ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/can-astrotalk-chart-a-future-beyond-astrology/)); InstaAstro reports about 25% international, mostly diaspora ([Open Magazine](https://openthemagazine.com/business/kundli-meets-code-instaastros-12-million-bet-on-ai-reading-the-customer)). InstaAstro frames the need as reassurance during recurring uncertainty with private, any-hour access "without social hesitation," which is the always-available assistant's pitch. In the US, **46% of women aged 18 to 49 and 54% of LGBT adults** consult astrology yearly, mostly for fun or insight ([Pew 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/)), and pay $11.99 to $14.99 a month for calm, ritual-rich content ([CHANI](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1532791252); [Sanctuary](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1417411962)).

Reviewers' complaints mark what to avoid: Co-Star's "ominous and too blunt" tone ([Study Breaks](https://studybreaks.com/tvfilm/co-star-easy-and-astonishing-astrology-app)), countdown timers on "free chat" ([Mint via Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/tr/stories/newspaper/Mint-Mumbai/WHEN-ASTROLOGERS-FAIL-TO-SEE-THEIR-OWN-FUTURE)), and documented manipulative retention tactics in AI companions. Expected privacy controls follow the ChatGPT norm: memory the user can see, edit, delete and switch off, with training opt-out separate ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/memory-and-new-controls-for-chatgpt/)). Market-size reports are unusable: Astrotalk's own revenue already exceeds some whole-market estimates ([ReportCubes](https://www.thereportcubes.com/report-store/astrology-app-market-india)).

## Health wording stops at general wellness in both markets

In the US, the FDA's revised General Wellness guidance (issued 6 January 2026) keeps the core rule: low-risk products claiming only general wellness (fitness, sleep, stress management, mental acuity, self-esteem) are not regulated as devices, but claims to diagnose, treat or mitigate a disease, such as "treats anxiety disorder," are ([Faegre Drinker](https://faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/1/key-updates-in-fdas-2026-general-wellness-and-clinical-decision-support-software-guidance); [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/insights/articles/fda-updates-general-wellness-and-clinical-decision-support-guidance-documents)). The FTC requires competent and reliable scientific evidence, generally randomized trials, for health-benefit claims ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance)), and has enforced hard on health data: BetterHelp paid **$7.8 million** for sharing data with advertisers, with even email addresses treated as health information ([Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/ftc-orders-virtual-mental-health-platform-to-pay-7-8-million-to-resolve-data-disclosure-allegations/)), and Cerebral paid $7 million ([WSGR](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/ftc-announces-proposed-settlement-agreements-with-two-digital-health-companies-for-disclosing-consumers-health-information-to-third-party-advertisers-among-other-violations.html)). Mood, sleep and stress entries in a Self dashboard are health data for these purposes. Astrology-derived health claims have no evidence base, so any framing as a health benefit raises substantiation risk.

In India, the **Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954** bars advertising remedies "alleged to possess magic qualities" for listed diseases ([India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/indiacode/bitstream/123456789/1412/1/A1954-21.pdf)); ASCI referred **233 ads** to the AYUSH Ministry for potential violations in 2024-25 ([Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/industry/pharma/asci-flags-233-health-ads-for-dmr-act-violation-reports-to-ayush-ministry-149226)) and routinely strikes "guaranteed" outcome claims ([ASCI](https://www.ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/asci_nov_2019_ccc_release.pdf)). The working rules: describe constitutional tendencies ("tends to run warm or restless"), never name or imply a disorder, never present a gemstone, mantra or puja as treating a condition, never guarantee results, point health concerns to a doctor, and **keep longevity (ayurdaya) out entirely**. The dhatu and blood cards are safe only as "what the tradition associates with your chart." A mood card should carry a crisis route (988 in the US, local services elsewhere). Gaps here are material: the FDA 2026 text was read via law-firm summaries, and the Consumer Protection Act 2019, CCPA misleading-ads guidelines, the DPDP Act 2023, Maharashtra's and Karnataka's anti-superstition laws, and EU/UK device rules were not researched. No ASCI ruling specific to astrology or gemstones was found.

## Conclusion

The research reframes customization from a headline feature into an insurance policy. Because most people will never edit, the real design work is choosing the eight default cards from each person's chart and confirmed facts, which is personalization, and the "+" tab exists to keep power users from leaving. The same logic applies to framing: a dated period board is not just calmer than lucky times, it turns astrological periods into fresh-start landmarks attached to if-then plans, the one part of this space with measured behavioural effects, and the specific, confirmable statements it requires are also the best available defence against the Barnum effect that makes every astrology product feel accurate.

Two gaps should shape next steps. The domain list needs a relationships card and a clear purpose card, and should demote play and drop or relabel chakras. And the Indian legal picture beyond the 1954 Act, plus the unverified classical references (dosha verses, body maps, Narayana dasa use, Tithi Pravesha dasha), should be checked against primary translations and counsel before any health-adjacent or remedy card ships. A prototype test of the default-plus-edit picker with segment A users would settle the questions no source could, including whether eight is the right number.
