# Rath ranks by question, not by system

Sanjay Rath does not apply one fixed hierarchy of techniques. He fixes the chart first, then chooses every tool by the question: the divisional chart, the house, the reference point (lagna, Moon, arudha lagna, a special lagna or a karaka), and the dasha system that answers that kind of question. In his own text the birth time is rectified from family facts before any divisional chart is judged; each house is read through ten factors that end in the relevant varga; a nakshatra dasha such as Vimshottari pinpoints an event but "will require confirmation" from a rasi dasha such as Narayana; and Narayana shows what the environment brings while Vimshottari shows the mind's experience of it. Where he does rank, the ranking is local and purpose-bound: occupancy before dignity when comparing signs, argala as the factor that decides a house, the D7 over the D1 for children, Drig dasha before the D20 Narayana for spirituality, capacity before curse, diagnosis before remedy. His student P.V.R. Narasimha Rao prints the only explicit six-step analysis order found, and it is compatible with Rath's ten factors. Transits, annual charts and Sudarshana have no Rath doctrine on the open record, so any product rule built on them is not his. For this product the consequence is concrete: the Work board's present tone counts five voting families (four clock families plus transits) and the concordance layer counts agreeing clocks, while the structure assembled from Rath and his students is a gate (natal promise, then a rasi dasha confirming the nakshatra dasha, then the year, then finer periods), and his "confirm before predict" step is a sequence of discriminating questions about siblings, spouse, children and dated past events, not general check-ins. Several of his core tools, notably Narayana dasha run inside a varga and Vimshottari seeded from a stronger star, need a calculation-engine capability that does not exist today.

**How to read this report.** Claims are sorted into three tiers: **(A)** Rath's own text, a book by him or an srath.com page with his byline; **(B)** his students and SJC teachers, chiefly Rao's *Vedic Astrology: An Integrated Approach*, whose acknowledgements call Rath "my guru"; **(C)** third parties. Most srath.com pages and parts of *Crux of Vedic Astrology: Timing of Events* were read through a summarising fetch, so statements from them are paraphrase and page numbers are locators that need a print check. Rules about death, longevity, serious illness and curses are marked **[Sensitive]** and recorded as the source's rules only; none is for use without a product-owner decision. The detailed companion to this report, a per-topic doc set at `docs/deep-research/sanjay-rath/` (files 01-15 plus a README), is being written separately; this report stands on its own. The calculation backend is called "the calculation engine" throughout.

## The chart is verified from family facts before anything is judged

Rath's own book prints the opening order. Before the divisional charts are judged, the birth time is first rectified from the navamsa, the dwadasamsa and other key vargas, then fine-tuned with the vighati rule of the Jaimini sutras ([Crux, Ch. II sect. 8, p.34](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)). His worked Chart 7 shows the sequence in practice. **The rising sign is accepted from physique first**: the native's height, build and complexion fitted Aquarius. **Siblings come next**, because he calls verifying existing relatives the first step of chart correction: elder siblings from the 11th and younger from the 3rd counted from the arudha lagna, then two candidate drekkana (D3) lagnas tested against the fate of the first younger sibling, then the sex of each later sibling in order. **The spouse then fixes the navamsa lagna**, through the Upapada, the 8th lord and the D9 placement of Venus, Mercury, Mars and Ketu, with a character check ("earns by own skill") confirmed by the native. **Children fix the saptamsa lagna**: two D7 lagnas both gave a disabled first son, and only the sex of the second child separated them. A vighati count confirms the native's sex, and **Kunda fine-tuning comes last**, moving the lagna 40 minutes of arc to put the Kunda in a star trine to the natal Moon's. He closes by calling predictive value "the final test of a corrected chart" ([Crux, pp.34-39](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)). His consultation intake asks for the birth time from a hospital record or the mother, studies the name and any name changes, and makes "a detailed study of the past" before forecasting ([srath.com, The Process](https://srath.com/consultation/the-process/)).

Two features of this sequence matter more than its details. Each question he asks is a **discriminator**: it is asked because two candidate varga lagnas predict different answers, and each answer narrows the range before the next question. And the questions run from low-sensitivity, well-known facts (siblings) toward more personal ones (spouse, children), with the father's death appearing only as a forecast that later came true, not as a question put cold. Rao, his senior student, agrees that known events decide the time but rejects Kunda and Pranapada because they "fail the acid test of twins", and he warns that client-reported times are "seldom accurate" and that fine sub-periods should not be used confidently on an unrectified time ([Rao, ch. 32.1, pp.421-422](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)). Rao's sensitivity figures explain why the gate matters for a product: the D10 lagna changes sign roughly every 12 minutes and the D24 every 5, so every varga-level claim inherits the birth-time error ([Rao, ch. 32.2](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)). When no birth time exists at all, Rath used a prashna to choose between candidate lagnas and then tested the result against marriage age, the children's number and sex, and the husband's death year ([srath.com, Rani Rasmani Jataka](https://srath.com/celebrity/india/rani-rasmani-jataka/)).

After verification comes preparation. *Crux* tells the reader to cast the D1, D9, D3 and other relevant vargas with the arudha and upagraha charts, mark the special lagnas, especially Hora Lagna and Ghatika Lagna, and tabulate the chara karakas in the eight-planet scheme with Rahu counted from the end of its sign; it then lists ten factors to judge for any house: the house with its sign, occupants and aspects; the lord and its strength; the lord's dispositor; that dispositor's navamsa lord; the house's arudha; its fixed, temporary and natural karakas; the house counted from the arudha lagna; good and bad yogas; argala and virodhargala; and finally the saham and a detailed check of the relevant divisional chart, backed by a Saravali citation that astrology cannot proceed without vargas ([Crux, Ch. II sect. 7](https://archive.org/stream/sanjayrathcruxofvedicastrologytimingofevents19982/Sanjay%20Rath%20-%20Crux%20of%20Vedic%20Astrology-Timing%20of%20Events%20(1998)_2_djvu.txt)). No Rath text prints a start-to-finish script beyond this. The firmest whole-procedure order in the school is Rao's, and it runs from the question outward ([Rao, sect. 13.4.1, pp.169-171](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)):

| Step | Rao's order (B) | Where Rath's own rules sit (A) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Rectify before trusting fine periods | Rectification by D3, D9, D7 facts; Kunda last (*Crux* II.8) |
| 1 | Choose the divisional chart for the matter (D9 marriage, D10 career, D24 learning, D20 religion, D16 vehicles) | Varga is the tenth house factor; five harmonic cycles of vargas |
| 2 | Choose the house within it | Factors 1-4: house, lord, dispositor, dispositor's navamsa lord |
| 3 | Choose the reference point: lagna, arudha lagna, or a karaka when it is stronger | Lagna, AL and Varnada read separately; Sun and Atmakaraka argala on the lagna |
| 4 | Choose the house or its arudha (awards are "maya", so A5) | Factor 5: arudha of the house; factor 7: house from the AL |
| 5 | Judge influences: rasi and graha drishti, argala, houses counted from it, badhaka from it | Factors 8-9: yogas, then argala as the decider |
| 6 | Apply standard results, then strength, avasthas, ashtakavarga and yogas | Timing by house position and a dasha chosen for the question |

Panchanga is not a pre-reading step in either tier; Rao teaches it as calendar background and muhurta only ([Rao, sect. 1.3.8](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)).

## Precedence is set by purpose, and occupancy outranks dignity

**No lagna outranks another; each answers a different question.** Rao's map is the clearest statement of the school's reference points: the lagna for the true self, the Moon for the mind, the Sun for soul and vitality, the arudha lagna for perception and status, the paka lagna (the sign of the lagna lord) for the physical self, the Karakamsa for the inner self, Ghatika Lagna for power, Hora Lagna for wealth and Sree Lagna for prosperity ([Rao, sect. 7.3, pp.70-74](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)). The same planet changes meaning with the reference: in the 10th from the lagna it shows developments in the profession, from the Moon the mental side of the profession, from the arudha lagna professional status. Rao adds that perception usually matters more than reality in material life, listing elections, competitive exams and promotion as perception questions, while inner matters need the true self ([Rao, sect. 9.1](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)). Rath's own text agrees in pieces: the Sun and Atmakaraka are read from the arudha lagna for status and from the Varnada for community standing, and a planet linked to both the lagna and Hora Lagna becomes the wealth-giver (dhana yogada), to both the lagna and Ghatika Lagna the power-giver ([Crux, Ch. II sect. 2.2; Ch. IV sect. 4.1](https://archive.org/stream/sanjayrathcruxofvedicastrologytimingofevents19982/Sanjay%20Rath%20-%20Crux%20of%20Vedic%20Astrology-Timing%20of%20Events%20(1998)_2_djvu.txt)). The only stated tiebreak between references is strength: each house is read from the lagna and from its karaka, and the stronger weighs more, so the 3rd from Mars can outweigh the 3rd from the lagna ([Rao, sect. 7.3.9](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)). A lagna-versus-arudha disagreement is therefore not resolved by picking one; it is read as reality against appearance. For personality specifically, Rath gives one hard precedence: **the navamsa lagna's traits predominate over the rasi lagna's**, with planets in the lagna modifying both, and matters of the mind are read through the nakshatra lord while bodily matters go through the sign lord ([Crux, Ch. IV sect. 4.3-4.4](https://archive.org/stream/sanjayrathcruxofvedicastrologytimingofevents19982/Sanjay%20Rath%20-%20Crux%20of%20Vedic%20Astrology-Timing%20of%20Events%20(1998)_2_djvu.txt)).

**Aspects differ in kind; argala decides.** Rath assigns the two aspect systems different meanings rather than a ranking: graha drishti is desire and comparatively temporary, rasi drishti is knowledge and a permanent feature of the signs ([srath.com, Tattva & Dristi](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/amateur/tattva-dristi/)). Rao ranks them by weight of influence: rasi drishti a small influence, graha drishti a more concrete one, and argala the "bolt" that decides parts of the matter ([Rao, sect. 10.4-10.5](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)). Rath's argala rules are the most precisely ordered contest in his public writing. Primary argala comes from the 2nd, 4th and 11th, obstructed from the 12th, 10th and 3rd; secondary argala from the 5th and 8th, obstructed from the 9th and 6th; malefics in the 3rd have argala that nothing obstructs; counting reverses when Ketu is involved. **The side with more planets wins; if tied, own sign, a benefic sign or a planetary aspect decides; an exact tie produces bandhana (bondage)** ([srath.com, Argala: Planetary Intervention](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/amateur/argala-planetary-intervention/)). This is why argala appears as the test in so many of his steps: the Sun's and Atmakaraka's unobstructed argala on the lagna gives status and health, and in a varga, argala on each significator decides which of a person's roles thrives as the dasha changes ([srath.com, Principles of Divisional Charts](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/principles-of-divisional-charts/)).

**Signs are compared by occupancy first.** Rath credits his sign-strength order to the Jaimini Upadesa Sutras and says sources differ on it; his own step list was not retrieved ([srath.com, Narayana Dasha](https://srath.com/misc/naraya%e1%b9%87a-dasa/); [srath.com, Mula Dasa](https://srath.com/?p=6254)). Rao prints it, each rule applied only if the one before ties: more planets in the sign; more of Jupiter, Mercury and the sign lord joining or rasi-aspecting it; an exalted occupant; a lord placed in a sign of different parity; natural strength (dual over fixed over movable); and the lord further advanced in its sign ([Rao, sect. 15.5.2, pp.203-205](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)). Co-lords of Scorpio and Aquarius, and the Rudra comparison, use their own variants with the same occupancy-first signature. This reverses ordinary dignity-first reasoning, and it is the Jaimini fingerprint of the school. Shadbala has a narrow job: when several planets join in a yoga, the shadbala-stronger one delivers it; ashtakavarga measures whether a planet can deliver in harmony with the rest of the chart ([Rao, sect. 15.1-15.4](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)).

**The three karaka classes must not be mixed.** Naisargika karakas give general results and are counted from (the 5th from Jupiter for children, the 7th from Venus for marriage); chara karakas, eight including Rahu, stand for the people who shape one's sustenance, raja yoga and spiritual life, so the Darakaraka is the spouse itself; sthira karakas time relatives' deaths only **[Sensitive]** ([Rao, sect. 8.1-8.4](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)). One tension is unresolved: *Crux* uses Jupiter as the spouse significator in a woman's chart in a worked example, while Rao reads the 7th from Venus for both sexes and keeps Jupiter for spouse-death timing.

**Varga against D1 has two stated outcomes and no general rule.** Rath makes the relevant varga the confirming, final step of every house; he says the D7 has the final say on children, pronounced only after a capacity check, and that raja yoga must be confirmed in the D5 and D10 ([Crux, Ch. VIII sect. 8.5.2, 8.5.5; Ch. XIII](https://archive.org/stream/sanjayrathcruxofvedicastrologytimingofevents19982/Sanjay%20Rath%20-%20Crux%20of%20Vedic%20Astrology-Timing%20of%20Events%20(1998)_2_djvu.txt)). Rao states the dasha principle that ties them together, that a planet gives in its period what its positions across the divisional charts promise, and keeps the rasi chart supreme for physical matters such as death **[Sensitive]** ([Rao, sect. 16.5.1; 14.2](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)). The working model, assembled from these and not printed anywhere, is that the D1 locates the matter, the question's varga confirms, qualifies or (for children) overrides it, and the dasha delivers what both promise. One varga-specific strength rule cuts against ordinary logic: exaltation or debilitation in the D10 shows success ([COVA Ch. XIII preview](https://sagittariuspublications.com/book-preview/cova-chapter-xiii-the-tenth-house)).

**Functional labels are relative to a reference.** Houses are whole signs in every worked case read; no bhava-chalit or cusp system appears in Rath's material or Rao's book, though neither states the stance outright, so this is an inference from usage. Badhaka is the 11th, 9th or 7th from a movable, fixed or dual sign, and the school applies it from any house, from the arudha lagna and from the paka lagna, not only the lagna; in Rath's career screen, a 10th house or lord in the badhaka sign from the paka lagna or arudha lagna obstructs the career, and in a dusthana from them destroys it ([COVA Ch. XIII preview](https://sagittariuspublications.com/book-preview/cova-chapter-xiii-the-tenth-house); [Rao, sect. 13.3](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)). Rao's functional benefic and malefic scheme is standard Parashari, with one override that matters: yogadas formed with Hora or Ghatika Lagna bring luck whatever their functional nature ([Rao, sect. 13.2](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)). Rath adds a dasha-relative rule: planets in the 6th, 8th and 12th from the dasha lord act inimically.

**Yogas, afflictions and remedies follow fixed sequences, mostly assembled.** In Rath's ten factors a yoga is the eighth thing read, after house, lord, dispositor, arudha and karakas, and before argala. Combining his rules with Rao's, a yoga is identified, its cancellers named, its strength graded (orb within about six degrees, no combustion or debility, count of good-dignity vargas), confirmed in the relevant varga (D5 and D10 for power), checked for the arudha lagna and A7 not being 2/12 or 6/8 apart, and only then timed by house position and dasha, where a raja yoga given in Rahu dasha does not last ([Rao, sect. 11.7](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf); [srath.com, Atmakaraka & Karakamsa](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/scholar/atmakaraka-karakamsa/)). Rath reads raja yoga potential through the Atmakaraka's link to the navamsa lagna, and the house the Atmakaraka falls in counted to the navamsa lagna names the life's dominant result ([srath.com, Atmakaraka & Karakamsa](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/scholar/atmakaraka-karakamsa/)). Remedies come strictly after diagnosis: his *Vedic Remedies in Astrology* frames a remedy as removing a combination that blocks the good yogas, treats muhurta itself as a remedy and condemns black-magic methods ([Exotic India listing](https://www.exoticindia.com/book/details/vedic-remedies-in-astrology-idj596)); *Crux* warns that mantras are prescribed too freely and refers fertility capacity to medical care before any remedy. Rao's rule that gemstones go only to functional benefics, never to marakas in maraka periods, is tier B ([Rao, sect. 34.2](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)).

## Each question gets its own clock, and the rasi dasha confirms the nakshatra dasha

Rath's dasha doctrine is a division of labour, stated most fully in his book *Narayana Dasa*. **Narayana shows the environment's impact on the native; Vimshottari shows the native's reaction, state of mind and the power of yoga** ([Narayana Dasa, Preface, pp.xiii-xiv](https://scienceoflight.net/wp-content/uploads/extra/Narayana%20Dasa%20by%20Sanjay%20Rath.pdf)). He calls a comparative ranking of the two "useless", says apparent disagreement between them reflects the astrologer's limited understanding, and calls Narayana "largely deterministic" and the indispensable tool for broadly timing an event, where other dashas foresee several possible events that are hard to narrow ([Narayana Dasa, pp.59-61, 89-90](https://scienceoflight.net/wp-content/uploads/extra/Narayana%20Dasa%20by%20Sanjay%20Rath.pdf)). For a full examination of a chart he names a standard set of five: **Narayana, Sudasa, Vimshottari, Shoola and Drig** (p.xv). *Crux* puts the relationship as a requirement: a nakshatra dasha such as Vimshottari pinpoints an event, but its occurrence will require confirmation from a rasi dasha such as Narayana ([Crux, Introduction, p.3](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)). His children chapter applies exactly that, with Vimshottari conditions on the D7 lagna lord and an independent Narayana as the double check. The line is a principle, not a procedure: the research pass through the *Narayana Dasa* book found no rule making agreement a formal precondition, and no minimum number of agreeing systems or method for resolving disagreement appears in any tier. Inside one rasi dasha his worked confirmation is that the dasha and antardasha signs both link to the event's significators, as in his reading of Tagore's 1883 marriage through the Darakaraka and the Upapada ([Narayana Dasa, pp.111-112](https://scienceoflight.net/wp-content/uploads/extra/Narayana%20Dasa%20by%20Sanjay%20Rath.pdf)).

| Question | Rath's clock (A unless marked) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| What happens in the environment; broad timing | Narayana dasha (stronger of lagna and 7th) | Chara dasha is the movable-lagna form of Narayana in his book, not a rival system |
| Mind, experience, power of a yoga; pinpointing | Vimshottari | Needs a rasi dasha to confirm |
| A specific life area | Narayana run inside that varga, started from the varga sign of the D1 lord of the controlling house | D10 career, D7 children, D4 residence, D24 education, D12 parents, D9 spouse and dharma |
| Prosperity (Sri Shakti) | Sudasa | Start from Sree Lagna and "always read Narayana with Sudasa" are Rao's (B) |
| Health and longevity (Bhoo Shakti) **[Sensitive]** | Shoola or another ayur dasha | Rath's "Shoola" starts from the stronger of the 1st and 7th, not Parashara's Niryana Shoola (per Rao) |
| Spirituality | Drig dasha in the rasi chart first, then the D20 Narayana | |
| Enemies (D6) and destruction (D11) | Paryaya dasha; Manduka dasha | From his Table 29 remarks |
| Curses and past karma **[Sensitive]** | Mula dasa | |
| Image and maya; dharma and ideals | Padanadhamsa dasa; Lagnamsaka (Narayana is still used in all charts) | |

The varga Narayana table is his own ([Narayana Dasa, Table 29, p.119](https://scienceoflight.net/wp-content/uploads/extra/Narayana%20Dasa%20by%20Sanjay%20Rath.pdf)), and the D4 version appears in worked form in *Crux*, timing residence changes from the D4 sign of the D1 4th lord ([Crux, Ch. VII sect. 7.6.2](https://archive.org/stream/sanjayrathcruxofvedicastrologytimingofevents19982/Sanjay%20Rath%20-%20Crux%20of%20Vedic%20Astrology-Timing%20of%20Events%20(1998)_2_djvu.txt)). The *Narayana Dasa* book also gives the full period rules, including a year added when the sign lord is exalted and subtracted when it is debilitated ([Narayana Dasa, Ch. II](https://scienceoflight.net/wp-content/uploads/extra/Narayana%20Dasa%20by%20Sanjay%20Rath.pdf)); K.N. Rao's chara dasa explicitly omits that adjustment, which is the best-sourced difference between the two schools ([Saptarishis Astrology, K.N. Rao](https://saptarishisastrology.com/?p=2656)).

**Vimshottari itself does not default to the Moon in his lesson.** Rath calls it the foremost nakshatra dasha because its 120 years equal the human span in this age, and seeds a natal chart from the strongest of the lagna, Janma (Moon) and Utpanna (5th from the Moon) nakshatras, judged by planets in kendras from each sign, with Jupiter, Mercury or the star lord breaking ties; a horary chart uses the stronger of the name and query stars; the Moon's star is the fallback "when in doubt". **When four or more planets occupy kendras from the lagna, Tara dasha replaces it** ([srath.com, Vimśottari Daśā](https://srath.com/?p=804)). *Crux* gives a narrower ruling, the stronger of the lagna and the Moon, ignoring the Utpanna, name and query stars ([Crux, Introduction, pp.4-6](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf)). The two Rath texts disagree on whether Utpanna is a candidate; both agree the Moon is not automatic. Conditional dashas apply only when their condition holds: Rath states Ashtottari (Rahu in a kendra or trine from the lagna lord's sign, with a paksha and day-night condition whose "both" wording is ambiguous on his page) and Dvisaptati Sama, and his book *Vimsottari and Udu Dasas* carries applicability sections for Shodashottari, Dwadashottari, Panchottari, Shatabdika, Chaturaseeti Sama, Shashtihayani and Shat-trimsha Sama and a chapter on Tithi Ashtottari, though their texts were not read ([Exotic India, book contents](https://exoticindiaart.com/book/details/vimsottari-and-udu-dasa-s-parasara-s-key-to-prognostication-naj126)). The only tiebreak among several qualifying conditional dashas is Rao's own 2014 "controlling planet" test, under which a condition is necessary but not sufficient ([Rao, Unified Nakshatra Dasa Approach](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/pp_cond_dasas.pdf)); it is his innovation, not Rath's. No Rath or SJC text was found for Yogini dasha, and Kalachakra appears only as a presentation title. Rao relays one further Rath principle, the "tripod of life": judge a mahadasha from the Sun, an antardasha from the Moon and a pratyantardasha from the lagna ([Rao, ch. 16.5.3](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)).

Two claims carried forward from the earlier research need downgrading. "Sudasa is preferred only when the Moon sign is stronger" and "Narayana is more accurate than Vimshottari for marriage" rest on summarised readings of *Crux*; neither appears in the *Narayana Dasa* book read directly, nor in Rao, whose Sudasa starts from Sree Lagna with no Moon condition. Until checked in print they should be treated as unconfirmed.

**Transits, annual charts and Sudarshana have no Rath doctrine on the open record.** No retrieved Rath text states a transit reference point, vedha, ashtakavarga transit rule, Sade Sati or double-transit rule, or any ranking among them. The one transit-heavy srath.com article read is mundane and judges a transiting significator by counting malefic conjunctions, aspects and hemming around it, with no dasha or ashtakavarga, and its byline was not shown ([srath.com, Curse of Jupiter in Sagittarius](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/mundane/curse-of-jupiter-in-sagittarius/)). *Crux* uses transits case by case: Jupiter over the 7th, its lord or the 2nd from the Upapada as a marriage trigger; Saturn over the A10 for loss of title; Jupiter over the arudha lagna for victory ([COVA Ch. XIII preview](https://sagittariuspublications.com/book-preview/cova-chapter-xiii-the-tenth-house)). For the year, Rath's co-credited Tithi Pravesha page gives only the casting rule: the Sun in its natal sign and the Moon at the birth angle from the Sun, cast for the birthplace; it names no year lord and no dasha inside the year ([srath.com, Tithi Pravesha](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/tithi-pravesa/tithi-pravesha/)). The reading rules are tier B and conflict: Sarajit Poddar makes the weekday lord the year lord, calls the hora lord the most important factor, reads the TP lagna from the natal lagna, and says **the natal chart overrides and the annual chart is never read alone** ([Sanskriti Magazine, Poddar](https://www.sanskritimagazine.com/principles-of-tithi-pravesh-chakra-in-vedic-astrology/)); Rao, in a snippet, calls the hora lord the lord of the year. No Rath text on Tajika varshaphala, muntha or mudda dasha was found. Rath also notes a practical reason to prefer Narayana for a dasha-entry chart: it begins on the birthday, whereas a few minutes' Moon error moves a nakshatra dasha start ([Narayana Dasa, p.90](https://scienceoflight.net/wp-content/uploads/extra/Narayana%20Dasa%20by%20Sanjay%20Rath.pdf)). Sudarshana chakra fares no better: *Crux* cites it to fix the year in two retrospective marriage examples, beside Saturn and Jupiter sign progressions, but no rule for it was found, and an srath.com site search returned no technical article ([srath.com search, sudarshana](https://srath.com/?s=sudarshana)). Taken together the structure is a hierarchy, not a vote: natal promise, then the question's rasi dasha confirming the nakshatra dasha, then the year, then finer periods, with transits as supporting triggers. Only the first two links are Rath's own; the year and finer layers come from Poddar (tier B) and a single unbylined srath.com example that ran Moon-started Vimshottari inside India's 2008 annual chart, so the sequence is assembled and no source prints it.

## Confirmation questions should discriminate between candidate charts

Rath's past-first method fits the product rule that the assistant confirms before it predicts, but it implies a different kind of question from the ones the product asks today. His questions are concrete, factual, easy for the person to answer, tied to one chart factor, and chosen because they separate candidates. The set below is drawn from his worked cases (A), with Rao's additions (B) marked; it is ordered as he orders them, from low to high sensitivity ([Crux, pp.34-39, 119-120, 213-214](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/Crux-of-Vedic-Astrology-Timing-of-Events1.pdf); [Rao, ch. 32.3-32.4](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)).

The product today asks something else. Its chart-led confirm questions in `src/reading/self/words.ts` (`CONFIRM_BY_HOUSE`) are broad recent-period check-ins such as "Did something change at work recently?", and the shade "pulse" questions in `src/reading/area/shadeResolver.ts` ask about preferences. Both are useful for present-tense personalisation, which Rath's method does not address, but neither is a discriminator in his sense, and none is anchored to a dated past event. Rath's practice suggests three changes. The first is a **calibration track separate from the present-tense track**: a short set of dated facts (siblings, marriage year, children, study, moves, first job) asked once, each tied to the varga lagna it tests, and stored as events in the existing `life_events` ledger rather than as yes/no answers. The second is that **each calibration question is chosen because candidate charts disagree on it**, which keeps the count within the project's one-question-per-visit policy (doc 48): when the D9 or D10 lagna is stable across the birth-time error band, no question is needed. The third is **gating by verification**: varga-level claims (D10 service against business, D7 children, D60) are shown only once the varga lagna they rest on has survived a discriminating question, as Rao's warning about unrectified fine timing implies. Rath's ordering constraints also map directly onto product rules: confirm the past before predicting, check capacity before any denial, and diagnose before any remedy.

## Conclusion

The useful shift is from asking "which technique is strongest" to asking "which tool answers this question, and has the chart earned it". Rath's precedence is a chain of gates rather than a leaderboard: a verified birth time gates the vargas, the question picks the varga, house, reference and clock, the varga confirms the D1, the rasi dasha confirms the nakshatra dasha, and the year and transits only place what the natal chart already promises. A product that votes across many clocks can produce a confident window that no single Rath step would support, and it can show varga-level claims on a birth time nothing has tested. The product already has the right shape in `life.timing_agreement` and the primary-plus-confirming clock design; requiring the rasi dasha as the confirmer for dated claims, and adding a discriminating, ledger-backed calibration track, would bring it closer to his method than adding more systems would.

Two limits remain. Much of the precise ordering is Rao's (tier B) rather than Rath's, and several Rath sources were read through summaries; the sign-strength order, the Vimshottari seeding conflict, the Sudasa condition and the transit and annual rules all need his own books in print before they are encoded as his. And the engine capabilities that his method leans on hardest, Narayana inside a varga, a chosen dasha seed, full argala and a stronger-sign comparison, are prerequisites, not refinements: without them the product can describe Rath's method but cannot run it.
