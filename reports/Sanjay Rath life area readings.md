# Rath reads every house through one checklist

Sanjay Rath's public text gives one method for every house rather than a separate script per life area. In *The Crux of Vedic Astrology: Timing of Events*, he lists what to prepare before reading (D1, D9, D3 and the other relevant vargas, arudhas, special lagnas, and an eight-planet chara karaka table). He then gives ten factors to judge for any house, and he closes each house chapter by fixing the matter in the relevant divisional chart and timing it with a dasa chosen for the question. The life-area guides below apply that frame. Career runs from the Rasi 10th through the arudha lagna (AL), the Rajya pada (A10) and the Amatyakaraka to the D10. Wealth runs from the 2nd house and Jupiter through the 11th and 12th from the AL, Hora Lagna, Sri Lagna and Sudasa. Children run through the 5th house and Jupiter to the D7, which has the final say. Parents run through the 4th and 9th houses, the Moon and Sun, the A4 and the D12, and siblings through the 3rd and 11th, the AL and a D3 frog-jump count. Only a few orders are printed by him: the preparation list, the ten house factors, the D7 count procedure, and two ordering constraints for children (capacity before curse, D7 last). Every other step order here is assembled from his chapter structure and worked charts, and each guide says which. This round fills several gaps the earlier summary left open. His own text states the eight-karaka scheme. The seventh-house chapter has now been read in large part. The Putrakaraka and Darakaraka are now seen in his text, though only in a table and in examples. A general start rule for a varga Narayana dasa is also found, though the Narayana order and period rules are still missing. Gaps remain. His own text gives no Darakaraka selection rule, no Kashinatha hora construction, no Sudasa calculation rules, no Indu Lagna rules and no D10 deity scheme. Many of his family rules concern death, illness or loss. They are recorded here briefly as the source's rules, marked sensitive, and are not for use in readings without a decision by the product owner. No independent evaluation of his predictions was found, and this report makes no accuracy claim.

## Sources, tiers and how to read the guides

**Three tiers.** Each guide separates three tiers.

- **Rath's own text** is a book by him or an srath.com page carrying his byline.
- **Secondary accounts** are other authors' descriptions, including his students P.V.R. Narasimha Rao and Sarbani Rath, publisher blurbs, forum reposts and uploads.
- **Critique** is material written to dispute his method or record.

No secondary account below is presented as his. Where a tier is empty for an area, the guide says none was found.

## 1. D1 procedure: ten factors per house, dual karaka check first

**Provenance of the order.** Steps 1 and 2 are printed by Rath as a preparation list and a ten-factor list. Steps 3 to 8 follow the order of his worked Ascendant-chapter chart (Chart 11). His srath.com celebrity articles reorder the same steps by topic, so the sequence is a pattern, not a fixed script.

### Rath's own text

1. **Prepare the chart set** (Ch. II sect. 7, approx. p.30). Cast D1, D9, D3 and the other relevant vargas, plus the arudha, Yamada and upagraha charts. Mark the special lagnas, especially Hora Lagna and Ghatika Lagna, and identify the Karta, Kevala and Yogada planets. Tabulate the chara karakas in the eight-planet scheme, with Rahu's longitude counted backward from the end of its sign.
2. **Apply the ten factors to the house in question** (Ch. II sect. 7, approx. pp.30-31). The ten factors are:
   1. the house, its sign, and the planets in it or aspecting it;
   2. the house lord, its strength and the sign it occupies;
   3. that lord's dispositor;
   4. the navamsa lord of that dispositor;
   5. the arudha and Yamada of the house;
   6. the fixed, temporary and natural karakas of the house;
   7. the house counted from the AL and from the Yamada lagna;
   8. good and bad yogas in the house or involving its lord;
   9. argala and virodhargala on the house;
   10. the relevant saham, plus a detailed check of the relevant divisional chart.

   He supports the varga step with a Saravali citation to the effect that astrology cannot proceed without the divisional charts.
3. **Read the three ascendants independently** (Ch. IV sect. 4.1, approx. p.54). Examine the lagna, the AL and the Yamada lagna separately, with their strength. He calls these, together with the Sun and the chara Atmakaraka, vital to the chart.
4. **Run the dual karaka check.**
   - The Sun's unobstructed argala on the lagna gives status and fame, and the lagna lord joining or aspecting the Sun gives good character.
   - The Atmakaraka's unobstructed argala on the lagna gives health and a blessed life. An ill-placed or obstructed AK mars the chart.
   - Both are then read from the AL for status, and from the Varnada lagna for community standing and for health. The Varnada reading also covers death matters, which are **[Sensitive]**.

   (Ch. IV sect. 4.1, approx. p.54.)
5. **Lagna lord, dispositor and nakshatra lord.** Chart 11 maps the D1 lagna and navamsa lagna to temperament. It then takes the lagna lord, its sign dispositor and its Vimshottari nakshatra lord, and the yoga that nakshatra lord forms. A table gives results for the lagna lord in each house, each conditional on its strength. For example, the lord in the 7th gives a wandering life unless the lagna lord is strong (Ch. IV sect. 4.4, approx. pp.59-63).
6. **Yogas, then what cancels them.** In Chart 11 a Mahapurusha yoga is noted, then a negative Kalamrita yoga and the planet that cancels it (Ch. IV, Chart 11).
7. **Special lagnas as yoga-givers.** A planet linked to both the lagna and Hora Lagna becomes a wealth-giver (dhana yogada). A planet linked to both the lagna and Ghatika Lagna becomes a power-giver (rajya yogada) (Ch. II sect. 2.2, approx. p.19).
8. **Promise to timing.** House position gates when a yoga acts. Yogas in the 2nd and 4th fructify early, and a rajayoga in the lagna acts late (Ch. II approx. p.19; Ch. IV Chart 11). Chart 11 closes with AL status, papakartari on the lagna and a Narayana dasa table tied to events.
9. **Choose the dasa for the question.** Vimshottari and the other nakshatra dasas time the state of mind, measured from the Moon. Narayana dasa times what the environment brings (Preface). The Vimshottari starting star is the strongest of four: the lagna star, the Moon star, the name-initial star, or the Moon star at the query, citing Jataka Parijata (Ch. I sect. 6, approx. p.5). Planets in the 6th, 8th and 12th from the dasa lord act inimically, and those in the 7th from it give adverse results (Ch. II, approx. pp.22-23).

A srath.com reading of a former US President follows a topical order. It rectifies the birth time through the Atmakaraka and the navamsa lagna first, then goes through Upapada, D10, the other vargas, rajayogas and the AL, and ends with dasa and transit forecasts ([srath.com, Bill Clinton](https://srath.com/celebrity/usa/bill-clinton/)).

**Gap filled: eight chara karakas.** The earlier summary found no statement of the karaka count. This round finds it in Rath's own text. *Crux* tells the reader to tabulate the karakas in the eight-planet scheme and to count Rahu's longitude from the end of its sign (Ch. II sect. 7, approx. p.30). The Chart 11 table labels all eight karakas (AK, AmK, BK, MK, PiK, PuK, GK, DK), with Rahu as Amatyakaraka (Ch. IV, Chart 11, approx. p.61). An srath.com article names Rahu as the Atmakaraka in two charts ([srath.com, Atmakaraka and Karakamsa](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/scholar/atmakaraka-karakamsa/)). Some points remain inference or unresolved:

- **Selection rule.** Choosing by highest degree within the sign is an inference from the table. It was not found as a written rule.
- **Ties.** No tie-break rule was found.
- **Mundane charts.** A seven-karaka scheme for mundane charts is reported only by a critic's document seen as a search summary.

## 2. Varga charts: five harmonic cycles, karya bhava and karyesha

**Provenance of the order.** Rath states the casting rule and defines the karya rasi, the karyesha and the Subhapati. He gives the karya rasi only for the D10, so applying steps 3 to 5 to other vargas extends that example. The varga dasa start rule is his own (step 6). The transit rule is shown by a chapter title only.

### Rath's own text

1. **Cast the varga.** Divide each sign into N equal parts, and place each part, with any planet or lagna in it, in a fixed sign. His worked D3 uses the Parasara drekkana: the first ten degrees go to the sign itself, the second to the 5th from it, and the last to the 9th ([srath.com, Principles of Divisional Charts](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/principles-of-divisional-charts/)).
2. **Know which plane the varga belongs to.** A division number repeats every twelve, so D16 and D40 are higher octaves of D4. The five cycles each sit on a deeper plane ([same article](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/principles-of-divisional-charts/)):

| Cycle | Plane | Charts in use, and what each signifies |
|---|---|---|
| D1-D12 | Physical | D1 body; D2 material wealth; D3 siblings; D4 fixed assets; D5 authority; D7 children; D9 spouse; D10 work; D12 parents (D6, D8 and D11 named, no area given) |
| D13-D24 | Conscious | D16 mental happiness, luxuries, vehicles; D20 spirituality; D24 learning |
| D25-D36 | Sub-conscious | D27 strengths and weaknesses; D30 evils and misfortune **[Sensitive]** |
| D37-D48 | Super-conscious | D40 maternal-line karma; D45 paternal-line karma |
| D49-D60 | Supra-conscious | D60 past-birth karma, the heaviest weight among the sixteen vargas |

3. **Read the varga from its own lagna.** Find the karya rasi, the house that is the varga's subject (his example is the 10th in the D10). Check that the D1 lord of that house is well placed in the varga. All the D1 basics apply inside a varga: rasi and graha drishti, argala, and yogas formed by conjunction, mutual aspect, exchange or mutual placement ([same article](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/principles-of-divisional-charts/)).
4. **Find the karyesha.** These are the significators of the activity, and there can be several (natural, chara and fixed karakas). In his example, a person who is both politician and lawyer, argala on each karyesha decides which role thrives or fails as the dasas change ([same article](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/principles-of-divisional-charts/)).
5. **Check the Subhapati.** The dispositor of the Moon, judged by its dignity across the vargas, shows health and longevity **[Sensitive]**. Links to the lagna or Atmakaraka, then to Hora Lagna or Ghatika Lagna, raise it to Kevala yoga, Kevala Yogada and Kevala Mahayogada ([same article](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/principles-of-divisional-charts/)).
6. **Time inside the varga.** Start a Narayana dasa from the sign occupied, in that division, by the Rasi lord of the relevant house, and then read the division as a separate chart. He demonstrates this on the D4, including job-linked moves in 1969 and 1991 (*Crux*, Ch. VII Fourth House, approx. pp.155-157). This general start rule is new this round. It does not unblock the D7 Narayana double check in section 5, because the Narayana order and period rules are still not retrieved from his text.

**D2: his own texts differ.** His srath.com article files the D2 under material wealth ([Principles of Divisional Charts](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/principles-of-divisional-charts/)). *Crux* lists the Hora against wealth but questions that common reading and ties the hora to life and health. It assigns fortune and immovable property to the D4, and movable property and luxuries to the D16 (Ch. II sect. 7). He taught a workshop on critical wealth houses read in D1, D9 and D60 together ([srath.com, BAVA 18th conference](https://srath.com/learn/workshops/bava-18th-international-conference/)). He edited *Varga Chakra* and wrote its "Hora Chakra" and "Shastyamsa" chapters, but their contents are not public ([srath.com, Varga Chakra](https://srath.com/?p=58)).

### Secondary accounts

**Kashinatha hora (Rao).** P.V.R. Narasimha Rao, Rath's student, says the school rejects the Parivritti-dvaya hora as a wealth chart and reads it as family support. The wealth chart is the Kashinatha hora from the Rath family tradition. In it, a planet in the Sun's half goes to the day-strong sign of its own sign lord, and a planet in the Moon's half to that lord's night-strong sign. He applies the karyesha rule to the D2 this way:

- the D1 2nd lord is the hora chart's karyesha;
- the hora chart's AL shows how finances are perceived;
- a Hora Narayana dasa starts from the D2 sign of the D1 2nd lord.

([Rao, Using Kashinatha Hora Chart, 2003, reposted](https://jyotish-blog.blogspot.com/2008/06/using-kashinatha-hora-chart.html)). This is Rao's account. No Rath-bylined construction of the Kashinatha hora was found.

**Other secondary material.**

- Rao's book treats the Rasi as one special case of a varga, and he reads the D3 as initiative and the D4 as direction behind action. These come from search summaries of his book.
- A Scribd lecture summary titled with Rath's name says the D9 shows skills and the D10 success or failure. Its byline was not checked ([Scribd, "Navamsa Sanjay Rath"](https://www.scribd.com/document/356674100/Navamsa-Sanjay-Rath)).
- Student chapters in *Varga Chakra* cover arudhas, special lagnas and dignities inside vargas, and transits from the varga Moon and lagna ([srath.com, Varga Chakra](https://srath.com/?p=58)).

## 3. Career: Rasi 10th, then AL and A10, then the D10 decides

**Provenance of the order.** This order is assembled from the tenth-house chapter's section sequence and about twenty worked charts. Rath does not print it as a list. The chapter itself is ordered as: 10th house; 10th from the Moon and Hora Lagna; navamsa and Karakamsa; unemployment and ill fame; professional determination and fame; professionals and other careers.

### Rath's own text

1. **Rasi 10th house and lord** ([COVA XIII preview](https://sagittariuspublications.com/book-preview/cova-chapter-xiii-the-tenth-house)).
   - **Significance.** The 10th ranks second only to the lagna, and the 10th lord is the most influential planet by lordship. Its karakas are Jupiter (prosperity, fame), Saturn (work), Mercury (career), the Moon (fame) and the Sun (status). Low ashtakavarga bindus in the 10th weaken the whole chart.
   - **Planets in the 10th.** Saturn gives a rapid rise and fall. Ketu gives a routine job. Mercury or Venus suit business.
   - **10th lord by house.** In the 2nd, a family business. In the 3rd, travel or short contracts. In the 5th, research or speculation.
2. **Argala on the 10th** ([COVA XIII preview](https://sagittariuspublications.com/book-preview/cova-chapter-xiii-the-tenth-house)). The lagna's argala decides job satisfaction, through friendship between the lagna planet and the 10th-house planet. The 11th's argala means incentives drive performance. The 8th's argala decides how a career ends.
3. **Paka Lagna and AL screen.** If the 10th or its lord falls in the badhaka sign from the Paka Lagna (the lagna lord's sign) or from the AL, the career is obstructed. In a dusthana from them, it is destroyed (same preview).
4. **10th from the Moon and from Hora Lagna.** After Kalyana Varma, with a Saravali table for planets in the 10th from the Moon. Mars there points to work far away. Malefics there can make a remover of evils, such as a doctor, priest or astrologer (same preview; *Crux* Ch. XIII sect. 13.6).
5. **Navamsa layer** (*Crux* Ch. XIII sect. 13.7).
   - Planets in the 10th from the navamsa lagna or the Karakamsa decide wealth: malefics bring fluctuation, benefics stability.
   - The 10th lord's navamsa dispositor can make or mar the career. If it is malefic and hostile, it spoils the 10th lord's dasa, above all in the dispositor's own antardasa.
   - The 10th lord's navamsa sign reads as follows: fixed, a desk job; movable, moving about; dual, mixed.
   - The planet owning or occupying the 10th names the source: the Sun the father, the Moon the mother, Mars enemies, and so on.
6. **Unemployment and ill fame** (*Crux* Ch. XIII sect. 13.8). Saturn with the 10th or its lord, debilitated planets in the 10th, or malefics in the navamsa 10th indicate unemployment or job loss. Mars and Saturn with the 10th, papakartari on the 10th, or the 10th lord in a malefic navamsa give ill fame.
7. **AL, A10 and A7: the visible career** (*Crux* Ch. XIII worked charts).
   - **AL.** The AL's occupants name the field. In Muhammad Ali's chart, Mars and Saturn in the AL gave boxing.
   - **A10.** The Rajya pada rules titles. Planets in it, or in trines to it, name the title, and several planets in it mean repeated titles.
   - **A7.** The Darapada is the business pada.
   - **Status.** The Sun and the chara Atmakaraka seen from the AL show status (*Crux* Ch. IV sect. 4.1).
8. **Amatyakaraka.** He defines it as the karaka next after the Atmakaraka. In the worked charts he reads it as shaping the profession: Mercury gives a writer, the Moon in the navamsa lagna public service, and Rahu in Pisces nursing (*Crux* Ch. XIII, Charts 108, 112, 115). No standalone rule text was found. His workshops list the Amatyakaraka with the 10th house, Saturn and the D10 as one set ([srath.com, BAVA 2017](https://srath.com/announcements/bava-2017/)).
9. **D10 as a full chart** (*Crux* Ch. II sect. 7; [COVA XIII preview](https://sagittariuspublications.com/book-preview/cova-chapter-xiii-the-tenth-house)). He calls it karmamsa and svarga chakra, and analyses it like a Rasi chart.
   - **Profession.** The artha trikona (2, 6, 10) gives the profession, and the 7th, as the pada of the 10th, is read too.
   - **Raja yoga.** Raja yoga must be confirmed in the D5 and the D10.
   - **Field by dominant planet.** Sun, politics; Moon, government or hotels; Mars, police or army; Jupiter, law, priesthood or astrology; Mercury, trade and writing; Venus, the arts; Saturn, heavy work; Rahu, imports; Ketu, exports.
   - **Dignity.** Exaltation or debilitation in the D10 shows success, against ordinary dignity logic. Debilitated Saturn as the D10 10th lord in the D10 lagna gave T.N. Seshan power (*Crux* Ch. XIII, Charts 110, 112).
10. **Service or business** ([COVA XIII preview](https://sagittariuspublications.com/book-preview/cova-chapter-xiii-the-tenth-house); *Crux* Ch. II sect. 7, Ch. XIII). In the D10:
    - The 6th rules service and the 7th business, and the stronger of the two decides. The preview misprints the 6th as business; the surrounding text makes service the intended word.
    - The 7th is the maraka of the 6th. The 9th is the boss and independence, and the 5th is subordinates and authority.
    - The lagna lord in the 9th suits business, and in the 5th, service.
    - He weighs where the 6th and 7th lords sit from the D10 lagna and its lord, not only house strength. In Seshan's chart both houses were empty and the lords decided. In Chart 124, Saturn in a stronger 7th still meant service.
    - To start a business, the 2nd is capital. The 5th, 8th and 11th from it are partners' capital, loans and own funds.
11. **Time with D10 house lords** (*Crux* Ch. XIII; [COVA XIII preview](https://sagittariuspublications.com/book-preview/cova-chapter-xiii-the-tenth-house)).
    - **House meanings for events.** The 5th marks joining a new job: its lord "will always give" openings. The 8th marks retirement or exit. The 7th lord unsettles service. The 6th lord in the 5th cuts authority. The 12th marks intrigue, loss or sales.
    - **Raja yogas by house.** Formed in the 2nd, 4th, 10th or 8th, they act early. In the 1st, 7th, 3rd or 9th, late. Elsewhere, in midlife.
    - **Rahu dasa.** A raja yoga given in Rahu dasa does not last.
    - **Narayana dasa.** Narayana signs tied to the AL or A10 time titles.
    - **Transits.** Saturn over the A10 brings loss of title, and Saturn aspecting the AL damages image. Jupiter over the AL gives a victory.
    - **Neechabhanga.** A debilitated planet in an exalted navamsa gives sudden promotion, short-lived if it is retrograde.
    - **Promotion side effects [Sensitive].** With Venus as the 6th lord in a Vipareeta yoga, misfortune or death in the spouse's family comes at the promotion. With Jupiter, one's own family suffers then (*Crux* Ch. IX sect. 9.3).

The worked charts mix fame and title cases (Ali, Seshan, Nightingale, two beauty-pageant winners) with three ordinary employees (Charts 123, 124, 126), whose job-change timings are the clearest. Several timings were forecasts written in 1997-98, and the text gives no confirmed outcome for them. Some charts also time deaths, which is **[Sensitive]**.

## 4. Finance and wealth: stored wealth, visible income, drains, then Sudasa

**Provenance of the order.** This order is assembled from *Crux* chapters II, V, XIV and XV. No numbered wealth checklist by Rath was found.

### Rath's own text

1. **Promise: the 2nd house and Jupiter** (*Crux* Ch. V sect. 5.1-5.3).
   - **The house.** The 2nd is stored wealth, family, speech and food. Jupiter is its karaka, and Hora Lagna is treated as the wealth point.
   - **Planets in the 2nd.** The Sun gives good income and lavish spending. Saturn gives hard-won money. The Moon gives large but unstable wealth, waxing raising it and waning lowering it. The nodes or Saturn can wipe wealth out.
   - **2nd lord.** In the lagna, earning by own effort. In the 2nd, growth. In the 6th, gains and losses through opponents. Any 12th link turns a source of wealth into a source of loss.
2. **Argala on the karaka and the lagna** (*Crux* Ch. II sect. 6; Ch. V sect. 5.3.5).
   - Planets in the 2nd, 4th and 11th from Jupiter give visible wealth: bank balance, fixed property and movable property.
   - The 5th and 8th from it give invisible wealth, the 8th tied to inheritance.
   - Papargala on the lagna brings loss, and subhargala brings profit. Worked example: J.R.D. Tata.
3. **Arudha checks on the 2nd** (*Crux* Ch. V sect. 5.3.3-5.3.4). The Dhanapada (A2) is the source of income. The Upapada in the 2nd brings wealth through the spouse. The Varnada in the 2nd brings wealth and status, and in the 12th, questionable activity.
4. **Visible income: the 11th from the AL** (*Crux* Ch. XIV sect. 14.3, 14.8-14.9).
   - **Occupants.** Each planet there names a source: the Sun government or father, Ketu exports, Venus clothing. Benefics show legitimate income and malefics illegitimate.
   - **11th lord's house names the channel.** The lagna, own effort. The 2nd, family. The 7th, business or in-laws. The Upapada, marriage into wealth.
   - **Ashtakavarga.** More bindus in the 11th than the 12th means income exceeds spending.
5. **Drains: the 12th from the AL** (*Crux* Ch. XV sect. 15.4, 15.7-15.10).
   - Planets that give gain from the 11th turn to loss if they are also linked to the 12th from the AL.
   - The 12th lord in the 2nd loses wealth.
   - The Moon in the 12th from the AL is loss of raja yoga.
   - The lagna lord or the Atmakaraka's dispositor aspecting the 12th from itself makes a spendthrift.
6. **Confirm with special lagnas, sahams and vargas** (*Crux* Ch. I sect. 7.3-7.4; Ch. II sect. 2.2, sect. 7).
   - A planet linked to both the lagna and Hora Lagna is the dhana yogada. Ghatika Lagna is power, not money.
   - Sri Lagna is the prosperity lagna and is used to time wealth in the Carnegie and N.T. Rama Rao cases.
   - Indu Lagna is named but given no rules.
   - The Artha saham is the 2nd cusp minus the 2nd lord plus the lagna, reversed at night.
   - The D4 carries fixed property and the D16 luxuries; the D2 question is set out under vargas.
7. **Time with Sudasa, Narayana and Vimshottari** (*Crux* Ch. I sect. 4; Ch. V sect. 5.5; Ch. VII sect. 7.5; Ch. XIV).
   - **Sudasa.** The Lagnadi rasi dasa (Sudasa) times finance, fortune and marriage. It is preferred over Narayana dasa only when the Moon sign is stronger, so he does not apply it to every chart.
   - **Narayana and Vimshottari.** Narayana dasa times changes in life. The 11th lord's Vimshottari dasa is generally unfavourable.
   - **D16 sub-periods.** Financial turns can be timed by the sub-sub-period of the relevant lord in the D16.
   - **Dasa lord's co-operators.** A friendly pachaka brings possessions, and a friendly vedhaka brings loss and travel.
8. **Debt, speculation and inheritance** (*Crux* Ch. XI sect. 11.1, 11.10; Ch. II sect. 7; Ch. V sect. 5.4).
   - **Debt.** Mars signifies debt. Debt comes when Mars is 8th from the Vimshottari dasa planet, or 8th from the Narayana dasa sign with no benefic in the 12th. It also comes in the period of the 2nd lord with Saturn, or of the 8th lord.
   - **Sources by house.** The 6th is loans and service. The 8th is insurance, dowry, inheritance and business loans.
   - **Speculation.** It appears only as the 8th from the D10 lagna.
   - **Inheritance [Sensitive].** Inheritance yogas are read in one case as the sign of a parent's or relative's death (Ch. V, Chart 23).
9. **Remedies are case-bound.** Fast on the weekdays of planets in the 12th (Ch. XV sect. 15.7). Use a Ganesha mantra in an income and job-change case (Chart 33). For 6th-house troubles: Surya and Shanti mantras, a crystal mala, propitiating the relatives' karaka, and pilgrimage (Ch. IX).

## 5. Children: fifth house and Jupiter, then capacity, then the D7 decides

**Provenance of the order.** Rath states two ordering constraints himself: the capacity check comes before any curse is declared, and the result is pronounced only after the D7 is examined (*Crux* Ch. VIII sect. 8.5.2, 8.5.5). He also prints the D7 count procedure as a sequence. The rest of the steps below follow chapter VIII's section order. Much of this rests on the earlier round's reading of the same chapter, restated here so this guide stands alone.

### Rath's own text

1. **Fifth house and Jupiter** (*Crux* Ch. VIII sect. 8.1-8.3, approx. pp.166-169).
   - The 5th governs children, and Jupiter is its karaka. Planets strong from Jupiter give the number and nature of children, and the 5th lord's position from Jupiter gives the gains from them.
   - Planets in the 5th: the Sun gives few children; Venus favours daughters; Saturn and Mars bring loss of children **[Sensitive]**.
2. **Modifiers** (*Crux* Ch. VIII sect. 8.5.1, approx. pp.171-172).
   - Benefics in the 5th do not harm children even as dusthana lords, and malefics there do not favour them.
   - Venus or the Moon owning and occupying the 5th in a watery sign gives many children, and fiery charts need remedies.
   - The Saturn-Mars and Jupiter-in-the-5th sign rules include denial **[Sensitive]**. The Jupiter passage pairs its own-house principle with Cancer and Aquarius, which are not Jupiter's signs. Check it against print before any use.
   - Many malefics in the 12th may prevent children.
3. **Adoption** (*Crux* Ch. VIII sect. 8.5.3, approx. p.172). The 5th house or lord linked to Mercury or Saturn signs indicates adoption. Rahu on the Upapada or 7th points to a marriage to someone with children from an earlier marriage.
4. **Curses before denial [Sensitive]** (*Crux* Ch. VIII sect. 8.5.4, approx. pp.172-173). Denial may come from past-birth curses listed in BPHS, each with stated conditions and remedies.
5. **Capacity check first [Sensitive]** (*Crux* Ch. VIII sect. 8.5.5(A), approx. p.174). Before pronouncing a curse, test physical capacity through the arudha padas of the 3rd and 9th, counted from the lagna and from the Sun. Infertility is indicated by the Sun in the 2nd, 6th, 8th or 12th from them, or by Venus with Mercury on them. If either set is undamaged, birth is delayed rather than denied. If the lagna padas are afflicted, he refers the person to medical attention and says not to attempt remedies unnecessarily.
6. **D7 has the final say** (*Crux* Ch. VIII sect. 8.5.2, 8.5.5(B), approx. pp.172-175). Saturn, Venus and Mercury placed only in trines of the D7 is called an overriding rule for barrenness **[Sensitive]**.
7. **Count and sex in the D7** (*Crux* Ch. VIII sect. 8.6.1, approx. pp.175-177).
   - **First child.** Read from the D7 5th, counted forward or backward by the D7 lagna's odd or even sign.
   - **Later pregnancies.** Every third house: 5th, 7th, 9th, 11th for an odd lagna, and 9th, 7th, 5th, 3rd for an even one.
   - **Sex.** Taken from the lord of the pregnancy house. Exalted, or with the Sun, Rahu, Jupiter or Mars, means male. Debilitated, or with the Moon, Ketu or Venus, means female. Saturn indicated a female and Mercury a male in his experience. Otherwise the sign decides, using the book's sign table.
8. **Each child's life** (*Crux* Ch. VIII sect. 8.6.1). Treat the pregnancy lord's sign as the child's lagna.
   - In the 7th from the D7 lagna, the child opposes the parent; in a quadrant, they cooperate. In the 12th, the child lives abroad.
   - The child's marriage is read from the Upapada of the child's lord.
   - The controlling and killer houses (the 5th and 6th in a male chart, the 9th and 10th in a female chart) indicate pregnancy loss or a child's death **[Sensitive]**.
9. **Birth timing** (*Crux* Ch. VIII sect. 8.6.2, approx. pp.177-178).
   - The Vimshottari dasa planet must give benefic argala to the D7 lagna lord, or obstruct papargala on it.
   - The antardasa planet relates to the child's lord, or sits in a trine, the 7th or the 12th from it.
   - A pratyantara planet in a trine to the D7 lagna contributes the rising sign.
   - As a double check, run a Narayana dasa from the D7 sign of the D1 7th lord. This shares the form of the general varga rule in section 2, but that rule starts from the lord of the matter's own house, which for children would be the 5th; the book's reason for the 7th lord here was not found. The double check still cannot be run, because the Narayana order and period rules are not retrieved from his text.
10. **Saham and remedies.** The Putra saham is Jupiter minus the Moon plus the lagna, with a reversed form (Ch. I saham table). Fifth-house remedies are placed beyond the book's scope. He warns that many astrologers prescribe mantras too freely, and that a mismatched propitiation can endanger children (Ch. VIII sect. 8.2, 8.4).

**Gap filled, partly: Putrakaraka.** The earlier summary found no Putrakaraka in the sections it read. This round finds it in his text twice. It is a column (PuK) in the Chart 11 karaka table (Ch. ## 6. Parents: house, karaka, arudha, chara karaka, then D12

**Provenance of the order.** This order is assembled from the fourth-house and ninth-house chapters. Rath does not print it as a list.

### Rath's own text

1. **House and lord** (*Crux* Ch. VII sect. 7.1-7.2; Ch. XII sect. 12.1-12.2, 12.9).
   - **Mother: the 4th.** Planets in the 4th have argala on the lagna. The Moon there gives the mother's blessings, and Jupiter protects.
   - **Father: the 9th, with the 10th also affecting him.** A malefic in the 10th harms the father, Saturn there gives large swings, and the nodes there are hostile to him.
   - **9th lord.** With the lagna lord, the native takes in the father's teachings. In the 3rd, the father is described unfavourably.
2. **Fixed karaka.** The Moon for the mother and the Sun for the father. The Sun and Jupiter in the 9th are exempt from the rule that a karaka in its own house harms it, and the Sun there makes the native dutiful (*Crux* Ch. XII sect. 12.8, 12.11).
3. **Arudha of the house from the AL** (*Crux* Ch. VII sect. 7.3). Read the Matru pada (A4) from the AL: the 3rd or 11th means friendly relations, the 6th enmity, the 8th a poorer maternal family, and the 12th losses.
4. **Chara karaka.** The chara Matrikaraka in a dusthana from the A4 means poor maternal health **[Sensitive]** (*Crux* Ch. VII sect. 7.3). No parallel chara Pitrikaraka rule was found; the father sections use the Sun.
5. **D12** (*Crux* intro; Ch. XII, approx. p.214 of that copy). The D12 governs parents, elders and lineage, and is one of the key rectification vargas.
   - The father is the 9th from the D12 lagna and the 9th from the D12 Sun.
   - The mother is the 4th from the D12 lagna and the 4th from the D12 Moon.
   - The 5th gives the paternal grandfather and the 12th the grandmother.
6. **Lineage karma.** Paternal-line karma is read in the D45 and its 9th, maternal-line karma in the D40 and its 4th, and past-birth karma in the D60 (*Crux* Ch. XII).
7. **Sahams.** The father's saham is Saturn minus the Sun plus the lagna (day), and the mother's is the Moon minus Venus plus the lagna (Ch. I).
8. **Remedies.** Yellow sapphire for Rahu, and ruby for Saturn in the 9th harming the father or teacher (*Crux* Ch. XII sect. 12.8).
9. **Parent longevity and death timing [Sensitive].** These are recorded only as the source's rules (*Crux* Ch. III approx. p.26; Ch. VII Charts 40-43; Ch. XII; Ch. V Chart 23):
   - Treat the parent's house as that parent's lagna, and count strong lords of the 1st, 8th and 10th from it for three longevity bands.
   - Time a death with Matru or Pitri Shoola dasa (nine years a sign), refined by Narayana, Vimshottari and the Sudarshana chakra.
   - Use the marakas from the father's house.
   - A D12 relative's lord in a Rasi dusthana means death near the native's birth.
   - Saturn with the Moon in the 4th, Rahu in the 4th, and Rahu afflicting the Sun are read as early loss.
   - In-law deaths are timed with Dara Shoola dasa.

   A non-death event in this set: separation from parents in infancy was timed in a one-year Narayana dasa (Ch. VII, Chart 44).

## 7. Siblings and family: the best-specified family method in the book

**Provenance of the order.** This order is assembled from the third-house chapter, which runs in this sequence. Rath does not number it.

### Rath's own text

1. **House** (*Crux* Ch. VI, approx. p.65). The 3rd is co-borns, courage and skills. Malefics there give valour and benefics timidity.
2. **Younger and elder, from the lagna and the AL** (*Crux* Ch. VI sect. 6.2). Younger siblings are read from the 3rd and elder from the 11th. Counted from the lagna they show physical presence. Counted from the AL they show number, counting forward for odd signs and backward for even.
3. **Count.** The number of siblings is the planets joining or rasi-aspecting the 3rd lord (younger) or the 11th lord (elder) (*Crux* Ch. VI sect. 6.2).
4. **Karakas.** Mars is the karaka. The chara Bhratrikaraka appears in examples: Venus as Bhratrikaraka in the 3rd shows a strong first younger sibling (Ch. II, Chart 7). Lagna lord in the 3rd: helpful brothers (Ch. IV).
5. **D3 ordinal count** (*Crux* Ch. VI; intro). Use a frog jump (manduka gati) through the D3. The first younger sibling is the 3rd lord from the D3 lagna and the next the 5th lord, and so on. Elder siblings run from the 11th lord, then the 9th. Saturn and Mars indicate brothers, and Mercury and the Sun sisters. Each sibling can be read like a child, by treating the sign as a lagna (Ch. VIII sect. 8.6.1).
6. **Saham.** The co-born saham is Jupiter minus Saturn plus the lagna (Ch. I).
7. **Disputes and remedies** (*Crux* Ch. VI sect. 6.7.4, Chart 37). Disputes with siblings over property are read from the D3 together with the D4. In the example, the dispute was timed in Sun dasa and ended after remedies.
8. **Harm and death of siblings [Sensitive]** (*Crux* Ch. VI sect. 6.2, 6.3, 6.7.3). Recorded only as the source's rules:
   - Malefics in the 3rd harm younger siblings.
   - Saturn with Rahu in the 3rd or 11th from the AL indicates a sibling's death.
   - Venus in the 8th suggests a stillborn sibling.
   - Bhratri Shoola dasa from the stronger of the 3rd and 9th, with the Sudarshana chakra for the year, times a sibling's death.
   - The 3rd from the AL shows the native's own manner of death.

A births case gives the arrival of twin brothers in a Ketu-Venus-Saturn sub-period (Chart 34).

**Family (kutumba): the 2nd house** (*Crux* Ch. V, approx. pp.48-58).

1. **The house.** The 2nd is family, speech and food. Jupiter is its karaka, and its lord is likened to Vishnu the sustainer.
2. **The A2 from the AL.** In a dusthana, no firm family bond. In a kendra or trikona, family members get along. In the 11th, the native gains from family, and in the 3rd, the family gains from the native.
3. **2nd lord.** In the 1st, the 4th (home) turns hostile. In the 2nd, it may harm offspring **[Sensitive]** and raises the chance of a second marriage, since the 2nd is the 8th from the 7th.
4. **Timing.** Family migration was timed in Sudasa and Vimshottari (the Carnegie case). Sudasa, Vimshottari and Narayana are named for this house.

## Voice and phrasing: lineage-bound, firm in cases, conditional in rules

This section describes traits of his public writing in neutral terms. It does not reproduce or imitate his voice.

### Rath's own text

**Authority and lineage.**

- He presents himself as carrying a family tradition from Puri, traced to Sri Achyuta Dasa and taught by his uncle Pt. Kasinath Rath ([srath.com, about](https://srath.com/series/about)).
- He frames his approach as holistic and rooted in the seers rather than a system of his own, built on BPHS, Jaimini's Upadesa Sutras, Brihat Jataka and Saravali.
- He argues that Jaimini is not a separate school, and he corrects named misconceptions directly; one example is education as the 4th house with the 5th as argala on it (*Crux* Preface).
- He admits his explanations may fall short and invites criticism.
- He says some methods, such as the 5th house in timing death **[Sensitive]**, are taught only inside Jyotish families.

**Devotional framing.**

- Prefaces and articles open with invocations and close with blessings ([srath.com, Bill Clinton](https://srath.com/celebrity/usa/bill-clinton/); *Crux* Acknowledgement).
- Deities appear inside house results: the lagna lord in the 9th is blessed by Jupiter as Maha-Vishnu, in the 10th by parents and Ganesha (*Crux* Ch. IV sect. 4.4).
- Sanskrit verses are printed and then explained in English.
- Epic precedent sits beside modern cases, as when Rama's exile is set next to a politician's rajayoga (Ch. II).

**How predictions are phrased.**

- Conditions are built into the rule rather than given as probabilities. A typical form is a result that holds unless the lagna lord is strong, or argala that works only if unobstructed.
- Case statements are mostly firm and declarative. In one case he calls a current dasa disastrous and says the next will be better, with remedies in the same paragraph (*Crux* Ch. IV, approx. p.75).
- A srath.com article carries a box of his past predictions, presented by him as fulfilled. That tone reading comes from a fetch summary, not a line-by-line read ([srath.com, Bill Clinton](https://srath.com/celebrity/usa/bill-clinton/)).
- Several worked-chart forecasts in *Crux* were written in 1997-98, and the text gives no confirmed outcome for them.
- The Darapada article is the most hedged text found, and its rules use "may" and "can".

**How remedies are phrased.**

- Remedies follow diagnosis. The Atmakaraka points to the remedy (Mercury as AK points to truthful speech) ([srath.com, Atmakaraka and Karakamsa](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/scholar/atmakaraka-karakamsa/)).
- In *Crux*, each remedy is tied to a planet or house in a named case rather than given as generic advice: mantras (Ganesha, Surya, Shanti), gems for the 9th, and fasting for planets in the 12th.
- He warns against prescribing mantras too freely (*Crux* Ch. VIII sect. 8.4).
- He refers capacity questions to medical care before any remedy (Ch. VIII sect. 8.5.5(A)).

**Site disclaimer.** The srath.com disclaimer says astrology does not replace licensed advice, that interpretations differ, and that remedies carry no guarantee. It asks clients to disclose their emotional and physical state so the astrologer can judge whether to advise ([srath.com, disclaimer](https://srath.com/misc/media/disclaimer/)).

## Conclusion

The practical finding is that Rath's method can be run in a fixed order on a stored chart more often than his books' loose structure suggests. The ten house factors and the varga karyesha rule are uniform, so each life-area guide above is the same machine aimed at a different house, karaka, arudha and varga. Where he is specific, as with the D7 sequence, the D10 service-against-business test and the sibling frog jump, the rules are categorical and need every input computed before they can fire. Where he is silent, the silence is usually filled in circulation by students (Rao on Sudasa and the Kashinatha hora, Sarbani Rath and a forum repost on curses). Those fills should stay labelled as theirs.

Two things change from the earlier summary. First, the karaka count is no longer an open question, so a reading that uses the Amatyakaraka, Putrakaraka or Darakaraka can at least cite his eight-karaka scheme, though the selection rule itself (ranking by degree) remains an inference from his table, not a stated rule. Second, a large share of the family material he does state is about death, loss and curses. A product built on his family method therefore faces a decision before any parent, sibling or children board: which of his rules may be shown at all. That decision belongs to the product owner, not to the method.
