# Rath's texts fix child rules, leave dasha open

Sanjay Rath's public texts state the most specific rules for children and marriage, and they name a Jaimini reference frame, Karakamsha, plus several dasha systems that each come with their own applicability rule. They do not state the number of chara karakas, any Chara Dasha rule, any Jaimini yoga, a Darakaraka selection rule, or a seventh-house partner rule, and the book's seventh-house chapter lies outside the text read. No Rath text ranks the dasha systems for life events, and none was found for Kaala, Yogini, Chaturaseeti Sama or Tithi Ashtottari. The earlier readings attributed child-timing windows and dasha overlays to his principles; as found, his public text does not support several of those systems. This is a finding about the sources, not a verdict on the readings. The most detailed material is the children chapter of *Crux of Vedic Astrology: Timing of Events*, read from a third-party scan of unconfirmed provenance. No systematic evaluation of Rath's predictions was found, so this summary makes no claim about how accurate any rule is.

**Source tiers.** Each section sorts its material into three groups. *Rath's own text* is a book by Rath or an srath.com page that carries his byline. The book is *Crux of Vedic Astrology: Timing of Events* (Sagar Publications). It is cited by title, section and page rather than by link, because the copy read was a third-party scan of unconfirmed provenance, and its page numbers come from running heads that may be off by one where the scan is damaged. The srath.com pages were read through a summarising fetch, so every statement from them is paraphrase, and any wording to be reused has to be checked against the live page. Some srath.com pages are co-credited or show no byline. A co-credited page is listed as Rath's text with that flag; a page with no byline is not counted as his. *Secondary accounts* are everything else: listings, uploads, other sites, co-authored books and search summaries. *Critique* covers statements that dispute a method or a prediction. Claims seen only in search output, with no pinned URL, appear under gaps and support no finding. The srath.com header reads 18 November 2026. That is after the research date and is a render date, not a publication date.

## Karakamsha is stated; the karaka count is not

**Rath's own text.** The Atmakaraka article defines Karakamsha as the navamsa, or another divisional chart, in which the Atmakaraka falls, and it names Rahu as Atmakaraka in two charts it discusses ([srath.com, Atmakaraka and Karakamsa](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/scholar/atmakaraka-karakamsa/)). It refers to other chara karakas as temporal significators without listing them or giving a count, and it gives no rule for choosing the Atmakaraka. The natural-zodiac article treats Karakamsha as Aries, reads planets from it through the natural zodiac, and links the 9th and 12th from Karakamsha to dharma and the chosen deity ([srath.com, Karakamsha and the natural zodiac](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/scholar/karakamsa-and-natural-zodiac/)). The same article counts four signs from the 10th house to Karakamsha, then says there is no dictum for extending that count to other houses; the page does not say what the count is for. The Darapada article gives the 12th from Karakamsha a reading of release from rebirth, and the 7th from Karakamsha a reading of bondage ([srath.com, Darapada and Upapada](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/scholar/darapada-and-upapada/)). The Complexity article says rashi drishti can cause lasting damage, and argues that the Jaimini sutra covers more than rashi drishti because Parashara taught sign aspects before planetary ones ([srath.com, The Complexity of the Jaimini Sutra](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/scholar/complexity-of-jaimini-sutra/)). The same article reports a commentator's reading that places Gaunapada at the fifth arudha, then gives Rath's own reading, which links the first vyaya-pada, as taught in BPHS, to the 12th house. The Upadesha Sutras pages list Karakamsa versus Swamsa, the chara, sthira and naisargika karakas, and special ascendants as topics, with no rule text ([srath.com, Upadesha Sutras](https://srath.com/misc/jaimini-maharishis-upadesa-sutra/); [srath.com, course outline](https://srath.com/misc/jaimini-scholar-course-book/)). The course outline says it has 19 chapters but lists 21, and only Volume I was checked.

**Secondary accounts.** A srath.com page (?p=3437) says Parashara prescribes sign aspects before graha drishtis, and that Jaimini named his work to explain Parashara's teaching. Its Tradition and Course Overview sections are credited to another author, Andrew Foss, with Rath listed as author, so the page is treated as mixed authorship and not as Rath's own text ([srath.com, page 3437](https://srath.com/?p=3437)). A srath.com search excerpt of a celebrity reading, which was not opened, gives the 12th from the Atmakaraka in the navamsa as the house of the guiding deity ([srath.com, reading excerpt](https://srath.com/celebrity/politics/shiv-sena-party/)).

**Critique.** No critique of these Jaimini methods was found.

**Gaps.** Not found on the pages checked: the number of chara karakas and their ordered list; the rule for choosing the Atmakaraka and for handling Rahu's degree; the Putrakaraka; how Karakamsha is computed from a chart and how it differs from Swamsha; the arudha calculation; the mechanics of rashi drishti; and any Jaimini yoga definition. The Darakaraka appears in chart examples but its selection rule does not (section 4). A search summary's claim that Rath uses eight karakas for human charts and seven for mundane charts has no URL and is not used. The Upadesha Sutras' date is 1997 on srath.com and 2002 in a catalogue record seen in search, which carries a 1997 copyright; the conflict is unresolved.

## Dasha systems carry their own rules; Chara Dasha is absent

**Rath's own text.** The Mula dasa article starts from the stronger of the Lagna and a kendra holding planets, then takes the other kendras, then panapara and apoklima planets. The parity of the Lagna decides whether panapara or apoklima follows, with Saturn and Ketu in the Lagna as overrides. Each planet's period comes from counting signs to its mulatrikona sign, adjusted by one year for exaltation or debilitation and taken from its Vimshottari years; a second cycle brings the total to 120 years. Rahu and Ketu are placed as in Narayana dasa. The page assigns the system to curses, hardship and adverse events, and to the period when a curse or blessing takes effect. The sign-strength ordering it uses is credited to Jaimini's Upadesa Sutra ([srath.com, Mula Dasa](https://srath.com/?p=6254)).

The Dvisaptati Sama article gives applicability when the lagna lord is in the 7th, the 7th lord is in the lagna, or both. It runs eight planets, from the Sun to Rahu, in weekday order at nine years each: 72 years a cycle and 144 over two. The start is set by counting from Moola to the birth nakshatra, removing multiples of eight, and counting the remainder from the Sun, with the balance taken from the Moon's longitude. It states that dashas tend toward adverse results in their closing sub-period. The page cites no source ([srath.com, Dvisaptati Sama Dasa](https://srath.com/?p=75)).

The Vimshottari Dasha #2 page, a continuation whose Part 1 was not read, builds Moon-based lines from the 4th, 5th and 8th nakshatras counted from birth, and compares their end dates with the Janma Vimshottari end dates. Its triggers are close or coinciding end dates, especially closing portions. Coincidence in two or more lines is the stronger signal, and a shorter period narrows the window. The page states its subject as illness, accidents and death. It adds triggers from the lords of the 2nd, 6th and 8th houses, a Rudra lord (the stronger of the 2nd and 8th lords), Gulika's longitude, rasi-sandhi, and dashas numbered 1, 3, 5 and 7, which it maps to infancy, adolescence, middle life and full life ([srath.com, Vimshottari Dasha #2](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/dasa/vimshottari-dasha-2/)).

The Narayana Dasha page states one rule in the text retrieved: the dasha starts from the stronger of the Lagna and the 7th house, a choice the page explains with a symbolic name for the 7th. It says the strength rules come from BPHS and the Jaimini Sutras and that sources differ on their order. Its contents list order, period, house effects, timing of marriage, transits, ashtakavarga and variants, but only the headings were retrieved ([srath.com, Narayana Dasha](https://srath.com/misc/naraya%e1%b9%87a-dasa/)). A dasa category listing says the Shula dasha can time events of the body and that the Moon is stressed for health; the article itself was not opened ([srath.com, dasa category](https://srath.com/category/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/dasa)).

### Annual charts are defined but not yet used for dasha

**Rath's own text.** The Tithi Pravesha page, co-credited to Rath and P.V.R. Narasimha Rao, defines the annual chart's moment as the point when the Sun reaches the natal sign and the Moon stands at the same angle from the Sun as at birth. The chart is cast for the birthplace, whatever the current residence, and the page says it throws light on the coming year. The page names no dasha running within the year. It uses the solar-return method only as a contrast, describing a Tajika annual method ([srath.com, Tithi Pravesha](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/tithi-pravesa/tithi-pravesha/)). Whether Tithi Pravesha itself is of Tajika origin is unresolved: one fetch read it that way, and the second did not confirm it. The only page found that places dasha periods inside an annual chart is a srath.com page on India's 2008 events. It shows no byline, so it is not counted as Rath's text, and it is a single example, so no general confirming rule can be drawn from it ([srath.com, India Tithi Pravesh 2008](https://srath.com/jyoti%e1%b9%a3a/mundane/india-tithi-pravesh-2008/)).

**Secondary accounts.** A Scribd Dasa Pravesh Chakra upload, with authorship unverified, says a new rashi-dasha period begins when the Sun reaches its natal longitude ([Scribd, Dasa Pravesh Chakra](https://www.scribd.com/document/448743437/Dasa-Pravesh-Chakra)). A Sanskrit Magazine article, seen in search, says the Tithi Pravesh chakra stayed within a tradition until Rath made it known ([Sanskrit Magazine](https://www.sanskritimagazine.com/principles-of-tithi-pravesh-chakra-in-vedic-astrology/)). A publisher listing for a marriage-timing book describes it as using Rath's Tithi Pravesha method; the author is not confirmed on the listing ([Motilal Banarsidass](https://www.motilalbanarsidass.com/products/timing-of-marriage-with-tested-techniques)).

**Critique.** None found on this method. No rule was found for confirming or refining a dasha by an annual chart, and no Rath text was found on Varshaphal.

**Other dasha systems, secondary only.** Kalachakra, per a search summary of an explainer that was not a Rath text, is sign-based: its order follows the Moon's nakshatra pada, sign periods come from planetary year values applied through each sign's lord, and a savya or apasavya birth changes which signs apply ([Satyori, Kalachakra Dasha](https://satyori.com/jyotish/dasha/kalachakra/)). A Scribd presentation titled "Kälacakra daśä" is attributed to Rath by its title only and was not opened ([Scribd, Kalachakra presentation](https://www.scribd.com/presentation/620260194/KCD-SanjayRath)). A search summary says the *Crux* book covers Kalachakra calculation and motion rules; only the title was seen, and no Kalachakra text was read. Yogini dasha is described, in a search snippet, as a 36-year system with eight Yoginis, no part for Ketu, and a start set by the Moon's nakshatra ([Vedicastro, Yogini Dasha](https://vedicastro.com/yogini1.aspx)). The source is not Rath's and gives no attribution. Tithi Ashtottari appears in P.V.R. Narasimha Rao's *Vedic Astrology: An Integrated Approach* (a search result, not opened), which covers Vimshottari and Ashtottari and names Rath as the author's teacher. That is a co-author's account, not Rath's text. Three secondary sources give different applicability conditions for Ashtottari, and the Yogini source reverses the others' conditions. No Rath text and no linked secondary source was found for Kaala or Chaturaseeti Sama.

**Gaps (dasha).** Chara Dasha: no direction, sub-period order or period length appears on any page checked. The Upadesha page names other systems (Narayana or Padakrama, Sthira, Shoola, Brahma, Varnada, Mandooka, Paryaya or Gochara, Kendradi, Sudasa) but not Chara Dasha. The course outline has a Dasha chapter with no text. A guessed Chara Dasha address returned 404, which does not show the page is absent. Other gaps: the priority order among dasha systems for life events (none found); whether a running period activates a house, lord or karaka (no rule found); how transits time events (the Narayana contents list transits as a heading only); Vimshottari Part 1, the base rules for Vimshottari Dasha #2 (unread); and the Narayana book's date, which is 1998 on the listing, 2001 in a search summary and 2019 in a bookseller listing. ## Children rules run through the fifth house and D7

**Rath's own text.** In *Crux of Vedic Astrology: Timing of Events* (sect. 8.1, 8.3, pp.166, 169), the fifth house governs children and Jupiter is its natural significator. The number and nature of children are read from Jupiter, and the fifth lord's position from Jupiter indicates the extent of gains from children. Planets in the fifth carry stated outcomes: the Sun gives few children and is less favourable for daughters; Venus favours daughters but can harm the mother; Saturn brings loss of children; Mars brings loss and domestic discord (sect. 8.2, p.167). Two modifiers follow. Benefics in the fifth do not harm children even when they own dusthanas, and malefics there do not favour child-related happiness (sect. 8.5.1(a)-(b), p.171). Venus or the Moon owning, occupying or aspecting the fifth in a fluid sign indicates many children, and fiery charts are said to need remedial measures (sect. 8.5.1(c), p.171). Saturn with Mars in the fifth, and Jupiter in the fifth, each carry outcomes by sign (sect. 8.5.1(d)-(e), p.171). Many malefics in the twelfth may prevent children (sect. 8.5.1(f), p.172). Adoption is indicated when the fifth house or lord is associated with Mercury- or Saturn-signs, and when the upapada or seventh is associated with Rahu, which the book links to marriage with a widow who has children (sect. 8.5.3, p.172).

The Jupiter passage needs checking before use. As recorded, its principle is that a natural significator placed in its own house damages its significations. Yet the denial it lists falls in Cancer and Aquarius, which are not Jupiter's own signs, while the own signs Sagittarius and Pisces give difficulty or few children. The pairing may be an error in the notes or in the book. It is reported here as recorded and not repaired.

The D7 is treated as the final authority. The book says the D7 holds the key to pregnancy and that the result is given only after the D7 is examined (sect. 8.5.2, p.172). It treats the D7 check as overriding for barrenness: Saturn, Venus and Mercury placed only in trines are read as making the native barren regardless of other combinations (sect. 8.5.5(B), pp.174-175). Before a curse-based denial is pronounced, physical capacity is checked through the arudha padas of the 3rd and 9th from the lagna and from the Sun. Infertility is indicated when the Sun falls in the 2nd, 6th, 8th or 12th from those padas, or when Venus and Mercury conjoin them. When either pada set is undamaged, the book reads childbirth as delayed, not denied, and it refers the matter to medical attention if the lagna padas are afflicted (sect. 8.5.5(A), p.174). Past-birth curse types are read from BPHS, each with stated conditions (sect. 8.5.4, pp.172-173).

Count and sex follow the D7 (sect. 8.6.1, pp.175-177). The first child is read from the fifth house of the D7, counted in direct or reverse order according to whether the D7 lagna is odd or even. Later pregnancies fall at every third house: for an odd lagna at the 5th, 7th, 9th and 11th, and for an even lagna at the 9th, 7th, 5th and 3rd. The child's sex follows the lord of the pregnancy-house sign. A lord that is exalted, or conjoined with the Sun, Rahu, Jupiter or Mars, indicates a male. A lord that is debilitated, or conjoined with the Moon, Ketu or Venus, indicates a female. Saturn and Mercury form a separate class in the book, which reports Saturn as indicating a female and Mercury a male in its author's experience. If neither rule applies, the sign's odd or even nature decides, with listed exceptions. Each child's life is read by treating the pregnancy lord's sign as a lagna. A child's lord in the 7th from the D7 lagna means opposition with the parent, and a quadrant means cooperation. A benefic in the 3rd or 6th may bring loss to the parent, and the 12th means living abroad. The child's marriage is read from the upapada of the child's lord. The 5th (in a male chart) or 9th (in a female chart) is the controlling house and the 6th or 10th the killer house. A child's lord in the killer house, or conjoining its lord, is read as a risk to the pregnancy or to the child's life (sect. 8.6.1, pp.176-177). That last indicator concerns medical outcomes and is treated as such in section 5.

Birth timing uses the D7 lagna lord (sect. 8.6.2, pp.177-178). The Vimshottari dasha planet must give benefic argala to the D7 lagna lord, or obstruct papa argala, for a birth to fall in its period. The antardasha planet should relate to the child's lord or sit in a trine, 7th or 12th from it. A pratyantara planet in a trine to the D7 lagna contributes the rising sign at birth. For a double check, the book suggests independent Narayana dashas from the sign of the seventh lord, placed in the D7. A worked example (p.184) reasons from a Rahu-Ketu axis across the D7's 2nd and 8th houses, modified by Jupiter in the D1 ascendant and a seventh-lord link in the D7. It is an example, not a general rule. The saham table (Ch. I, p.13) gives a Putra saham of Jupiter minus Moon plus the ascendant, with a reversed form; its column labels are not legible in the scan. The book places remedies outside its scope for the fifth house (sect. 8.2, p.168) and warns against mantras prescribed without a match (sect. 8.4, pp.169-170).

**Secondary accounts.** srath.com readings of named public figures (Bill Clinton, Franklin Roosevelt, Jyoti Basu) apply the same D7 count and sex logic. They were seen as search snippets and not opened, so they are treated as illustrations, not tests ([srath.com, Bill Clinton reading](https://srath.com/celebrity/usa/bill-clinton/); [srath.com, Jyoti Basu reading](https://srath.com/celebrity/jyoti-basu/)). A Scribd "D-7 Chart" note matches the Jupiter-from-fifth-lord passage to *Crux* p.169, which the scan's placement supports, and its Drekkana-for-co-borns point matches sect. 8.6.1. Its navamsa-gain count and shared-sign survival rule are not in the text read, so they are unverified ([Scribd, D-7 Chart](https://www.scribd.com/document/419033424/D-7-Chart)). General sites, not Rath: PanchangBodh treats Jupiter as the natural significator of children and the D7 fifth house as the lead house ([PanchangBodh, saptamsa](https://panchangbodh.com/saptamsa-chart-calculator)), and VedicBirth says counting children from the D7 is less predictive today and that the D7 is better read for capacity and relationships ([VedicBirth, saptamsa](https://www.vedicbirth.com/varga/saptamsa)). A jagannathhora page describes the Putrakaraka as the Jaimini significator of children; it is unattributed and its link to Rath is not established ([jagannathhora, Putrakaraka](https://jagannathhora.com/putrakaraka-children-creativity/)).

**Critique.** No critique of the children method was found. The VedicBirth caveat is a general statement, not a critique of Rath.

**Gaps.** Not found in the text read: the navamsa-gain count of the fifth lord, the survival rule for two child-signifying lords in one sign, and the condition that the fifth lord share a placement with Jupiter as navamsa karaka for a method to work. The term Putrakaraka does not occur in the sections read. The Narayana double check cannot be applied because the Narayana rule text was not retrieved. The book states its children rules categorically, and no independent test of them was found.

## Marriage placements are stated; the Darakaraka rule is not

**Rath's own text.** The Darapada and Upapada article labels the upapada A12 or UL and the Darapada A7. Venus signifies marriage and the A7, and Ketu signifies renunciation and moksha. It gives placement readings, each hedged. Venus in the 12th from the lagna or arudha lagna may mean loss of a spouse. Ketu in the 12th may mean delayed marriage or denial of marital happiness. Venus in the 6th from the arudha lagna may mean no marriage or separation. Ketu in the 7th or A7, with Venus in the 12th or UL, may mean two spouses. Venus in the 7th or A7, with Ketu or Rahu in the 12th or UL, may mean loss of a spouse, remarriage or renunciation. Renunciation is promised only where Venus sits in a dusthana from the arudha lagna, which is the article's firmest wording. Harmony between the 7th and 12th houses and their arudhas, especially from Karakamsha, is said to benefit the native and can support growth through a spouse. The A7 governs sexual desire and its fulfilment or renunciation. The article says the upapada and the 7th house alone cannot settle whether a person marries, so the A7 must be examined too. It reports no failed case ([srath.com, Darapada and Upapada](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/scholar/darapada-and-upapada/)). A companion article advises looking for a connection between the 12th house and/or the upapada and the Sun, Venus and Rahu, without stating effects ([srath.com, Complexity of the Jaimini Sutra](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/scholar/complexity-of-jaimini-sutra/)).

The *Crux* text adds these points. Spouse matters are examined from the upapada, and Venus is the sthira karaka for wife and in-laws, per Jaimini. Spouse-related shoola dashas begin from the first or seventh house, whichever is stronger, and run nine years each (Ch. VII, p.139). In a co-borns worked example, Jupiter is the spouse significator for a female chart (Ch. II, p.37). In one chart example the 8th house and its lord are read as the second, maraka, from the seventh, with a check in the navamsa; the book presents this as reasoning inside a single chart (Ch. II, p.37). The 2nd from the upapada or from the 7th is read for the sustenance of marriage, but the scan's reference to a natural significator is ambiguous, so it is not assigned to the upapada-second alone (Ch. V, p.79). Wealth from a spouse is read from an upapada in the 2nd (Ch. V sect. 5.3.4, page not fixed). The argala of the 3rd lord on the 7th gives benefit from the spouse, and a 7th lord in the 3rd indicates a spouse of doubtful character, while an 8th lord in the 3rd indicates more than one attachment (Ch. VI p.111; sect. 6.5, p.112). Where an earlier emotional bond is strong, the 8th from the upapada is treated as the upapada for a second marriage, illustrated by two charts (Ch. VII, p.143). Love and affection are read from Venus and the D9 (sect. 8.1, p.166).

Marriage timing uses progressions. Auspicious events, including marriage, are timed from Jupiter's sign or its seventh at one year per sign. Inauspicious events are timed with Saturn at two and a half years per sign (Ch. I, page not fixed). A worked example times a marriage through Saturn progressions over natal Venus and cites the Sudarshan Chakra for the year. The Vivaha saham is Venus minus Saturn plus the ascendant, with a reversed form (Ch. I saham table, p.14). A delayed-marriage example (pp.183-184) came in a Moon-Venus sub-period, with Venus in the upapada linked to a Sudarshan Chakra sign. The example is retrospective.

Chart examples in *Crux* name a chara Darakaraka. The rule that selects it is not stated in the text read. A srath.com reading seen only as a search snippet names Mars as the chara Darakaraka and ties a wedding to a Mars dasha with a Jupiter antardasha; it was not opened and is retrospective ([srath.com, Franklin Roosevelt reading](https://srath.com/jyoti%E1%B9%A3a/dasa/franklin-delano-roosevelt/)).

**Secondary accounts.** A publisher listing for *Timing of Marriage with Tested Techniques* says it combines classical Parashari and Jaimini material, the Vivaha saham, Tithi Pravesha and transits with the Darakaraka, and draws on Rath's Tithi Pravesha method and on K.N. Rao's techniques. The author is not confirmed on the listing, and the book was not read ([Motilal Banarsidass](https://www.motilalbanarsidass.com/products/timing-of-marriage-with-tested-techniques)). A Vedicfeed article says the planet with the lowest degree is the Darakaraka and that its period often brings marriage. It does not attribute this to Rath, so the convention cannot be credited to him ([Vedicfeed, When Will I Get Married](https://vedicfeed.com/when-will-i-get-married/)).

**Critique.** No critique of the marriage methods was found. The Darapada article's own hedges are the nearest thing to a stated limit, and section 5 covers them.

**Gaps.** The derivation of the upapada and of the A7 is not stated. The Darakaraka selection rule is absent; the term appears only in examples. The seventh-house chapter lies outside the extract, which ends near printed page 249, and the chapter starts near page 251. The book's seventh-house partner rules are therefore unread, and so are its D9 partner rules beyond the love passage. A search summary says a Rahu Darakaraka denies marriage; that claim is not in the fetched text and is not used. Some Ch. V and VII page numbers are not fixed. A Scribd upload titled "Navamsa Sanjay Rath" was listed but not fetched, and its authorship is unverified.

**Applying the table.** Every rule needs its inputs computed before it can run. This project treats the calculation engine as the only source of astrological data, so any application goes through that engine and not through application code. The table lists what each rule needs and gives no calculation path. A rule marked partial or absent must not be run as though it were complete. Where a stored chart lacks an input, such as an arudha calculation, the rule cannot run; that is different from a rule that returns nothing.

**Finding on earlier readings.** Earlier readings attributed child-timing windows and dasha overlays to Rath's principles. This report did not examine those readings and does not know which systems they used; it works only from the sources above. Against those sources, his public text does not support several of the systems such attributions would need. No Rath text was found for Kaala, Yogini, Chaturaseeti Sama or Tithi Ashtottari, and no Chara Dasha rule was found. The child-timing method in his book is specific: it runs through Vimshottari dasha and antardasha checks against the D7 lagna lord, with a pratyantara condition and a Narayana cross-check whose rules were not retrieved. A child-timing window built on another system therefore goes beyond the text found. A dasha overlay built on Mula dasa, Dvisaptati Sama or Vimshottari lines does have Rath pages behind it, though those were read through a summarising fetch and, for Vimshottari, without the base rules. The only page placing dasha periods inside an annual chart carries no byline. This is a finding about the sources, not a judgment of the readings.

**Limits Rath states himself.** The *Crux* preface says its explanations may be inadequate and invites criticism. Its introduction says that overlooking the riders of Vimshottari dasha produces incorrect predictions. The children chapter places the full range of fifth-house remedies beyond the book's scope and warns that mantras prescribed without a match can endanger children (sect. 8.2, p.168; sect. 8.4, pp.169-170). The srath.com disclaimer says astrology does not replace medical, financial or legal advice, that interpretations differ between astrologers, and that remedies carry no guarantee of accuracy or result ([srath.com, disclaimer](https://srath.com/misc/media/disclaimer/)). The Darapada article hedges its placements and says no single house settles marriage. None of these statements gives a probability for a prediction.

**Rules that touch illness, death and medical outcomes.** Vimshottari Dasha #2 ties its windows to illness, accidents and death. The *Crux* children chapter gives indicators for pregnancy loss, child death, infertility and denial. The book itself refers capacity questions to medical attention (sect. 8.5.5(A), p.174). This summary records these as the sources' rules only. A product that applies them should not present them as predictions. The project's rule that the assistant confirms the question before it predicts applies to every row of the table.

**Accuracy.** No systematic or independent evaluation of Rath's predictions was found. The critical material is anecdotal forum posts about named predictions. One IndiaDivine thread repeats a 2003 message attributed to K.N. Rao, which says most charts in *Crux* were wrong and shows no chart ([IndiaDivine, K.N. Rao thread](https://www.indiadivine.org/content/topic/1216566-knrao-about-sanjay-rath/)). A 2004 thread has a former client describing a predicted event during a Ketu sub-period that did not occur, with no transcript or chart ([IndiaDivine, 2004 thread](https://www.indiadivine.org/content/topic/1183996-sanjay-raths-failure/)). A 2005 supporter's blog post, seen as a search snippet, lists claimed hits and one miss ([blog, 2005](https://jyotish-blog.blogspot.com/2005/04/predictive-success-etc.html)). None of these can be checked from the material, and none concerns a method as a whole. A general paper on prediction evaluation notes that accuracy cannot be reliably estimated from isolated predictions chosen after the fact; it is not about astrology ([arXiv 1602.01013](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.01013)). This summary makes no accuracy claim about any rule, in either direction.

## Conclusion

The gap pattern is the most useful result. Rath's public material is strongest where it is a single book with explicit placements, as in the children and marriage chapters, and weakest where it has to combine systems. Each dasha system carries its own applicability rule and purpose, and the texts found never rank them or show how a Chara Dasha layer would sit above them. A reading that stacks windows from systems with no Rath text on top of his children rules combines sources his texts do not combine, and that is the practical consequence of the finding above.

Four checks would close most open questions before any further use: a check of the printed book's Jupiter passage (sect. 8.5.1(e)); a reading of the rest of *Crux*, including the seventh-house chapter and any Kalachakra material, which lie outside the extract read; a check of the live srath.com pages behind the Mula, Narayana, Darapada and Atmakaraka summaries, which were read through a summarising fetch; and a settled byline for the rashi drishti, India Tithi Pravesh 2008 and Tithi Pravesha pages. Until the bylines are settled, those pages should not be cited as Rath's alone. The Chara Dasha, Darakaraka and karaka-count rules need a source that has not yet been found, and none of them should be filled from secondary material.
