# Classical texts crown one clock; we serve forty-three

The research documents more than forty dasha systems across the classical texts, yet those texts treat only one, Vimshottari, as the default for every chart; everything else is conditional, confirmatory, annual, or confined to longevity, and the texts judge any running period by the condition of its lord in a fixed order (promise, then dasha, then transit, then the year). AI AdAstra's calculation engine already serves 43 dasha systems, 23 divisional charts, the Jaimini points, the annual Tajika layer and the daily panchang, which is more breadth than the texts require. The problem is fidelity, not coverage: of 133 techniques compared against the texts, **eight serve wrong data today** to a board or reader, among them the Mudda annual dasha (wrong starting lord for 15 of 27 birth stars, confirmed by running the engine) and the annual sahams (day and night taken from the birth instead of the year), with the Chara, Narayana and Yogini sub-periods and three ashtakavarga cells suspected from the code and awaiting a reference check. A further 33 techniques and engine issues are needed by a board but are missing, computed in Node against the project's own rule, or wrong but not yet read. The specs compound this by describing planned work (vedha, murti, Sudarshana, Karakamsha) as built. The recommended path is to build a reference-test harness in the engine and fix the served numbers before adding any new clock. The full index is in [docs/deep-research/README.md](../docs/deep-research/README.md), the row-by-row comparison in [06-gap-matrix.md](../docs/deep-research/current-specs-review/06-gap-matrix.md) and the order of work in [07-recommendation.md](../docs/deep-research/current-specs-review/07-recommendation.md).

## Parashara lists dozens of clocks but trusts only one

Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra chapter 46 opens with a long list of dashas and then ranks them: Vimshottari is the chief (*mukhyā*) period for most people, and verse 46.11 says Ashtakavarga, Sandhya, Pachaka and "the others beginning with Tara" are **not universally agreed** ([BPHS 46, Sanskrit](https://enjoylearningsanskrit.com/scriptures/parashara/chapter-46)). Vimshottari's arithmetic is fixed in the same chapter: count from Krittika to the birth star, take the lord in the order Sun, Moon, Mars, Rahu, Jupiter, Saturn, Mercury, Ketu, Venus with 6, 10, 7, 18, 16, 19, 17, 7 and 20 years, and shorten the first period by the part of the Moon's star already crossed ([BPHS English e-text](https://archive.org/stream/ParasharaHoraSastra/BrihatParasharaHoraSastraVedicAstrologyEbook_djvu.txt)). Nine **conditional nakshatra dashas** follow, each with a one-line chart condition: Ashtottari (Rahu in a kendra or trikona from the lagna lord, or a day birth in the dark fortnight), Shodashottari, Dwadashottari, Panchottari, Shatabdika, Chaturashiti-sama, Dwisaptati-sama, Shashtihayani and Shat-trimshat-sama. The text never says which wins when several apply; P.V.R. Narasimha Rao's modern answer is to pick the one whose "controlling planet" is strongest, or fall back to Vimshottari ([PVR, Unified Nakshatra Dasa Approach](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/pp_cond_dasas.pdf)). Yogini (36 years, no condition) is the most popular second clock in practice, and Kalachakra, built on the nakshatra pada, is praised as the most authoritative of its group but survives mainly in specialist schools.

The **sign (rasi) dashas** are a second family, central to Jaimini's Upadesa Sutras and repeated in BPHS: Chara, Narayana, Sthira, Shoola, Niryana Shoola, Brahma, Navamsa, Drig, Trikona, Mandooka, Yogardha, Sudasa, Lagna Kendradi and a handful more ([Jaimini Sutras, tr. B. Suryanarain Rao](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/articles/JAIMINISUTRAS.pdf)). The texts rarely say what each is for; most stated purposes come from modern authors. Narayana is P.V.R. Narasimha Rao's and Sanjay Rath's general clock, Chara is K.N. Rao's, Sudasa serves wealth and status, Drig serves spiritual growth, and Shoola, Niryana Shoola, Sthira, Brahma and Navamsa dashas exist to **time death**, which keeps them out of any consumer reading ([PVR, Vedic Astrology: An Integrated Approach](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf)). Even within one name the schools disagree: the BPHS "Shoola" (from the 2nd and 8th) is a different calculation from Jaimini's "Shoola" (from the 1st and 7th), and there is no agreed rule set for Chara.

A third group covers strength, longevity and the year. BPHS 74 devotes a full chapter to **Sudarshana Chakra**, a year, month and two-and-a-half-day house of focus counted together from lagna, Moon and Sun and to be checked against ashtakavarga ([BPHS 74](https://enjoylearningsanskrit.com/scriptures/parashara/chapter-74)). The Tajika annual tradition adds the solar-return chart with its Muntha, year lord, sixteen yogas and sahams, timed inside the year by Mudda and Patyayini ([Hayanaratna, Gansten tr.](https://wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/hayanaratna-the-jewel-of-annual-astrology)). Tithi Pravesha, the lunar-return year chart, is a modern SJC construct rather than a classical one. Naisargika and the Pinda, Amsa and Nisarga longevity dashas complete the set from BPHS 43 and Brihat Jataka; the popular "planetary maturity ages" have no verse at all.

## The texts judge the lord, then let transits pull the trigger

How a running period is read is more settled than which clock to use. BPHS 34 and Phaladeepika 20 fix the **functional nature** of each planet for a given lagna (trine lords good, 3/6/11 lords bad, kendra lords neutralised, a yogakaraka owning a kendra and a trine). BPHS 47 then grades the period lord by dignity and house, judged both at birth and at the moment the dasha begins, with the planet's dreshkana deciding whether results come early, mid or late; Phaladeepika 20.30 supplies the only numeric dignity scale found (1, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25, 0), and 20.29 makes the sub-period lord's house counted from the main lord decisive for the sub-period ([Phaladeepika ch. 20](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/phaladeepika-by-mantreswara-text-and-translation/d/doc1621592.html)). Saravali adds a check of the Moon and the lord at the dasha start ([Saravali, English](https://lakshminarayanlenasia.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/docs2/LakshmiNarayanLenasia.com/Astrology%20(Jyotish)%20collection/Saravali%20(Kalyana%20Varma).pdf)). Below the third level the texts offer arithmetic and a single judging rule (BPHS 61.2), so **levels four to six are timing aids, not independent verdicts**.

Transits sit under the dasha. Phaladeepika 26 makes the natal Moon the chief reference, lists the good houses for each planet, and pairs every good house with an obstruction (vedha) house that cancels it ([Phaladeepika 26.1-5](https://www.siva.sh/phaladeepika/26/1-5)); ashtakavarga bindus override the plain house rule, with 28 as the threshold for a supportive sign ([Phaladeepika 23.16-20](https://www.siva.sh/phaladeepika/23/16-20)). No verse found states "dasha first, transit second" outright, but every school reads it so, and the Tajika texts likewise integrate the year with the natal chart rather than with Vimshottari: a natal house lord strong in the year and aspecting that house delivers it, otherwise the result comes "like a dream" ([Hayanaratna 6.1](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/hayanaratna-the-jewel-of-annual-astrology/d/doc1500951.html)). The precedence the boards already use, promise, then natal dasha, then transit, then annual confirmation, then in-year timing, is therefore consistent with the texts, though only its first two links are textual.

## Eight served numbers disagree with the texts

Set against these rules, the engine is broad and mostly faithful at the top level. It exports 43 dasha systems, every `dasha.*` type defaulting to **four levels and up to six** (sign dashas five, Kalachakra one), while the server stores three for life; the "two levels" figure in older notes applies only to legacy and annual types ([03-engine-exports.md](../docs/deep-research/current-specs-review/03-engine-exports.md)). Vimshottari's levels one to three follow the text's proportional rule exactly.

The failures cluster in the clocks the boards vote with. **Mudda** adds completed years to a planet's id instead of its position in the Vimshottari order, so the annual dasha starts from the wrong lord for Moon stars ruled by Rahu, Saturn, Mercury, Ketu or Venus; a researcher confirmed this by running the engine ([mudda-dasha.md](../docs/deep-research/dashas-other-and-annual/mudda-dasha.md)). **Annual sahams** switch their formulas on the birth's day or night, not the year chart's, against the Tajika rule ([tajika-sahams.md](../docs/deep-research/tajika-prashna-muhurta/tajika-sahams.md); [Hayanaratna 4.2](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/hayanaratna-the-jewel-of-annual-astrology/d/doc1500922.html)). Four more come from reading the code and need a reference run before anything changes: **Chara** sub-periods appear to repeat one list in every main period, **Narayana** sub-periods take the 8th sign where the rule names the 7th ([dashas-rasi-jaimini overview](../docs/deep-research/dashas-rasi-jaimini/00-overview.md)), the Yogini star table disagrees with BPHS 46.199 for the first six birth stars, and three bindu cells in the ashtakavarga table differ from Phaladeepika 23 while leaving every total intact, so the standard 337 checksum cannot catch them. The remaining two P0 rows were already known: stored Tithi Pravesha year-dasha rows still span 108 years instead of one (the engine at the inspected commit already bounds them, so a re-pull fixes the data), and the plain `yogas` table reports Sun and Moon yogas for empty houses. Just below the line sits the transit table that sets "pressure" for the five-clock tones: it reads Jupiter as merely mixed from the 1st, 3rd, 4th and 10th from the Moon, where Phaladeepika calls those houses bad, and no table covers the Sun, Mercury or Venus; which table is canonical is already an open decision, and the text answers it.

Below the P0 line the gaps are rule fidelity and missing pieces. The Tajika year lord is picked as the strongest of all five candidates rather than the strongest of those aspecting the year's lagna, as Balabhadra's sources require ([Hayanaratna 5.8](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/hayanaratna-the-jewel-of-annual-astrology/d/doc1500936.html)); Venus's harsha joy house is the 12th instead of the 5th; Ashtottari's second condition is not checked, and Shodashottari and Shat-trimshat-sama are never checked at all, so the conditional clock under-reports. Dasha options never reach the library, which is why Narayana runs only on the birth chart and never on the D9 or D10 that Jaimini marriage and career readings use. Vedha, murti, Sudarshana, a Karakamsha frame and houses counted from the Upapada are absent, and nothing in any export says which school's variant was used.

## The specs describe a system that does not yet exist

The 45 per-technique specs and the 142-row technique catalogue overstate what runs. Every catalogue row is marked built and, with governance columns never seeded, every technique reads as verified; specs say vedha and murti are ready though the engine has neither, and several "declared charts" blocks list strength types the code never declares ([01-technique-spec-inventory.md](../docs/deep-research/current-specs-review/01-technique-spec-inventory.md); [02-stored-technique-contracts.md](../docs/deep-research/current-specs-review/02-stored-technique-contracts.md)). The annual layer is hollow: the Tajika data type is not in the annual pull set, the Tithi Pravesha rows never carry a year lord, and the specs give one name to two different year lords (the weekday lord of the lunar return and the Tajika Varshesha). Node still computes astrology in about 25 places, including vargottama and neecha bhanga, conjunctions by sign, house classes, the houses a period activates, and a fallback that sets a missing lagna to Pisces, all outside the one folder the guard test scans ([05-findings.md](../docs/deep-research/current-specs-review/05-findings.md)). One routing choice runs against the texts outright: the Love plan reads romance from D7, which BPHS assigns to children ([shodashavarga file](../docs/deep-research/natal-techniques/11-shodashavarga-divisional-charts.md)).

Some uncertainty remains in the research itself. Several primary texts were read only through summaries or not at all (Uttara Kalamrita, Sarvartha Chintamani, Muhurta Chintamani verses, Tajika Neelakanthi), BPHS chapter numbers differ by about two between the Santhanam-numbered e-text and the Sharma edition, and of the eight P0 findings only three are proven (Mudda by a run, the stored 108-year rows and the empty-house yogas by inspection of stored data); the sahams bug is unambiguous in the code, and four await a reference run. Each is marked as such in the matrix, and all 86 findings are filed in `docs/TODOS.md`.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which techniques are we missing" to "which numbers can we trust". The engine already holds almost every clock a classical or modern school uses; what it lacks is a way to prove each one against a reference and to say which variant it computed. That makes a reference harness the highest-leverage piece of work, because it converts the four code-read P0 suspicions into facts or retractions, guards every later fix, and turns the catalogue's meaningless "verified" flag into something earned. The recommended order follows from that: harness, then the served P0 fixes and a re-pull, then a complete annual layer, then one options-and-variant contract that unlocks D9 and D10 Narayana for Love and Work, then moving Node's rules into engine fields, and only then new techniques such as vedha, Karakamsha and Sudarshana.

A second implication is restraint. Most sign dashas have no stated purpose in the texts, five exist to time death, and the conditional dashas carry no priority rule; the boards are right to let Vimshottari lead and to treat every other clock as agreement evidence. Adding more clocks would raise the chance of spurious agreement between them, which is the opposite of what a vote across clocks is meant to measure.
